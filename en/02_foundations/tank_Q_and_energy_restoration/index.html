Tank Q and Energy Restoration

The "quality factor QQQ (the measure of how 'sharp' a resonance is and how much energy is lost per cycle)" appears on almost every page of this site: the Leeson model writes it as 12Q\dfrac{1}{2Q}2Q1​ (derivation_leeson), the LC-vs-ring comparison hinges on it (lc_vs_ring), and the tank-swing trade-off needs it too (tank_swing). Yet on all of those pages it is used as a given — it has never been cleanly derived from the circuit. This page fills that hole: starting from the most basic parallel RLC tank, we derive the three equivalent forms of QQQ and its energy definition step by step, then show how it connects to the active core's −R-R−R, to the tank thermal noise 4kT/Rp4kT/R_p4kT/Rp​, and finally to how it determines phase noise.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): the tank (resonant tank — the energy-storage element in which LLL and CCC exchange energy back and forth) is like a pendulum. QQQ measures "how reluctant this pendulum is to stop" — per radian of oscillation it leaks only a tiny fraction of its stored energy into the loss resistance RpR_pRp​. The higher the QQQ, the sharper the resonance peak, the narrower the bandwidth, and the steeper the phase-vs-frequency slope. Think of noise as a force trying to push the oscillation frequency off: the higher the QQQ, the harder the tank "bites down" on ω0\omega_0ω0​ and refuses to be pushed, so the same lump of noise buys less phase noise. That is why the first slogan of low-phase-noise design is always "make QQQ high."

This page answers three questions:

  1. What exactly is QQQ? Why are the three forms Q=RpC/L=ω0RpC=Rp/(ω0L)Q=R_p\sqrt{C/L}=\omega_0 R_p C=R_p/(\omega_0 L)Q=Rp​C/L​=ω0​Rp​C=Rp​/(ω0​L) equivalent, and why does it equal ω0×\omega_0\timesω0​× (stored energy / dissipated power)?
  2. A real tank always has loss RpR_pRp​, so the oscillation decays — how does the active core restore it with −R-R−R? And why is that cancelled RpR_pRp​ precisely the source of the tank thermal noise 4kT/Rp4kT/R_p4kT/Rp​?
  3. How does QQQ connect to phase noise? Why "high QQQ → narrow band → steep phase slope → less noise per unit offset," and why is this the same thing as the ISF's Γrms/qmax\Gamma_{rms}/q_{max}Γrms​/qmax​?

This site does not reinvent these constants; it only derives them cleanly and connects them to the verified ISF results within the 5 PDFs ([P1] Eq.(21), etc.). Step 1: the parallel RLC tank and the three equivalent forms of QQQ​

The most basic LC-oscillator tank is a parallel RLC: an inductor LLL, a capacitor CCC, and one parallel loss resistor RpR_pRp​ (all tank losses — inductor series resistance, capacitor dielectric loss, radiation — lumped into this single equivalent parallel resistance).

  • Loaded QQQ (QLQ_LQL​, loaded quality factor): the QQQ after external loading (downstream circuitry, measurement instruments, buffer input impedance) is folded in. What this page derives, and what determines phase noise, is primarily the loaded QQQ (what the oscillator actually sees); inductor design, however, cares about the unloaded QQQ. Below, unless stated otherwise, QQQ means the oscillation loop's actual effective (loaded) QQQ.

Step 3: the active core supplies −R-R−R to cancel RpR_pRp​ — and RpR_pRp​ is precisely the source of the 4kT/Rp4kT/R_p4kT/Rp​ thermal noise​

Step 2 showed: as long as RpR_pRp​ is there, the oscillation decays as e−ω0t/(2Q)e^{-\omega_0 t/(2Q)}e−ω0​t/(2Q). To sustain steady oscillation, the oscillator's active core (e.g., the negative conductance provided by a cross-coupled differential pair) must replenish, cycle by cycle, the energy that RpR_pRp​ leaks. The cleanest view: the active core presents a negative resistance −R-R−R (negative conductance −Gm-G_m−Gm​) across the tank, in parallel with RpR_pRp​.

1Rtank=1Rp−Gm,起振條件: Gm≥1Rp (即 −R≤−Rp).\frac{1}{R_{tank}}=\frac{1}{R_p}-G_m,\qquad \text{起振條件}:\ G_m\ge\frac{1}{R_p}\ \big(\text{即 } -R\le -R_p\big).Rtank​1​=Rp​1​−Gm​,起振條件: Gm​≥Rp​1​ (即 −R≤−Rp​).
  • Physical meaning: positive resistance absorbs energy; negative resistance supplies it. When the active core's negative conductance GmG_mGm​ exactly equals RpR_pRp​'s conductance 1/Rp1/R_p1/Rp​, the total loss is zero and the oscillation holds constant amplitude (the Barkhausen start-up boundary). In practice, GmG_mGm​ is designed slightly larger than 1/Rp1/R_p1/Rp​ (loop gain >1>1>1) to guarantee start-up, and nonlinear saturation then pulls the effective GmG_mGm​ back to exact cancellation — the circuit-level incarnation of the limit-cycle amplitude-restoring mechanism described in oscillator_phase.
  • Unit check: GmG_mGm​ and 1/Rp1/R_p1/Rp​ are both in siemens (S) ✓.
  • Key clarification: what the active core cancels is RpR_pRp​'s deterministic energy loss (keeping the oscillation from decaying). It does not — and cannot — cancel the random thermal noise that RpR_pRp​ brings. That is the point of the next paragraph, and the bridge from this whole page to phase noise.

RpR_pRp​ is the physical source of the tank thermal-noise current 4kT/Rp4kT/R_p4kT/Rp​. Any dissipative resistance (including the equivalent loss RpR_pRp​) is necessarily accompanied by thermal noise, per the Johnson–Nyquist theorem. A parallel resistance is most conveniently represented by its Norton-equivalent noise current source, with single-sided PSD:

in,R2‾Δf=4kTRp.\frac{\overline{i_{n,R}^2}}{\Delta f}=\frac{4kT}{R_p}.Δfin,R2​​​=Rp​4kT​.
  • Physics used: the fluctuation–dissipation theorem — where there is dissipation, there is fluctuation. The resistor thermal-noise voltage PSD is 4kTRp4kTR_p4kTRp​ (covered in psd_phase_noise_jitter); converting to a parallel Norton current source means dividing by Rp2R_p^2Rp2​: in2‾/Δf=(4kTRp)/Rp2=4kT/Rp\overline{i_n^2}/\Delta f=(4kTR_p)/R_p^2=4kT/R_pin2​​/Δf=(4kTRp​)/Rp2​=4kT/Rp​.
  • Unit check: [4kT/Rp]=J/Ω=(V⋅A⋅s)/(V/A)=A2s=A2/Hz[4kT/R_p]=\text{J}/\Omega=(\text{V·A·s})/(\text{V/A})=\text{A}^2\text{s}=\text{A}^2/\text{Hz}[4kT/Rp​]=J/Ω=(V⋅A⋅s)/(V/A)=A2s=A2/Hz ✓ (single-sided current PSD, consistent with the spec's notation of in2‾/Δf\overline{i_n^2}/\Delta fin2​​/Δf in A²/Hz).
  • The deep point: you cannot cancel this noise with −R-R−R as well. The active core's −R-R−R cancels RpR_pRp​'s loss term on the "energy balance" ledger; but the random 4kT/Rp4kT/R_p4kT/Rp​ current that RpR_pRp​ injects on the "fluctuation" ledger is sustained — equally amplified — by the very energy the active core pumps back in. So the tank loss RpR_pRp​ is simultaneously the source of two things: it forces you to add an active core to restore energy, and it hands you an unavoidable baseline thermal-noise floor. This is the physical root tying QQQ to phase noise. (The active core's own devices add still more noise — that is the topic of device_noise_mapping; this page covers only the tank's own 4kT/Rp4kT/R_p4kT/Rp​.)

Expressing RpR_pRp​ in terms of QQQ (to hook into phase noise next): from Step 1, Rp=Q/(ω0C)=Q ω0L=Q R0R_p=Q/(\omega_0 C)=Q\,\omega_0 L=Q\,R_0Rp​=Q/(ω0​C)=Qω0​L=QR0​, so at the same capacitance/frequency,

4kTRp=4kT ω0CQ⇒in,R2‾/Δf ∝ 1Q.\frac{4kT}{R_p}=\frac{4kT\,\omega_0 C}{Q}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \overline{i_{n,R}^2}/\Delta f\ \propto\ \frac{1}{Q}.Rp​4kT​=Q4kTω0​C​⇒in,R2​​/Δf ∝ Q1​.
  • Reading: higher QQQ → larger RpR_pRp​ → smaller tank-injected thermal-noise current PSD (∝1/Q\propto 1/Q∝1/Q). This is the first layer of "high QQQ → low phase noise" (the noise source shrinks); the "narrow-band / steep-slope" effect of Step 2 is the second layer. Stack the two and you get the next step.

Step 4: connecting QQQ to phase noise — narrow band, steep phase slope, and the equivalence with Γrms/qmax\Gamma_{rms}/q_{max}Γrms​/qmax​​

(a) High QQQ = narrow bandwidth = steep phase slope. The standard relation between QQQ and the 3-dB bandwidth:

Q=ω0Δω3dB⇔Δω3dB=ω0Q.Q=\frac{\omega_0}{\Delta\omega_{3\mathrm{dB}}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \Delta\omega_{3\mathrm{dB}}=\frac{\omega_0}{Q}.Q=Δω3dB​ω0​​⇔Δω3dB​=Qω0​​.
  • Derivation sketch: the parallel-RLC impedance is Z(ω)=(1Rp+jωC+1jωL)−1Z(\omega)=\big(\tfrac{1}{R_p}+j\omega C+\tfrac{1}{j\omega L}\big)^{-1}Z(ω)=(Rp​1​+jωC+jωL1​)−1; expanding to first order near ω0\omega_0ω0​ with ω=ω0+Δω\omega=\omega_0+\Delta\omegaω=ω0​+Δω, the susceptance part is ≈j 2CΔω\approx j\,2C\Delta\omega≈j2CΔω, giving Z≈Rp/(1+j 2QΔω/ω0)Z\approx R_p/(1+j\,2Q\Delta\omega/\omega_0)Z≈Rp​/(1+j2QΔω/ω0​). ∣Z∣|Z|∣Z∣ drops to 1/21/\sqrt21/2​ of the peak (−3 dB) when 2QΔω/ω0=12Q\Delta\omega/\omega_0=12QΔω/ω0​=1, i.e. half-bandwidth Δω=ω0/(2Q)\Delta\omega=\omega_0/(2Q)Δω=ω0​/(2Q), full bandwidth ω0/Q\omega_0/Qω0​/Q ✓.

The higher the QQQ, the steeper the phase-vs-frequency slope.
  • Physical meaning (why a steep phase slope = low phase noise): the oscillation locks to the frequency where "the total loop phase shift = 0." If noise tries to push the phase away from 0, the tank's steep phase slope applies a large "frequency restoring force" pulling it back to ω0\omega_0ω0​ — the steeper the phase slope dϕ/dωd\phi/d\omegadϕ/dω, the smaller the frequency (and hence long-term phase) offset that a given phase perturbation corresponds to. The tank acts like a very stiff spring clamping the oscillation frequency at ω0\omega_0ω0​. This is precisely the physical origin of the Leeson shaping term (ω02QΔω)2\big(\tfrac{\omega_0}{2Q\Delta\omega}\big)^2(2QΔωω0​​)2 (derivation_leeson, Step 2).

(b) 1/Q21/Q^21/Q2 scaling (Leeson). Stack (a)'s narrow-band shaping on top of Step 3's 1/Q1/Q1/Q noise source, then run it through the autonomous oscillator's phase integration (1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2): the QQQ dependence of phase noise lands at 1/Q21/Q^21/Q2:

L(Δω) ∝ (ω02Q Δω)2 ∝ 1Q2.\mathcal{L}(\Delta\omega)\ \propto\ \Big(\frac{\omega_0}{2Q\,\Delta\omega}\Big)^2\ \propto\ \frac{1}{Q^2}.L(Δω) ∝ (2QΔωω0​​)2 ∝ Q21​.
  • Reading: doubling QQQ → phase noise improves by 10log⁡10(22)=6.0210\log_{10}(2^2)=6.0210log10​(22)=6.02 dB. This is the same inverse-square law as tank_swing's "double qmaxq_{max}qmax​ → −6 dB" — QQQ and qmaxq_{max}qmax​ are two independent levers that each "pay off quadratically" toward low phase noise.
  • Note: the shaping expression (ω02QΔω)2\big(\tfrac{\omega_0}{2Q\Delta\omega}\big)^2(2QΔωω0​​)2 belongs to the Leeson model (external literature, not among the 5 source PDFs; see derivation_leeson).

  • Why the ISF is more general: QQQ is an LC-tank concept (you need a resonant energy-storage element to even have a QQQ); a ring oscillator has no high-QQQ tank — no QQQ at all — yet it still has Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ and qmaxq_{max}qmax​, so [P1]'s Γrms/qmax\Gamma_{rms}/q_{max}Γrms​/qmax​ holds for rings just as well, while Leeson's 1/(2Q)1/(2Q)1/(2Q) fails for rings (see lc_vs_ring). That is the full meaning of the Q↔Γrms/qmaxQ\leftrightarrow\Gamma_{rms}/q_{max}Q↔Γrms​/qmax​ equivalence claimed in derivation_leeson.

Example (QQQ, RpR_pRp​, and thermal-noise floor of a 5 GHz LC tank): take L=1L=1L=1 nH, C=1.013C=1.013C=1.013 pF (tuned so f0=5f_0=5f0​=5 GHz), tank parallel loss Rp=314 ΩR_p=314\ \OmegaRp​=314 Ω, T=300T=300T=300 K. Find QQQ, the 3-dB bandwidth, and the tank thermal-noise current PSD.

Q_1 = w0*Rp*C            # ω0 Rp C

Step 5: the practical ceiling — on-chip inductor Q and parasitics​

In theory you could keep raising QQQ by making RpR_pRp​ large, but in a silicon process QQQ hits a hard ceiling, and the causes almost all trace back to the inductor and parasitics:
