Closed Forms for the Asymmetric Triangular ISF ([P2] Appendix B)

Prerequisites: fourier_series_of_isf (c0c_0c0​ and Parseval), rms_isf (definition and role of Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​), flicker_noise_upconversion (why c0c_0c0​ is the only gate for flicker upconversion) | Next: symmetry (turning these closed forms into design knobs), lab_32 (equation-level ring ISF extraction — where the triangular approximation breaks)

The symmetry page gives the qualitative rule "symmetric rise/fall → c0→0c_0\to0c0​→0 → 1/f³ suppressed," but its numerical example plugs in an assumed c0c_0c0​. This page fills the missing piece: given a ring oscillator's stage count NNN and how asymmetric its waveform is, what exactly are Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​, c0c_0c0​, and the 1/f³ corner? [P2] Appendix B (p.803) answers with a full set of closed forms, Eq.(52)–(57), built on an asymmetric triangular ISF model — one of the very few places on this site where c0c_0c0​ is computed directly from topology parameters, so it is worth walking through step by step.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): a ring's ISF concentrates at the two transitions: a positive lobe at the rising edge and a negative lobe at the falling edge, with lobe height inversely proportional to that edge's slope (steeper edge → less sensitive). If the rise is steeper than the fall (A=frise′/ffall′>1A=f'_{rise}/f'_{fall}>1A=frise′​/ffall′​>1), the positive lobe is short and narrow while the negative lobe is tall and wide — the two lobe areas no longer cancel, and the leftover net area is exactly Γdc\Gamma_{dc}Γdc​ (i.e. c0/2c_0/2c0​/2), which upconverts device 1/f noise into close-in 1/f³. The closed forms turn this geometric picture into three formulas — Γrms2\Gamma_{rms}^2Γrms2​ (Eq.55), Γdc\Gamma_{dc}Γdc​ (Eq.56), corner (Eq.57) — and the corner depends on only two dimensionless numbers, AAA and NNN.

Step 0: the model — the asymmetric triangular ISF of [P2] Fig. 18​

[P2] Fig. 18, p.803 (verified against the rendered PDF page for this unit): "Approximate waveform and the ISF for asymmetric rising and falling edges." The figure plots Γ(x)\Gamma(x)Γ(x) over one period (xxx from 000 to 2π2\pi2π) as two triangular lobes:

negative1/ffall′1/f'_{fall}1/ffall′​ (depth)2/ffall′2/f'_{fall}2/ffall′​falling edge

Here frise′f'_{rise}frise′​, ffall′f'_{fall}ffall′​ are the maximum slopes of the normalized waveform (f(x)f(x)f(x) normalized to amplitude 1, x=ω0τx=\omega_0\taux=ω0​τ) during the rising/falling edges (verbatim: "where frise′f'_{rise}frise′​ and ffall′f'_{fall}ffall′​ are the maximum slope during the rising and falling edge, respectively", p.803).

  • Unit check: x=ω0τx=\omega_0\taux=ω0​τ is [rad/s]⋅[s]=[rad][\text{rad/s}]\cdot[\text{s}]=[\text{rad}][rad/s]⋅[s]=[rad], and this site treats rad as a dimensionless pure number; f(x)f(x)f(x) is dimensionless, so f′=df/dxf'=df/dxf′=df/dx and 1/f′1/f'1/f′ are also dimensionless — consistent with Γ\GammaΓ being dimensionless ✓.
  • Why the height is 1/f′1/f'1/f′: the ISF peak is inversely proportional to the waveform slope — the steeper the edge, the smaller the phase shift caused by the same charge perturbation (see waveform_slope; this is the starting point of the triangular approximation in the main text of [P2]).
  • Key geometric observation (slope = 1): each lobe has "height =1/f′=1/f'=1/f′, base width =2/f′=2/f'=2/f′", so the half-width equals the height and the two sides of the triangle have slope ±1\pm1±1 in the xxx coordinate. This is no accident: the sensitivity peak is ∝1/f′\propto 1/f'∝1/f′ and the sensitive window (the phase width the transition occupies) is also ∝1/f′\propto 1/f'∝1/f′; they share the same origin, so their ratio is fixed at 1. This "unit slope" makes the integrals below extremely clean.
  • The positions of the lobes along xxx (where the positive and negative lobes sit) do not affect Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ or Γdc\Gamma_{dc}Γdc​ (they only affect the harmonic phases θn\theta_nθn​), so the derivation may place the lobes at any non-overlapping positions.

Step 1: piecewise integration for Γrms2\Gamma_{rms}^2Γrms2​ — Eq.(52)​

Start from the definition of Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ (the [P1] Eq.(20) form, see rms_isf) and integrate the two triangular lobes piecewise. Because the sides have slope =±1=\pm1=±1, on each half-side we can use the "value of the ISF" itself as the integration variable (Γ\GammaΓ walks linearly from 0 to the peak 1/f′1/f'1/f′, with dΓ=± dxd\Gamma = \pm\,dxdΓ=±dx); one lobe = two half-sides = 2∫01/f′x2 dx2\int_0^{1/f'}x^2\,dx2∫01/f′​x2dx. Step 2: swap frise′f'_{rise}frise′​ for circuit parameters — the period constraint Eq.(54)​

Eq.(52) still contains the waveform slope. To express it in "parameters a designer holds" (the stage count NNN), use the ring's period constraint. [P2] Eq.(14), p.794 defines the normalized stage delay t^D=η/fmax′\hat t_D=\eta/f'_{max}t^D​=η/fmax′​ (η≈1\eta\approx1η≈1 is a proportionality constant: one stage's delay is roughly the inverse of the transition slope, times η\etaη). In a single-ended inverting ring, the signal travels around the loop twice per full period (every node rises once and falls once), i.e. 2N2N2N stage delays — NNN of them rising-edge delays η/frise′\eta/f'_{rise}η/frise′​ and NNN falling-edge delays η/ffall′\eta/f'_{fall}η/ffall′​. Writing the period's phase length 2π2\pi2π as the sum of these delays ([P2] Eq.(54), p.803, verbatim):

2π=ηN ⁣(1frise′+1ffall′)=ηNfrise′(1+A)2\pi=\eta N\!\left(\frac{1}{f'_{rise}}+\frac{1}{f'_{fall}}\right)=\frac{\eta N}{f'_{rise}}(1+A)2π=ηN(frise′​1​+ffall′​1​)=frise′​ηN​(1+A)

Solving for the positive-lobe peak height:

1frise′=2πηN(1+A),1ffall′=2πAηN(1+A).\frac{1}{f'_{rise}}=\frac{2\pi}{\eta N(1+A)},\qquad \frac{1}{f'_{fall}}=\frac{2\pi A}{\eta N(1+A)}.frise′​1​=ηN(1+A)2π​,ffall′​1​=ηN(1+A)2πA​.
  • Dimension check: both sides are rad (dimensionless) ✓; η,N,A\eta,N,Aη,N,A are all dimensionless.
  • Sanity check: at A=1A=1A=1 this reduces to the symmetric case 2π=2Nt^D2\pi=2N\hat t_D2π=2Nt^D​ (exactly the phase-domain form of [P2] Eq.(15), f0=1/(2NτD)f_0=1/(2N\tau_D)f0​=1/(2NτD​)); larger NNN or larger A+1A+1A+1 shrinks the positive lobe — every piece of the geometry is "diluted" by the fixed 2π2\pi2π budget.

Step 4: the DC value Γdc\Gamma_{dc}Γdc​ — Eq.(56), connecting to c0c_0c0​​

Γdc\Gamma_{dc}Γdc​ is the average of the ISF over one period. Triangle area =12⋅=\tfrac12\cdot=21​⋅base⋅\cdot⋅height: positive-lobe area 12⋅2frise′⋅1frise′=1frise′2\tfrac12\cdot\dfrac{2}{f'_{rise}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{f'_{rise}}=\dfrac{1}{f'^2_{rise}}21​⋅frise′​2​⋅frise′​1​=frise′2​1​, and likewise 1ffall′2\dfrac{1}{f'^2_{fall}}ffall′2​1​ for the negative lobe (with a minus sign):

Eq.(57) has three structural properties, each carrying a design message:

  1. The corner vanishes quadratically as A→1A\to1A→1: the numerator (1−A)2(1-A)^2(1−A)2 — near the symmetry point the corner is second-order insensitive to asymmetry, but once you drift away the degradation also accelerates quadratically (lab_33: at A=1.01A=1.01A=1.01 corner/f1/f/f_{1/f}/f1/f​ is only 2.97×10−52.97\times10^{-5}2.97×10−5, while at A=1.10A=1.10A=1.10 it is already 2.70×10−32.70\times10^{-3}2.70×10−3 — nearly 100× apart). This is the analytic version of the bowl-shaped minimum of [P2] Fig. 17, p.802 ("phase noise vs symmetry control voltage").
  2. Invariant under A→1/AA\to1/AA→1/A: (1−A)2/(1−A+A2)(1-A)^2/(1-A+A^2)(1−A)2/(1−A+A2) is unchanged when AAA is replaced by 1/A1/A1/A (multiply top and bottom by A2A^2A2; lab_33 verifies corner(A=2)=(A{=}2)=(A=2)= corner(A=0.5)=1.000000×10−1 f1/f(A{=}0.5) =1.000000\times10^{-1}\,f_{1/f}(A=0.5)=1.000000×10−1f1/f​). The V-shaped valley is left-right symmetric on a log-AAA axis — "which edge is steeper" does not matter.
  3. Corner ∝1/N\propto 1/N∝1/N: at fixed AAA, more stages means a lower corner. Verbatim (p.803, end of App. B): "As can be seen for a constant rise-to-fall ratio, the 1/f³ corner decreases inversely with the number of stages; therefore, ring oscillators with a smaller number of stages will have a larger 1/f³ noise corner. As a special case, if the rise and fall time are symmetric, A = 1, and the 1/f³ corner approaches zero."

Reconciling with the "NNN-independence" conclusion: [P2]'s main-text Eq.(23) says that at fixed f0f_0f0​ and power, phase noise in the white-noise (1/f²) region is approximately NNN-independent (see the paper_002 deep dive and lc_vs_ring). There is no contradiction: NNN does not move the height of the 1/f² region, but it pushes the 1/f³ knee toward lower frequency (∝1/N\propto1/N∝1/N). Design message: if your pain point is close-in flicker (e.g. the PLL loop bandwidth is too narrow to clean up the VCO's 1/f³), more stages is an effective lever; if the pain point is white-region jitter, adding stages does not help. This is the new design takeaway of this page — called out by [P2] itself.
