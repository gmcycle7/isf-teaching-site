Beyond the Lorentzian: 1/f³ Lineshape and Nonstationarity — What the Instrument Actually Measures

Prerequisites: lorentzian_linewidth (the full white-noise → Lorentzian chain and the "spurious divergence"), flicker_noise_upconversion (where the 1/f31/f^31/f3 skirt comes from), stochastic_noise_basics (stationarity, Wiener–Khinchin) | Next: allan_variance (the canonical time-domain tool for characterizing flicker FM), measurement_and_spurs (instrumentation practicalities)

lorentzian_linewidth resolved the spurious divergence of 1/f21/f^21/f2 as Δω→0\Delta\omega\to0Δω→0 with one clean logical chain: phase random walk (Var⁡[Δϕ]=2D∣t∣\operatorname{Var}[\Delta\phi]=2D|t|Var[Δϕ]=2D∣t∣) → Gaussian characteristic function → exponential autocorrelation → Lorentzian lineshape. But the first link of that chain hides an assumption: the noise driving the phase is white. In a real oscillator, the skirt closest to the carrier is usually 1/f31/f^31/f3 (flicker FM, see flicker_noise_upconversion) — so is the close-in lineshape still Lorentzian?

This page answers two questions:

  1. Part A (lineshape): under flicker FM, Var⁡[Δϕ(t)]\operatorname{Var}[\Delta\phi(t)]Var[Δϕ(t)] no longer grows linearly but as t2×log⁡t^2\times\logt2×log (with an explicit role for a low-frequency cutoff flf_lfl​); the characteristic-function step therefore yields a near-Gaussian line core, not a Lorentzian.
  2. Part B (nonstationarity): the free-running ϕ(t)\phi(t)ϕ(t) is a random walk, so strictly speaking Sϕ(f)S_\phi(f)Sϕ​(f) does not exist (it is not a stationary process and has no stationary PSD); what the instrument really measures is the spectrum of V(t)=cos⁡(ω0t+ϕ)V(t)=\cos(\omega_0t+\phi)V(t)=cos(ω0​t+ϕ) — which is stationary (the [E2] Demir view). Every SϕS_\phiSϕ​ formula on this site is a conditional spectrum under "finite observation time, offset ≫\gg≫ linewidth"; the two descriptions agree exactly within that range.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): the lineshape is set by the "speed profile of phase memory loss". Under white noise the phase variance accumulates linearly (∝t\propto t∝t), the memory-loss envelope is the exponential e−D∣t∣e^{-D|t|}e−D∣t∣, and its Fourier transform is a Lorentzian. Under flicker FM, low-frequency noise parks the frequency itself on one side for long stretches, and the phase variance accumulates almost as t2t^2t2 (up to a log) — like a "walk with drift" — so the memory-loss envelope becomes the near-Gaussian e−(const)t2log⁡e^{-(\text{const})t^2\log}e−(const)t2log, and the Fourier transform of a Gaussian is again a Gaussian: the line core becomes a bell-shaped Gaussian with shoulders far steeper than a Lorentzian's. For the very same L(10 kHz)\mathcal{L}(10\,\text{kHz})L(10kHz) spec, the white-noise version has a 50 Hz linewidth and the flicker version 3.1 kHz — a dBc/Hz number at one offset does not determine the linewidth at all; the noise "color" does. This is the main event of the lab_29 numerical demo.

Try it: the lorentzian_linewidth page embeds an interactive explorer — a slider for the same L(10 kHz)\mathcal{L}(10\,\text{kHz})L(10kHz) spec, a white FM / flicker FM toggle, and a spectrum-analyzer RBW slider — so you can see this page's "same spec, 100× difference in linewidth" conclusion first-hand, plus how too-wide an RBW smears away the flattening / near-Gaussian shoulder.

Part B: nonstationarity — SϕS_\phiSϕ​ strictly does not exist, so what does the instrument measure?​

Step 5: the free-running ϕ(t)\phi(t)ϕ(t) is not a stationary process​

Wide-sense stationarity requires two things: a mean that does not change with time, and an autocorrelation Rϕ(t1,t2)R_\phi(t_1,t_2)Rϕ​(t1​,t2​) that depends only on τ=t2−t1\tau=t_2-t_1τ=t2​−t1​. For a white-noise-driven Wiener phase started at t=0t=0t=0 (ϕ(0)=0\phi(0)=0ϕ(0)=0), compute RϕR_\phiRϕ​ step by step:

(i) Take t2≥t1t_2\ge t_1t2​≥t1​ and split ϕ(t2)=ϕ(t1)+Δ\phi(t_2)=\phi(t_1)+\Deltaϕ(t2​)=ϕ(t1​)+Δ, where Δ=ϕ(t2)−ϕ(t1)\Delta=\phi(t_2)-\phi(t_1)Δ=ϕ(t2​)−ϕ(t1​) is the noise integral over the interval (t1,t2](t_1,t_2](t1​,t2​], independent of ϕ(t1)\phi(t_1)ϕ(t1​) (white noise is uncorrelated across disjoint intervals; for Gaussians, uncorrelated = independent).

(ii)

Step 6: what does exist is the spectrum of V(t)V(t)V(t) (the [E2] Demir view)​

Although ϕ\phiϕ diverges, V(t)=cos⁡(ω0t+ϕ(t))V(t)=\cos(\omega_0t+\phi(t))V(t)=cos(ω0​t+ϕ(t)) is bounded, and its statistics converge to stationary:

  • The divergence of the "absolute" phase does not matter — VVV only sees ϕ mod 2π\phi\bmod 2\piϕmod2π, and the  mod 2π\bmod 2\pimod2π distribution of a random walk tends to uniform over time (the initial phase is forgotten).
  • The autocorrelation retains only the phase difference: ⟨V(t)V(t+τ)⟩→12cos⁡(ω0τ)E(τ)\langle V(t)V(t+\tau)\rangle\to\tfrac12\cos(\omega_0\tau)E(\tau)⟨V(t)V(t+τ)⟩→21​cos(ω0​τ)E(τ) (the Step-1 computation of lorentzian_linewidth, which uses precisely the stationary increment Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ) — depends only on τ\tauτ ✓.

Step 7: reconciliation — what this site's SϕS_\phiSϕ​ formulas actually measure​

So what do the pagefuls of SϕS_\phiSϕ​ and L\mathcal{L}L formulas on this site (and in [P1]) mean? They are conditional spectra at finite observation time: take a record of length TTT (or let the PLL/instrument subtract the slow drift), and do spectral estimation on "the increments that look stationary within this record". One can show (and lab_29 panel (d) demonstrates numerically) that for f≫1/Tf\gg1/Tf≫1/T the expectation converges to

Sϕ(T)(f) →f≫1/T 4D(2πf)2 （單邊，white FM）,b−3f3 （flicker FM）,S_\phi^{(T)}(f)\ \xrightarrow[f\gg1/T]{}\ \frac{4D}{(2\pi f)^2}\ \text{（單邊，white FM）},\qquad \frac{b_{-3}}{f^3}\ \text{（flicker FM）},Sϕ(T)​(f) f≫1/T​ (2πf)24D​ （單邊，white FM）,f3b−3​​ （flicker FM）,

with measured ÷ theory =1.001=1.001=1.001 (10 kHz; see the marker above). So every SϕS_\phiSϕ​ formula on this site is rigorously usable in the range Δf≫max⁡(1/T, 線寬)\Delta f\gg\max(1/T,\ \text{線寬})Δf≫max(1/T, 線寬); where they fail is precisely the "spurious divergence" region of lorentzian_linewidth — for Δf≲\Delta f\lesssimΔf≲ linewidth you must switch to reading SVS_VSV​ (the Lorentzian / near-Gaussian lineshape). The two descriptions splice seamlessly over their common range of validity (Part A's 0.6 dB exercise quantifies the splice point).

Flicker adds one more twist (this page's own lesson): because flf_lfl​ enters the variance through ln⁡\lnln, "finite observation" leaves a logarithmic afterimage on flicker forever — lab_29's cutoff experiment (flf_lfl​ moved from 1/321/321/32 Hz to 1 Hz halves Var⁡(10 ms)\operatorname{Var}(10\,\text{ms})Var(10ms)) and the single-record linewidth spread (2944±1422944\pm1422944±142 Hz, 6 records of only 32 s each) are direct displays of it. In practical language: for a flicker-dominated oscillator, the "linewidth" and the close-in integrated jitter are (weak) functions of "how long you measure"; quote the observation time / measurement bandwidth alongside the number — which is also why allan_variance exists (flicker FM shows up as a clean plateau on ADEV, well defined without any flf_lfl​). For the textbook treatment of this viewpoint see Rubiola 2009 (cited above, external literature).

Key takeaways​

  • The Lorentzian is exclusive to white FM: it is inherited from the linear growth Var⁡[Δϕ]=2D∣t∣\operatorname{Var}[\Delta\phi]=2D|t|Var[Δϕ]=2D∣t∣.
  • The universal engine: Var⁡[Δϕ(τ)]=2∫0∞Sϕ單邊(f)(1−cos⁡2πfτ) df\operatorname{Var}[\Delta\phi(\tau)]=2\int_0^\infty S_\phi^{\text{單邊}}(f)(1-\cos2\pi f\tau)\,dfVar[Δϕ(τ)]=2∫0∞​Sϕ單邊​(f)(1−cos2πfτ)df (increment high-pass kernel; single-sided bookkeeping — substituting the white 4D/Δω24D/\Delta\omega^24D/Δω2 recovers 2D∣t∣2D|t|2D∣t∣; lab_29 measures a ratio of 1.001).
  • Flicker FM (Sϕ=b−3/f3S_\phi=b_{-3}/f^3Sϕ​=b−3​/f3, needs a low-frequency cutoff flf_lfl​): Var⁡=4π2b−3τ2[ln⁡12πflτ+32−γE]\operatorname{Var}=4\pi^2b_{-3}\tau^2[\ln\frac{1}{2\pi f_l\tau}+\frac32-\gamma_E]Var=4π2b−3​τ2[ln2πfl​τ1​+23​−γE​] — t2×log⁡t^2\times\logt2×log growth; flf_lfl​ enters only through ln⁡\lnln (a 10× change shifts it by only ln⁡10\ln10ln10).

  • Shape fingerprint, the −10-10−10 dB / −3-3−3 dB half-width ratio: Lorentzian 3.003.003.00, Gaussian 1.821.821.82, flicker with log correction 1.861.861.86 (lab_29 measures white 2.872.872.87, flicker 1.891.891.89; Lorentzian-fit rms error 0.24 vs 4.37 dB).
