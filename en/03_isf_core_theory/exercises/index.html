This page is the complete exercise set for Chapter 03, ISF Core Theory. The problems span derivations, numerical problems, and design back-calculations, all built around the [P1] Hajimiri–Lee ISF framework, using the site-wide notation.

Exercise 6 solution (frequency-translation meaning of the Fourier coefficients — derivation)

Target expression. The phase contribution of the nnn-th harmonic term ([P1] Eq.(13)):

ϕn(t)=1qmax∫t ⁣ ⁣cncos⁡(nω0τ+θn) I0cos⁡((nω0+Δω)τ) dτ.\phi_n(t)=\frac{1}{q_{max}}\int^{t}\!\!c_n\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)\,I_0\cos\big((n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau\big)\,d\tau.ϕn​(t)=qmax​1​∫tcn​cos(nω0​τ+θn​)I0​cos((nω0​+Δω)τ)dτ.

Step (i): product-to-sum. Let A=nω0τ+θnA=n\omega_0\tau+\theta_nA=nω0​τ+θn​, B=(nω0+Δω)τB=(n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tauB=(nω0​+Δω)τ, and use cos⁡Acos⁡B=12[cos⁡(A−B)+cos⁡(A+B)]\cos A\cos B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)]cosAcosB=21​[cos(A−B)+cos(A+B)]:

A−B=θn−Δωτ,A+B=(2nω0+Δω)τ+θn.A-B=\theta_n-\Delta\omega\tau,\qquad A+B=(2n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau+\theta_n.A−B=θn​−Δωτ,A+B=(2nω0​+Δω)τ+θn​.

Integrand =I0cn2[cos⁡(Δωτ−θn)⏟slow term, ≈Δω+cos⁡((2nω0+Δω)τ+θn)⏟fast term, ≈2nω0]=\dfrac{I_0c_n}{2}\Big[\underbrace{\cos(\Delta\omega\tau-\theta_n)}_{\text{slow term, }\approx\Delta\omega}+\underbrace{\cos((2n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau+\theta_n)}_{\text{fast term, }\approx2n\omega_0}\Big]=2I0​cn​​[slow term, ≈Δωcos(Δωτ−θn​)​​+fast term, ≈2nω0​cos((2nω0​+Δω)τ+θn​)​​].

Step (ii): the integrator is a low-pass filter — the fast term averages out.

  • Slow term: ∫cos⁡(Δωτ−θn)dτ=sin⁡(Δωτ−θn)Δω\int\cos(\Delta\omega\tau-\theta_n)d\tau=\dfrac{\sin(\Delta\omega\tau-\theta_n)}{\Delta\omega}∫cos(Δωτ−θn​)dτ=Δωsin(Δωτ−θn​)​ — the denominator is only the tiny Δω\Delta\omegaΔω, so it gets amplified and survives.
  • Fast term: sin⁡(⋯ )2nω0+Δω\dfrac{\sin(\cdots)}{2n\omega_0+\Delta\omega}2nω0​+Δωsin(⋯)​ — the denominator is the huge 2nω02n\omega_02nω0​; the amplitude is crushed by a factor ∼Δω/(2nω0)\sim\Delta\omega/(2n\omega_0)∼Δω/(2nω0​), hence negligible.

Step (iii): keep only the slow term.

ϕn(t)≈1qmax⋅I0cn2⋅sin⁡(Δωt−θn)Δω=I0cn2qmax⋅sin⁡(Δωt−θn)Δω.■\phi_n(t)\approx\frac{1}{q_{max}}\cdot\frac{I_0c_n}{2}\cdot\frac{\sin(\Delta\omega t-\theta_n)}{\Delta\omega}=\frac{I_0 c_n}{2q_{max}}\cdot\frac{\sin(\Delta\omega t-\theta_n)}{\Delta\omega}.\qquad\blacksquareϕn​(t)≈qmax​1​⋅2I0​cn​​⋅Δωsin(Δωt−θn​)​=2qmax​I0​cn​​⋅Δωsin(Δωt−θn​)​.■

Physical meaning (mixer view): the nnn-th ISF harmonic cnc_ncn​ acts like one tooth of an LO (local-oscillator) comb, downconverting noise at the injection frequency nω0+Δωn\omega_0+\Delta\omeganω0​+Δω to Δω\Delta\omegaΔω in baseband; the fast term (sum frequency ≈2nω0\approx2n\omega_0≈2nω0​) is filtered out by the integrator's low-pass action. The oscillator is itself a mixer sampling its own harmonics — this is why cnc_ncn​ is "the conversion coefficient from each harmonic to the phase output" (see fourier_series_of_isf).

Dimension check: the units of ϕn=I0cn2qmax⋅sin⁡(⋯ )Δω\phi_n=\dfrac{I_0 c_n}{2q_{max}}\cdot\dfrac{\sin(\cdots)}{\Delta\omega}ϕn​=2qmax​I0​cn​​⋅Δωsin(⋯)​ are [A]⋅(dimensionless)[C]⋅[rad/s]\dfrac{[\text{A}]\cdot(\text{dimensionless})}{[\text{C}]\cdot[\text{rad/s}]}[C]⋅[rad/s][A]⋅(dimensionless)​; substituting C=A⋅s\text{C}=\text{A·s}C=A⋅s and treating rad as dimensionless, =A(A⋅s)⋅(1/s)=AA=1=\dfrac{\text{A}}{(\text{A·s})\cdot(1/\text{s})}=\dfrac{\text{A}}{\text{A}}=1=(A⋅s)⋅(1/s)A​=AA​=1 (dimensionless), so ϕ\phiϕ is dimensionless (phase in rad) ✓.

import numpy as np
# Numerical check: do the integral directly; the slow term survives, the fast term vanishes
n, w0, dw, c_n, I0, qmax, th_n = 1, 1.0, 1e-3, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 0.4
tau = np.linspace(0, 2000*np.pi, 4_000_000)         # spans many slow periods
integrand = c_n*np.cos(n*w0*tau+th_n)*I0*np.cos((n*w0+dw)*tau)
phi = np.cumsum(integrand)*(tau[1]-tau[0])/qmax
analytic = I0*c_n/(2*qmax)*np.sin(dw*tau-th_n)/dw
print(np.max(np.abs(phi-analytic-np.mean(phi-analytic)))/np.max(np.abs(analytic)))
# -> slow-term envelope matches (relative error ~5e-4; the residual is the averaged-out fast term)
Exercise 7 solution (effective ISF, cyclostationary)

Step-by-step substitution. effective ISF ([P1] Eqs.(25)–(27)): Γeff(θ)=Γ(θ) α(θ)\Gamma_{eff}(\theta)=\Gamma(\theta)\,\alpha(\theta)Γeff​(θ)=Γ(θ)α(θ). Here α\alphaα is square-wave gating (conducting for half the cycle), so

Γeff(θ)={−sin⁡θ,θ∈[0,π)0,θ∈[π,2π).\Gamma_{eff}(\theta)=\begin{cases}-\sin\theta,&\theta\in[0,\pi)\\[2pt]0,&\theta\in[\pi,2\pi).\end{cases}Γeff​(θ)={−sinθ,0,​θ∈[0,π)θ∈[π,2π).​

Take the rms:

Γeff,rms2=12π∫02πΓeff2 dθ=12π∫0πsin⁡2θ dθ=12π⋅π2=14.\Gamma_{eff,rms}^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\Gamma_{eff}^2\,d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\frac14.Γeff,rms2​=2π1​∫02π​Γeff2​dθ=2π1​∫0π​sin2θdθ=2π1​⋅2π​=41​.Γeff,rms=12=0.5.\Gamma_{eff,rms}=\frac12=0.5.Γeff,rms​=21​=0.5.

Comparison: always conducting gives Γrms=1/2≈0.707\Gamma_{rms}=1/\sqrt2\approx0.707Γrms​=1/2​≈0.707; with half-cycle gating, Γeff,rms=0.5\Gamma_{eff,rms}=0.5Γeff,rms​=0.5. The ratio is 0.5/0.707=1/20.5/0.707=1/\sqrt20.5/0.707=1/2​ — gating off half the phase drops the rms by 2\sqrt22​ (halves the power).

Result: Γeff,rms=0.5\Gamma_{eff,rms}=0.5Γeff,rms​=0.5 (≈3\approx3≈3 dB lower in power than the always-on 0.7070.7070.707).

Design message: letting the noise conduct only at phases where the ISF is small greatly reduces its effective contribution — this is the design intuition of steering noise current away from the high-sensitivity region (the zero crossings) (see effective_isf). The square-wave gating here is an illustrative toy; the real NMF α(θ)\alpha(\theta)α(θ) is set by the device's bias-dependent thermal noise.

Dimension check: Γ\GammaΓ, α\alphaα, Γeff\Gamma_{eff}Γeff​ are all dimensionless ✓.
