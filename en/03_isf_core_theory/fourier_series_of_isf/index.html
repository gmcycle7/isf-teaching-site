Fourier Series of the ISF

β: This English translation is in beta — the Traditional-Chinese original is the authoritative version.

Prerequisites: isf_definition (Γ\GammaΓ is dimensionless and 2π2\pi2π-periodic), impulse_to_phase_shift (the operational definition of Γ\GammaΓ), convolution_derivation (the phase integral for continuous noise).

Hands-on verification: the Fourier-coefficient extraction, reconstruction, and Parseval numerical checks for this page are in lab_05.

The previous chapter impulse_to_phase_shift derived the operational definition of the ISF, Δϕ=Γ(ω0τ) Δq/qmax\Delta\phi=\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)\,\Delta q/q_{max}Δϕ=Γ(ω0​τ)Δq/qmax​, and pointed out that Γ\GammaΓ is a dimensionless, 2π2\pi2π-periodic function. This page answers the next key question:

Given that Γ\GammaΓ is periodic, once it is expanded as a Fourier series, what physics does each term represent? The answer is the most elegant piece of ISF theory — it spells out exactly which band of device noise the oscillator moves to the vicinity of the carrier. The core result ([P1] Eq.(12), p.183):

Γ(ω0τ)=c02+∑n=1∞cncos⁡(nω0τ+θn)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)=\frac{c_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_n\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)Γ(ω0​τ)=2c0​​+n=1∑∞​cn​cos(nω0​τ+θn​)

Physical intuition (conclusion first): an oscillator is a time-varying mixer. Its phase sensitivity to noise, Γ\GammaΓ, varies periodically with the waveform phase — equivalent to multiplying the noise by a "periodic weight" with fundamental ω0\omega_0ω0​. Multiplying noise by a periodic function is, mathematically, shifting the noise spectrum to 0, ω0, 2ω0,…0,\ \omega_0,\ 2\omega_0,\dots0, ω0​, 2ω0​,… and summing. So device noise near each nω0n\omega_0nω0​ is weighted by the nnn-th Fourier coefficient cnc_ncn​ and then down-converted to the carrier vicinity as phase noise. The c0c_0c0​ (DC) term is especially important: it moves the 1/f flicker noise near DC straight up, producing the close-in 1/f31/f^31/f3 phase noise.

Step 1: why Γ\GammaΓ can be expanded as a Fourier series​

Γ(ω0τ)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)Γ(ω0​τ) describes "how far the phase is pushed by a kick at a given point of the waveform". The oscillator sits in periodic steady state (the output waveform repeats every T=1/f0T=1/f_0T=1/f0​), so "kicking at phase xxx" and "kicking at phase x+2πx+2\pix+2π" have exactly the same effect. In other words Γ\GammaΓ is 2π2\pi2π-periodic in its argument x≡ω0τx\equiv\omega_0\taux≡ω0​τ:

Γ(x+2π)=Γ(x).\Gamma(x+2\pi)=\Gamma(x).Γ(x+2π)=Γ(x).
  • Math used: any 2π2\pi2π-periodic function that is square-integrable over one period (∫02π∣Γ∣2dx<∞\int_0^{2\pi}|\Gamma|^2dx<\infty∫02π​∣Γ∣2dx<∞) admits a Fourier series (Dirichlet conditions). Real ISFs are continuous and piecewise smooth, so the conditions are easily satisfied.
  • Physical basis: the periodicity comes from the oscillator's limit cycle — in steady state the state point goes around the cycle once per period, and the sensitivity depends only on the "position on the cycle (phase)", not on "which lap".
  • Unit check: x=ω0τx=\omega_0\taux=ω0​τ is [rad/s]⋅[s]=[rad][\text{rad/s}]\cdot[\text{s}]=[\text{rad}][rad/s]⋅[s]=[rad] ✓, dimensionless; Γ\GammaΓ itself is dimensionless, and so are c0,cnc_0,c_nc0​,cn​ (see notation).

Step 2: write the standard Fourier expansion (cos/sin form)​

First write the familiar real cos/sin Fourier series, then merge it into the amplitude–phase form of [P1]. For a 2π2\pi2π-periodic function:

Γ(x)=a02+∑n=1∞[ancos⁡(nx)+bnsin⁡(nx)],\Gamma(x)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\big],Γ(x)=2a0​​+n=1∑∞​[an​cos(nx)+bn​sin(nx)],

with the coefficients obtained by inner products (projections) over one period:

a0=1π∫02πΓ(x) dx,an=1π∫02πΓ(x)cos⁡(nx) dx,bn=1π∫02πΓ(x)sin⁡(nx) dx.a_0=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\Gamma(x)\,dx,\qquad a_n=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\Gamma(x)\cos(nx)\,dx,\qquad b_n=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\Gamma(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.a0​=π1​∫02π​Γ(x)dx,an​=π1​∫02π​Γ(x)cos(nx)dx,bn​=π1​∫02π​Γ(x)sin(nx)dx.
  • Math used: {cos⁡(nx),sin⁡(nx)}\{\cos(nx),\sin(nx)\}{cos(nx),sin(nx)} are orthogonal on [0,2π][0,2\pi][0,2π], ∫02πcos⁡(mx)cos⁡(nx) dx=π δmn\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(mx)\cos(nx)\,dx=\pi\,\delta_{mn}∫02π​cos(mx)cos(nx)dx=πδmn​ (m,n≥1m,n\ge1m,n≥1); this orthogonality "sifts" out the corresponding component.
  • Watch the 1/π1/\pi1/π and the DC factor: a0=1π∫02πΓ dxa_0=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\Gamma\,dxa0​=π1​∫02π​Γdx, while the mean (DC value) of Γ\GammaΓ is 12π∫02πΓ dx=a02\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\Gamma\,dx=\frac{a_0}{2}2π1​∫02π​Γdx=2a0​​. So the first term of the series must be written a02\frac{a_0}{2}2a0​​ to equal the true mean. This 1/21/21/2 is not arbitrary; the definition of c0c_0c0​ below inherits it.

Step 3: merge into amplitude–phase form to get cn,θnc_n,\theta_ncn​,θn​​

Combine the same-frequency pair ancos⁡(nx)+bnsin⁡(nx)a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)an​cos(nx)+bn​sin(nx) into a single cosine via the trig identity:

ancos⁡(nx)+bnsin⁡(nx)=cncos⁡(nx+θn),a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)=c_n\cos(nx+\theta_n),an​cos(nx)+bn​sin(nx)=cn​cos(nx+θn​),

where

cn=an2+bn2,θn=atan2⁡(−bn, an).c_n=\sqrt{a_n^2+b_n^2},\qquad \theta_n=\operatorname{atan2}(-b_n,\,a_n).cn​=an2​+bn2​​,θn​=atan2(−bn​,an​).
  • Math used: cncos⁡(nx+θn)=cncos⁡θncos⁡(nx)−cnsin⁡θnsin⁡(nx)c_n\cos(nx+\theta_n)=c_n\cos\theta_n\cos(nx)-c_n\sin\theta_n\sin(nx)cn​cos(nx+θn​)=cn​cosθn​cos(nx)−cn​sinθn​sin(nx); matching coefficients gives an=cncos⁡θna_n=c_n\cos\theta_nan​=cn​cosθn​, bn=−cnsin⁡θnb_n=-c_n\sin\theta_nbn​=−cn​sinθn​; inverting yields the expressions above.
  • DC correspondence: set c0≡a0c_0\equiv a_0c0​≡a0​ (the DC coefficient), so the constant term a02=c02\frac{a_0}{2}=\frac{c_0}{2}2a0​​=2c0​​. Substituting gives [P1] Eq.(12):
 Γ(ω0τ)=c02+∑n=1∞cncos⁡(nω0τ+θn) [P1] Eq.(12), p.183\boxed{\ \Gamma(\omega_0\tau)=\frac{c_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_n\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)\ }\qquad[\text{P1] Eq.(12), p.183} Γ(ω0​τ)=2c0​​+n=1∑∞​cn​cos(nω0​τ+θn​) ​[P1] Eq.(12), p.183
  • Notation trap (important): c0c_0c0​ is the Fourier coefficient, while the DC value of the ISF is c0/2c_0/2c0​/2. This factor is the easiest place to slip when computing the 1/f31/f^31/f3 corner (Eq.(24)); later chapters will keep reminding you. The a0 returned by this site's Python compute_fourier_coefficients equals this c0c_0c0​ (see Step 8).

Step 4: the physics of c0c_0c0​ — DC, controls 1/f1/f1/f upconversion​

Substitute the Fourier expansion of the ISF back into the LTV phase response ([P1] Eq.(11)→Eq.(13), p.183):

ϕ(t)=1qmax ⁣[c02 ⁣∫−∞t ⁣in dτ+∑n=1∞cn ⁣∫−∞t ⁣incos⁡(nω0τ+θn) dτ][P1] Eq.(13), p.183\phi(t)=\frac{1}{q_{max}}\!\left[\frac{c_0}{2}\!\int_{-\infty}^{t}\!i_n\,d\tau+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_n\!\int_{-\infty}^{t}\!i_n\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)\,d\tau\right]\qquad[\text{P1] Eq.(13), p.183}ϕ(t)=qmax​1​[2c0​​∫−∞t​in​dτ+n=1∑∞​cn​∫−∞t​in​cos(nω0​τ+θn​)dτ][P1] Eq.(13), p.183

The first term (the c0c_0c0​ term) integrates the noise current directly, without any cos⁡(nω0τ)\cos(n\omega_0\tau)cos(nω0​τ) modulation. Meaning:

  • The c0c_0c0​ term responds to the DC / very-low-frequency components of the noise current — push the node slowly and the phase drifts slowly.
  • Inject a small tone near DC, i(t)=I0cos⁡(Δωt)i(t)=I_0\cos(\Delta\omega t)i(t)=I0​cos(Δωt) (Δω≪ω0\Delta\omega\ll\omega_0Δω≪ω0​): only the c0c_0c0​ term responds (the other harmonics are averaged out by cos⁡(nω0τ)\cos(n\omega_0\tau)cos(nω0​τ)), giving ([P1] Eq.(15), p.183):
ϕ(t)≈I0 c0sin⁡(Δωt)2qmax Δω.\phi(t)\approx\frac{I_0\,c_0\sin(\Delta\omega t)}{2q_{max}\,\Delta\omega}.ϕ(t)≈2qmax​ΔωI0​c0​sin(Δωt)​.
  • Why this is called "1/f1/f1/f upconversion": device flicker / 1/f1/f1/f noise is concentrated at low frequency (near DC). The c0c_0c0​ term moves (up-converts) this "noise living at baseband" next to the carrier, turning it into close-in phase noise. Later, flicker_noise_upconversion uses this to derive the 1/f31/f^31/f3 skirt ([P1] Eq.(23),(24)).
  • Design implication: the smaller c0c_0c0​, the weaker the flicker upconversion. With c0=0c_0=0c0​=0 (a perfectly symmetric waveform), close-in theoretically has no 1/f31/f^31/f3 contribution. This is the mathematical root of the "waveform symmetry → close-in phase noise" design rule (experimentally supported by [P2] Fig. 17, p.802).

Step 5: the physics of cnc_ncn​ — frequency-translating noise near nω0n\omega_0nω0​ to the carrier​

Look at the nnn-th component of the second term of Eq.(13): it multiplies the noise current by cos⁡(nω0τ+θn)\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)cos(nω0​τ+θn​) and then integrates. Multiplying by cos⁡(nω0τ)\cos(n\omega_0\tau)cos(nω0​τ) is, in the frequency domain, shifting the noise spectrum by ±nω0\pm n\omega_0±nω0​ (modulation theorem). So:

  • Device noise near nω0+Δωn\omega_0+\Delta\omeganω0​+Δω (and nω0−Δωn\omega_0-\Delta\omeganω0​−Δω) is weighted by cnc_ncn​ and down-converted to Δω\Delta\omegaΔω, becoming the phase noise at offset Δω\Delta\omegaΔω from the carrier.
  • Inject a small tone near nω0n\omega_0nω0​, i(t)=I0cos⁡((nω0+Δω)t)i(t)=I_0\cos((n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)t)i(t)=I0​cos((nω0​+Δω)t), into Eq.(13): only the nnn-th harmonic responds, giving ([P1] Eq.(16)/(17), p.183):
ϕ(t)≈I0 cnsin⁡(Δωt)2qmax Δω.\phi(t)\approx\frac{I_0\,c_n\sin(\Delta\omega t)}{2q_{max}\,\Delta\omega}.ϕ(t)≈2qmax​ΔωI0​cn​sin(Δωt)​.

Explicit algebra: using cos⁡Acos⁡B\cos A\cos BcosAcosB to move noise near nω0n\omega_0nω0​ to the carrier​

The line above is the "result"; now we work the frequency-translation algebra out in full, not just the intuition. Take the nnn-th component of the second term of Eq.(13) and substitute the single tone in(τ)=I0cos⁡((nω0+Δω)τ)i_n(\tau)=I_0\cos((n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau)in​(τ)=I0​cos((nω0​+Δω)τ):

ϕn(t)=cnqmax∫−∞tcos⁡(nω0τ+θn) I0cos⁡((nω0+Δω)τ) dτ.\phi_n(t)=\frac{c_n}{q_{max}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)\,I_0\cos\big((n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau\big)\,d\tau .ϕn​(t)=qmax​cn​​∫−∞t​cos(nω0​τ+θn​)I0​cos((nω0​+Δω)τ)dτ.

Step (i): product-to-sum. The product of the two cosines uses the identity

cos⁡Acos⁡B=12[cos⁡(A−B)+cos⁡(A+B)],\cos A\cos B=\tfrac12\big[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)\big],cosAcosB=21​[cos(A−B)+cos(A+B)],

with A=nω0τ+θnA=n\omega_0\tau+\theta_nA=nω0​τ+θn​, B=(nω0+Δω)τB=(n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tauB=(nω0​+Δω)τ; compute the difference and sum frequencies:

A−B=θn−Δωτ(frequency≈Δω),A+B=(2nω0+Δω)τ+θn(frequency≈2nω0).A-B=\theta_n-\Delta\omega\tau\quad(\text{frequency}\approx\Delta\omega),\qquad A+B=(2n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau+\theta_n\quad(\text{frequency}\approx 2n\omega_0).A−B=θn​−Δωτ(frequency≈Δω),A+B=(2nω0​+Δω)τ+θn​(frequency≈2nω0​).

The integrand becomes two terms:

cos⁡(nω0τ+θn)cos⁡((nω0+Δω)τ)=12[cos⁡(Δωτ−θn)+cos⁡((2nω0+Δω)τ+θn)].\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)\cos\big((n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau\big)=\tfrac12\Big[\cos(\Delta\omega\tau-\theta_n)+\cos\big((2n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau+\theta_n\big)\Big].cos(nω0​τ+θn​)cos((nω0​+Δω)τ)=21​[cos(Δωτ−θn​)+cos((2nω0​+Δω)τ+θn​)].

This line is the core of "frequency translation": noise that originally lived at nω0+Δωn\omega_0+\Delta\omeganω0​+Δω, once multiplied by cos⁡(nω0τ)\cos(n\omega_0\tau)cos(nω0​τ), has its difference frequency collapse down to Δω\Delta\omegaΔω at baseband (moved down), while its sum frequency is pushed to 2nω02n\omega_02nω0​ (higher, useless).

Step (ii): the integrator amplifies only the slow term and averages out the fast one. ∫tdτ\int^t d\tau∫tdτ is a low-pass:

∫tcos⁡(Δωτ−θn) dτ=sin⁡(Δωt−θn)Δω (slow; small denominator⇒amplified, survives),\int^{t}\cos(\Delta\omega\tau-\theta_n)\,d\tau=\frac{\sin(\Delta\omega t-\theta_n)}{\Delta\omega}\ (\text{slow; small denominator}\Rightarrow\text{amplified, survives}),∫tcos(Δωτ−θn​)dτ=Δωsin(Δωt−θn​)​ (slow; small denominator⇒amplified, survives), ∫tcos⁡((2nω0+Δω)τ+θn) dτ=sin⁡(⋯ )2nω0+Δω (fast; denominator≈2nω0⇒suppressed to negligible).\int^{t}\cos\big((2n\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau+\theta_n\big)\,d\tau=\frac{\sin(\cdots)}{2n\omega_0+\Delta\omega}\ (\text{fast; denominator}\approx2n\omega_0\Rightarrow\text{suppressed to negligible}).∫tcos((2nω0​+Δω)τ+θn​)dτ=2nω0​+Δωsin(⋯)​ (fast; denominator≈2nω0​⇒suppressed to negligible).

Step (iii): keep only the slow term.

ϕn(t)≈cnqmax⋅I02⋅sin⁡(Δωt−θn)Δω=I0 cnsin⁡(Δωt−θn)2qmax Δω,\phi_n(t)\approx\frac{c_n}{q_{max}}\cdot\frac{I_0}{2}\cdot\frac{\sin(\Delta\omega t-\theta_n)}{\Delta\omega}=\frac{I_0\,c_n\sin(\Delta\omega t-\theta_n)}{2q_{max}\,\Delta\omega},ϕn​(t)≈qmax​cn​​⋅2I0​​⋅Δωsin(Δωt−θn​)​=2qmax​ΔωI0​cn​sin(Δωt−θn​)​,

Absorbing the fixed phase θn\theta_nθn​ into the origin recovers Eq.(16/17). Conclusion: the nnn-th Fourier coefficient cnc_ncn​ really does take the noise near nω0n\omega_0nω0​, "multiply it by 12cn\tfrac12 c_n21​cn​ and collapse it to Δω\Delta\omegaΔω" — this is the algebraic proof of frequency translation, matching the mixer picture below. (The same downconversion integral is used again in white_noise_to_phase_noise to accumulate the 1/f21/f^21/f2 summation.)

  • This is exactly the picture of [P1] Fig. 8 (p.183): noise is spread across the bands at 0,ω0,2ω0,…0,\omega_0,2\omega_0,\dots0,ω0​,2ω0​,…; the ISF's c0,c1,c2,…c_0,c_1,c_2,\dotsc0​,c1​,c2​,… act like a bank of mixer gains, each folding its band's noise back to the carrier vicinity, all superposing into the final phase-noise sideband.

(Remember C=A⋅sC=\text{A}\cdot\text{s}C=A⋅s.)

  • Why flicker becomes 1/f31/f^31/f3 through c0c_0c0​: flicker is already 1/f1/f1/f (power spectrum ∝1/Δω\propto1/\Delta\omega∝1/Δω).   • Note (mechanism of the toy model): the gamma_asymmetric in the table props c0c_0c0​ up in the simplest possible way — adding a DC offset to the whole ISF (cos⁡θ+α\cos\theta+\alphacosθ+α); in real circuits, asymmetric rise/fall transition slopes are a different — and more common — mechanism that likewise makes c0≠0c_0\neq0c0​=0 and turns on 1/f1/f1/f upconversion, but with a different waveform shape. The toy on this page exists only to isolate the "c0c_0c0​ knob".
  • This is exactly the message of the figure below: symmetric waveform c0=0c_0=0c0​=0, asymmetric waveform c0≠0c_0\neq0c0​=0.

Step 8: computing the coefficients by numerical integration (real function)​

Beyond the theory, here is code that runs. This site's compute_fourier_coefficients (read from simulations/common/isf_utils.py) simply carries out the Step-2 integrals with the trapezoidal rule:

import numpy as np
from simulations.common.isf_utils import (
    gamma_lc_ideal, gamma_asymmetric,
    compute_fourier_coefficients, reconstruct_from_fourier, gamma_rms,
)

# theta must span exactly one period [0, 2*pi] (endpoint included) for the trapezoidal rule to give the correct (1/pi)*∫ value
theta = np.linspace(0.0, 2 * np.pi, 4096, endpoint=True)

# Ideal LC ISF: Gamma(theta) = -sin(theta) (odd function)
gamma = gamma_lc_ideal(theta)

# a0 is c0 in Hajimiri's notation; a,b are the cos/sin coefficients; c_n = sqrt(a_n^2+b_n^2)
a0, a, b, c, phase = compute_fourier_coefficients(theta, gamma, n_harmonics=8)

print("c0 =", a0)            # -> ~0.0  (odd function: no DC, no 1/f upconversion)
print("c1 =", c[1])         # -> ~1.0  (fundamental component only)
print("c2..c8 =", c[2:])    # -> ~0    (pure single tone)

# Reconstruct the waveform to verify the Eq.(12) reconstruction
gamma_hat = reconstruct_from_fourier(theta, a0, a, b)
print("max reconstruction error =", np.max(np.abs(gamma_hat - gamma)))  # -> ~1e-15
  • Why the trapezoidal rule suffices: the integrand is a smooth periodic function, and the trapezoidal rule converges exponentially for periodic functions (the endpoint errors cancel); a few thousand points reach machine precision.
  • The endpoints must include 2π2\pi2π: the function docstring states explicitly that theta must span [0, 2*pi] (endpoint included), otherwise 1π∫\frac{1}{\pi}\intπ1​∫ misses one cell. This is the most common source of numerical off-by-one errors.
  • Asymmetric toy-model comparison: plug in gamma = gamma_asymmetric(theta, alpha=0.3) (i.e. cos⁡θ+0.3\cos\theta+0.3cosθ+0.3) and you get c0≈2α=0.6c_0\approx2\alpha=0.6c0​≈2α=0.6, c1≈1c_1\approx1c1​≈1, and the rest ≈0\approx0≈0 — confirming that α\alphaα is exactly the knob that "props up the DC and turns on 1/f1/f1/f upconversion" (this is a pedagogical toy model, not transistor-level).

Numerical example (building a feel for the numbers)​

Take the ideal-LC Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ and hand-compute the first few coefficients.

View −sin⁡x-\sin x−sinx as a Fourier expansion of itself: −sin⁡x=c1cos⁡(x+θ1)-\sin x=c_1\cos(x+\theta_1)−sinx=c1​cos(x+θ1​), where cos⁡(x+θ1)=cos⁡xcos⁡θ1−sin⁡xsin⁡θ1\cos(x+\theta_1)=\cos x\cos\theta_1-\sin x\sin\theta_1cos(x+θ1​)=cosxcosθ1​−sinxsinθ1​; for this to equal −sin⁡x-\sin x−sinx requires θ1=π/2\theta_1=\pi/2θ1​=π/2 (then cos⁡(x+π/2)=−sin⁡x\cos(x+\pi/2)=-\sin xcos(x+π/2)=−sinx), hence c1=1c_1=1c1​=1, θ1=π/2\theta_1=\pi/2θ1​=π/2.

  • c0=1π∫02π(−sin⁡x) dx=0c_0=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}(-\sin x)\,dx=0c0​=π1​∫02π​(−sinx)dx=0 (odd function, zero DC).

Key takeaways​

  • Γ\GammaΓ is a 2π2\pi2π-periodic function, expandable as Γ=c02+∑n≥1cncos⁡(nω0τ+θn)\Gamma=\dfrac{c_0}{2}+\sum_{n\ge1}c_n\cos(n\omega_0\tau+\theta_n)Γ=2c0​​+∑n≥1​cn​cos(nω0​τ+θn​) ([P1] Eq.(12), p.183).
  • The oscillator acts like a time-varying mixer: the nnn-th coefficient cnc_ncn​ down-converts device noise near nω0n\omega_0nω0​ to the carrier ([P1] Eq.(16), Fig. 8).
  • c0c_0c0​ (the DC coefficient; the DC value =c0/2=c_0/2=c0​/2) upconverts baseband flicker noise into close-in 1/f31/f^31/f3 ([P1] Eq.(15),(23),(24)).
  • Waveform symmetry sets the parity of the coefficients: odd function ⟹ c0=0c_0=0c0​=0 (no 1/f1/f1/f upconversion); half-wave symmetry ⟹ even harmonics vanish.
  • Ideal LC: c0=0, c1=1c_0=0,\ c_1=1c0​=0, c1​=1, the rest zero; Γrms=1/2≈0.707\Gamma_{rms}=1/\sqrt2\approx0.707Γrms​=1/2​≈0.707.
  • Numerically, compute_fourier_coefficients (trapezoidal rule; theta including both the 000 and 2π2\pi2π endpoints) computes the coefficients.
