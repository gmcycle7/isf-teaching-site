From Impulse to Phase Shift — the Derivation

β: This English translation is in beta — the Traditional-Chinese original is the authoritative version.

Prerequisites: oscillator_phase (limit-cycle and phase/amplitude geometry), lti_vs_ltv (why an oscillator is LTV with respect to noise) | Up next: isf_definition (the rigorous ISF definition and the multi-node picture) → convolution_derivation (superposing arbitrary noise by convolution)

This page answers a very concrete question: when a current impulse is injected into some node of an oscillator, by how much does the oscillator's phase shift? The answer is the operational definition at the heart of ISF theory:

Δϕ=Γ(ω0τ)qmax Δq\Delta\phi=\frac{\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)}{q_{max}}\,\Delta qΔϕ=qmax​Γ(ω0​τ)​Δq

We will not simply memorize this line — we will derive it from the capacitor relation q=Cvq=Cvq=Cv, one step at a time, stating at every step which physics is invoked, which approximation is made, what the units are, and how to run the dimension check.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): the noise current first becomes a packet of charge Δq\Delta qΔq on the node; that charge nudges the oscillator's instantaneous state slightly off its steady orbit; how much of that nudge turns into phase and how much into amplitude depends on at which phase of the waveform you deliver the kick — and that conversion ratio is precisely the ISF Γ\GammaΓ. Kick where the waveform slope is large and the phase gets pushed a lot; kick at the peak and you mostly perturb the amplitude.

Step 1: the current impulse becomes charge​

Current integrated over time is charge. A very narrow, very short current pulse i(t)i(t)i(t) injected into the node deposits a total charge

Δq=∫i(t) dt.\Delta q=\int i(t)\,dt .Δq=∫i(t)dt.
  • Physics used: charge conservation / the definition of current, i=dq/dti=dq/dti=dq/dt.
  • Unit check: [A]⋅[s]=[C][\text{A}]\cdot[\text{s}]=[\text{C}][A]⋅[s]=[C] ✓ (amperes times seconds are coulombs).
  • Why this is legitimate: when the pulse width is far smaller than the period TTT, the injection can be treated as dumping Δq\Delta qΔq onto the node "instantaneously".

Step 2: charge becomes a voltage step​

The node carries a total capacitance CnodeC_{node}Cnode​. Charge appearing instantaneously makes the node voltage jump by a step ([P1] Eq.(9), p.181):

ΔV=ΔqCnode.\Delta V=\frac{\Delta q}{C_{node}} .ΔV=Cnode​Δq​.
  • Physics used: the capacitor relation q=Cvq=Cvq=Cv; differentiate for a small change, Δq=C ΔV\Delta q=C\,\Delta VΔq=CΔV.
  • Unit check: [C]/[F]=[C]/[C/V]=[V][\text{C}]/[\text{F}]=[\text{C}]/[\text{C/V}]=[\text{V}][C]/[F]=[C]/[C/V]=[V] ✓.
  • Key observation: in a parallel LC, a current impulse changes only the capacitor voltage (instantaneously); it cannot change the inductor current (an inductor current cannot jump). In state space, the perturbation is therefore a horizontal displacement along the voltage axis.

Step 3: the voltage step pushes the state off the limit cycle — but only the tangential component becomes phase​

Draw the oscillator's state in a 2-D plane (say, horizontal axis = capacitor voltage vvv, vertical axis = www, proportional to the inductor current). In steady state, the state point circulates along the limit cycle. The voltage jump from Step 2 shoves the state point sideways:

  • The radial component, pushing away from the cycle → changes the amplitude AAA. But the oscillator has an amplitude-restoring mechanism, so this part is slowly pulled back and leaves no permanent residue.
  • The tangential component, along the cycle → changes the phase ϕ\phiϕ. Phase has no restoring force, so this part stays forever.

The same ΔV\Delta VΔV, kicked at a different phase τ\tauτ, splits differently between tangential and radial. Collect the "ΔV\Delta VΔV-to-Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ conversion ratio" into a periodic function that depends only on the injection phase — that is the ISF:

Δϕ=(phase-conversion ratio)⏟depends only on ω0τ×ΔV.\Delta\phi=\underbrace{\big(\text{phase-conversion ratio}\big)}_{\text{depends only on }\omega_0\tau}\times\Delta V .Δϕ=depends only on ω0​τ(phase-conversion ratio)​​×ΔV.

The figure below draws this decomposition on the state plane. The same packet of charge produces a horizontal ΔV=Δq/C\Delta V=\Delta q/CΔV=Δq/C (the current kicks only the capacitor-voltage axis; the inductor current cannot jump), which the dashed lines split into a tangential component along the cycle (green — permanent phase) and a radial component off the cycle (red — decays). Injected near the zero crossing, the horizontal kick lands almost entirely on the tangent (the phase gets pushed a lot); injected near the peak, it lands almost entirely on the radius (only the amplitude changes). The lower-left corner of each panel prints the value of Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ — the tangential fraction is exactly Γ(θ)\Gamma(\theta)Γ(θ), which makes the ideal-LC Γ=−sin⁡θ\Gamma=-\sin\thetaΓ=−sinθ geometrically inevitable (the algebraic derivation of Γ=−sin⁡θ\Gamma=-\sin\thetaΓ=−sinθ is in isf_definition).

  • Math used: project the perturbation vector onto the tangent direction (the phase direction) of the limit cycle.
  • Why only the tangential part is kept: see phase_vs_amplitude_noise and oscillator_phase — amplitude is pulled back by the restoring mechanism; phase is not.

Step 4: normalize by qmaxq_{max}qmax​ to get the dimensionless ISF​

Chain Steps 1–3 together: Δϕ∝ΔV=Δq/Cnode\Delta\phi\propto\Delta V=\Delta q/C_{node}Δϕ∝ΔV=Δq/Cnode​. Hajimiri–Lee chose to normalize by the node's maximum charge swing qmax=CnodeVmaxq_{max}=C_{node}V_{max}qmax​=Cnode​Vmax​, writing that "phase-conversion ratio" as the dimensionless function Γ(ω0τ)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)Γ(ω0​τ) ([P1] Eq.(10)–(11), p.182):

 Δϕ=Γ(ω0τ)qmax Δq \boxed{\ \Delta\phi=\frac{\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)}{q_{max}}\,\Delta q\ } Δϕ=qmax​Γ(ω0​τ)​Δq ​
  • Why the qmaxq_{max}qmax​ normalization: it turns Γ\GammaΓ into a dimensionless, amplitude-independent "shape" that describes only where the waveform is sensitive. The actual magnitude of the phase shift is set by Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ (the injected charge relative to the signal charge).
  • Why Γ\GammaΓ is dimensionless: Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ is in rad (dimensionless) and Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ is dimensionless, so Γ\GammaΓ must be dimensionless. Dimension check: [rad]=Γ⋅[C]/[C][\text{rad}]=\Gamma\cdot[\text{C}]/[\text{C}][rad]=Γ⋅[C]/[C] ⇒Γ\Rightarrow\Gamma⇒Γ dimensionless ✓.
  • Small-signal assumption: Step 3 treats the projection as linear, which requires Δq≪qmax\Delta q\ll q_{max}Δq≪qmax​ (the kick must not knock the oscillator over). [P1] Fig. 6 confirms, on an actual Colpitts and a 5-stage ring, that Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ is proportional to Δq\Delta qΔq for small charge.

The corresponding impulse response (setting up the next chapter)​

Because the phase step is retained forever, writing it as an impulse response brings along a unit step u(t−τ)u(t-\tau)u(t−τ) ([P1] Eq.(10)):

hϕ(t,τ)=Γ(ω0τ)qmax u(t−τ).h_\phi(t,\tau)=\frac{\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)}{q_{max}}\,u(t-\tau).hϕ​(t,τ)=qmax​Γ(ω0​τ)​u(t−τ).

Note that it depends on the absolute injection instant τ\tauτ (through Γ(ω0τ)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)Γ(ω0​τ)), not merely on t−τt-\taut−τ — the very signature of an LTV (linear time-variant) system. The next chapter, convolution_derivation, uses it to superpose an arbitrary noise current. The complete ISF definition and the multi-node discussion are in isf_definition.

Δϕ=Γ Δqqmax=0.5×(1×10−15 C)1×10−12 C=5×10−4 rad.\Delta\phi=\frac{\Gamma\,\Delta q}{q_{max}}=\frac{0.5\times(1\times10^{-15}\,\text{C})}{1\times10^{-12}\,\text{C}}=5\times10^{-4}\ \text{rad}.Δϕ=qmax​ΓΔq​=1×10−12C0.5×(1×10−15C)​=5×10−4 rad.

  • Dimension check: [rad]/[rad/s]=[s][\text{rad}]/[\text{rad/s}]=[\text{s}][rad]/[rad/s]=[s] ✓ (note that 2πf02\pi f_02πf0​ carries units of rad/s).
  • Feel for the numbers: at 5 GHz (a 200 ps period), a single 1 fC packet of charge (about 6240 electrons) causes only ~16 fs of timing error even at the most sensitive phase. One kick is tiny — but the noise kicks continuously, and the integral accumulates (next chapter).

dt   = phase_to_time_error(dphi, f0=5e9)
print(dphi, "rad", dt*1e15, "fs")   # -> 0.0005 rad  15.92 fs

Key takeaways​

  • Noise current → charge Δq\Delta qΔq → voltage jump ΔV=Δq/C\Delta V=\Delta q/CΔV=Δq/C → projected through the ISF into phase Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ.
  • Δϕ=Γ(ω0τ) Δq/qmax\Delta\phi=\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)\,\Delta q/q_{max}Δϕ=Γ(ω0​τ)Δq/qmax​; Γ\GammaΓ is dimensionless, 2π2\pi2π-periodic, and depends on the injection phase.
  • qmaxq_{max}qmax​ normalizes Γ\GammaΓ into a "shape"; the magnitude of the phase shift is set by Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​.
  • A single 1 fC at 5 GHz, with Γ=0.5\Gamma=0.5Γ=0.5 and qmax=1q_{max}=1qmax​=1 pC → 16 fs.
