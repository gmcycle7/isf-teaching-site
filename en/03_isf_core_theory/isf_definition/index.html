The Definition of the ISF

β: This English translation is in beta — the Traditional-Chinese original is the authoritative version.

Prerequisites: oscillator_phase (the geometry of the limit cycle and excess phase), phase_vs_amplitude_noise (the tangential-phase vs radial-amplitude decomposition), impulse_to_phase_shift (the operational charge→voltage→phase chain).

This page answers a question that sounds simple and runs deep: what exactly is the ISF (Impulse Sensitivity Function)? It is a function — but what is its argument, what does its value represent, what are its units, why is it periodic, and why does every node and every noise source get one of its own?

The ISF is the central object of Hajimiri–Lee's 1998 LTV (Linear Time-Variant) phase-noise theory. Its operational definition: inject a packet of charge Δq\Delta qΔq at waveform phase ω0τ\omega_0\tauω0​τ, and the resulting permanent phase shift Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ is ([P1] from Eq.(10)-(11), p.182):

Δϕ=Γ(ω0τ)qmax Δq\Delta\phi=\frac{\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)}{q_{max}}\,\Delta qΔϕ=qmax​Γ(ω0​τ)​Δq

Written as an impulse response, this is [P1] Eq.(10), p.182:

hϕ(t,τ)=Γ(ω0τ)qmax u(t−τ)h_\phi(t,\tau)=\frac{\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)}{q_{max}}\,u(t-\tau)hϕ​(t,τ)=qmax​Γ(ω0​τ)​u(t−τ)

Physical intuition (conclusion first): in steady state, the oscillator's state point travels round and round a closed trajectory (the limit cycle). Poke it with a finger (a current impulse) — the resulting displacement splits into a component tangential to the trajectory and a component radial, perpendicular to it. The tangential part changes how far along the loop the state has turned — that is, the phase — and because phase has no restoring force, this shift stays forever. The radial part changes the amplitude and gets slowly pulled back by the oscillator's amplitude-restoring mechanism, leaving no trace. Γ(ω0τ)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)Γ(ω0​τ) is exactly the sensitivity weight answering "poke one unit of charge at phase ω0τ\omega_0\tauω0​τ — how much becomes permanent phase?" It is not the noise itself; it is the conversion coefficient that translates noise into phase.

That is the entire intuition behind Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ and LTV behavior.

The complete step-by-step charge→voltage→phase derivation lives in impulse_to_phase_shift. This page concentrates on what Γ\GammaΓ is and what properties Γ\GammaΓ has, and computes the ideal-LC Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ from the geometry by hand.

Step 1: from impulse to state perturbation (recap of the key physics)​

Run quickly through the chain from the previous page, because the definition of Γ\GammaΓ stands on it:

  1. current impulse → charge: a very narrow current pulse deposits a charge Δq=∫i(t) dt\Delta q=\int i(t)\,dtΔq=∫i(t)dt. Units [A]⋅[s]=[C][\text{A}]\cdot[\text{s}]=[\text{C}][A]⋅[s]=[C] ✓.
  2. charge → voltage step: on the node capacitance CnodeC_{node}Cnode​ the voltage jumps instantaneously by ΔV=Δq/Cnode\Delta V=\Delta q/C_{node}ΔV=Δq/Cnode​ ([P1] Eq.(9), p.182). Units [C]/[F]=[V][\text{C}]/[\text{F}]=[\text{V}][C]/[F]=[V] ✓.
  3. voltage step → state perturbation: in an LC, a current impulse can only change the capacitor voltage instantaneously (the inductor current cannot jump), so the perturbation is a horizontal displacement along the voltage axis in state space.

At this point the state has been nudged slightly off the limit cycle. The key question: how much of that displacement becomes phase?

Step 2: projection onto the phase direction​

Draw the oscillator state as a 2-D vector z=(v,w)\mathbf{z}=(v,w)z=(v,w), where vvv is the capacitor voltage and www is proportional to the inductor current. The steady-state trajectory is a closed loop, and the state moves along it at angular rate ω0\omega_0ω0​. Define the phase on the loop, θ=ω0t\theta=\omega_0 tθ=ω0​t, as "the angle you have turned to".

A small displacement along the voltage axis, Δz=(ΔV,0)\Delta\mathbf{z}=(\Delta V,0)Δz=(ΔV,0), strikes the loop at some point. Decompose this displacement at that point into the tangential direction (the phase direction, i.e. the direction of ∂z/∂θ\partial\mathbf{z}/\partial\theta∂z/∂θ) and the normal direction (the amplitude direction):

Δz=(Δz⋅t^)⏟tangential→phaset^+(Δz⋅n^)⏟normal→amplitude (decays)n^.\Delta\mathbf{z}=\underbrace{(\Delta\mathbf{z}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{t}})}_{\text{tangential→phase}}\hat{\mathbf{t}}+\underbrace{(\Delta\mathbf{z}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})}_{\text{normal→amplitude (decays)}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}.Δz=tangential→phase(Δz⋅t^)​​t^+normal→amplitude (decays)(Δz⋅n^)​​n^.
  • Math used: project the perturbation vector onto the unit tangent t^\hat{\mathbf{t}}t^ of the limit cycle. This is the embryo of the rigorous machinery behind it — the PPV (perturbation projection vector) / Floquet theory (PPV/adjoint/Floquet are not in the five downloaded PDFs; they belong to Demir et al., external literature — see effective_isf).
  • Why only the tangential part is kept: the normal component changes "how far from the loop" — the amplitude; a stable oscillation always has amplitude restoring that pulls it back (see phase_vs_amplitude_noise). The tangential component changes "the angle along the loop" — the phase; phase is the neutral direction with no restoring force, so the shift is retained permanently and accumulates (claim C2, [P1] Sec. III-A).

Divide the tangential projection by "how much state displacement corresponds to one unit of phase along the loop", and ΔV\Delta VΔV converts into Δθ=Δϕ\Delta\theta=\Delta\phiΔθ=Δϕ. The entire conversion depends only on at which angle of the loop you strike — and that is why Γ\GammaΓ is a function of ω0τ\omega_0\tauω0​τ alone.

Step 3: normalize the ratio into the dimensionless Γ\GammaΓ​

Chain Steps 1–2 together: Δϕ∝ΔV=Δq/Cnode\Delta\phi\propto\Delta V=\Delta q/C_{node}Δϕ∝ΔV=Δq/Cnode​, with a proportionality coefficient that depends only on the injection phase. Hajimiri–Lee normalize by the node's maximum charge swing qmax=CnodeVmaxq_{max}=C_{node}V_{max}qmax​=Cnode​Vmax​ and define the dimensionless function Γ(ω0τ)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)Γ(ω0​τ):

 Δϕ=Γ(ω0τ)qmax Δq \boxed{\ \Delta\phi=\frac{\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)}{q_{max}}\,\Delta q\ } Δϕ=qmax​Γ(ω0​τ)​Δq ​
  • Why Γ\GammaΓ is dimensionless: Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ is in rad (dimensionless) and Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ is a charge ratio (dimensionless), so Γ\GammaΓ must be dimensionless. Dimension check: [rad]=Γ⋅[C]/[C]⇒Γ[\text{rad}]=\Gamma\cdot[\text{C}]/[\text{C}]\Rightarrow\Gamma[rad]=Γ⋅[C]/[C]⇒Γ dimensionless ✓.
  • Why normalize by qmaxq_{max}qmax​: it lets Γ\GammaΓ describe only the shape of "where the waveform is sensitive", decoupled from the absolute amplitude; the actual size of the phase shift is set by Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ (the injected charge relative to the signal charge). This also yields the design conclusion C3 directly: phase noise ∝Γrms2/qmax2\propto\Gamma_{rms}^2/q_{max}^2∝Γrms2​/qmax2​ — to push it down, "raise qmaxq_{max}qmax​, shrink Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​" ([P1] Eq.(21)).

"Not the noise itself" is the most common misconception. Γ\GammaΓ is a deterministic periodic function fixed by the circuit structure and its waveform — it has nothing to do with how large the noise is, or whether it is white or flicker. Swapping the noise source (changing ini_nin​) does not change Γ\GammaΓ; it changes the resulting ϕ\phiϕ. Only changing the injection node (changing the projection geometry) changes Γ\GammaΓ.

Hands-on derivation: Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ for the ideal LC​

A theory must not merely sound elegant. Here we compute Γ\GammaΓ end to end for a lossless parallel LC, the reference waveform for the entire site.

Setup: the ideal LC state executes uniform circular motion (harmonic resonance); write it as

z(θ)=A (cos⁡θ, sin⁡θ),θ=ω0t,\mathbf{z}(\theta)=A\,(\cos\theta,\ \sin\theta),\qquad \theta=\omega_0 t,z(θ)=A(cosθ, sinθ),θ=ω0​t,

where the first component v=Acos⁡θv=A\cos\thetav=Acosθ is the capacitor voltage (output waveform ∝cos⁡θ\propto\cos\theta∝cosθ).

Step A — the state displacement caused by the injection: the current impulse changes only the capacitor voltage, Δv=Δq/C\Delta v=\Delta q/CΔv=Δq/C, so

Δz=(Δv, 0)=(ΔqC, 0).\Delta\mathbf{z}=(\Delta v,\,0)=\Big(\tfrac{\Delta q}{C},\,0\Big).Δz=(Δv,0)=(CΔq​,0).

Step B — project onto the tangent. The tangent vector along the loop:

∂z∂θ=A (−sin⁡θ, cos⁡θ),∣∂z∂θ∣=A.\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial\theta}=A\,(-\sin\theta,\ \cos\theta),\qquad \left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial\theta}\right|=A.∂θ∂z​=A(−sinθ, cosθ),​∂θ∂z​​=A.

The phase increment Δθ\Delta\thetaΔθ obeys "tangential displacement = tangential speed × phase increment": dot Δz\Delta\mathbf{z}Δz into the unit tangent, then divide by ∣∂z/∂θ∣|\partial\mathbf{z}/\partial\theta|∣∂z/∂θ∣:

Δϕ=Δθ=Δz⋅(∂z/∂θ)∣∂z/∂θ∣2=(Δv,0)⋅A(−sin⁡θ,cos⁡θ)A2=−Asin⁡θ ΔvA2=−sin⁡θA Δv.\Delta\phi=\Delta\theta=\frac{\Delta\mathbf{z}\cdot(\partial\mathbf{z}/\partial\theta)}{|\partial\mathbf{z}/\partial\theta|^2}=\frac{(\Delta v,0)\cdot A(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)}{A^2}=\frac{-A\sin\theta\,\Delta v}{A^2}=\frac{-\sin\theta}{A}\,\Delta v.Δϕ=Δθ=∣∂z/∂θ∣2Δz⋅(∂z/∂θ)​=A2(Δv,0)⋅A(−sinθ,cosθ)​=A2−AsinθΔv​=A−sinθ​Δv.

Step-by-step algebra (every equals sign above unpacked — no skipped steps):

numerator (dot product): (Δv, 0)⋅A(−sin⁡θ, cos⁡θ)=Δv⋅(−Asin⁡θ)+0⋅(Acos⁡θ)=−Asin⁡θ Δv,denominator: ∣∂z∂θ∣2=(A(−sin⁡θ))2+(Acos⁡θ)2=A2(sin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ)=A2,divide: Δϕ=−Asin⁡θ ΔvA2=−sin⁡θA Δv.\begin{aligned} \text{numerator (dot product)}&:\ (\Delta v,\,0)\cdot A(-\sin\theta,\ \cos\theta) =\Delta v\cdot(-A\sin\theta)+0\cdot(A\cos\theta)=-A\sin\theta\,\Delta v,\\ \text{denominator}&:\ \left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial\theta}\right|^2=\big(A(-\sin\theta)\big)^2+\big(A\cos\theta\big)^2=A^2(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta)=A^2,\\ \text{divide}&:\ \Delta\phi=\frac{-A\sin\theta\,\Delta v}{A^2}=\frac{-\sin\theta}{A}\,\Delta v. \end{aligned}numerator (dot product)denominatordivide​: (Δv,0)⋅A(−sinθ, cosθ)=Δv⋅(−Asinθ)+0⋅(Acosθ)=−AsinθΔv,: ​∂θ∂z​​2=(A(−sinθ))2+(Acosθ)2=A2(sin2θ+cos2θ)=A2,: Δϕ=A2−AsinθΔv​=A−sinθ​Δv.​
  • Why divide by ∣∂z/∂θ∣2|\partial\mathbf z/\partial\theta|^2∣∂z/∂θ∣2 rather than ∣∂z/∂θ∣|\partial\mathbf z/\partial\theta|∣∂z/∂θ∣: first dot into the unit tangent t^=∂z/∂θ∣∂z/∂θ∣\hat{\mathbf t}=\dfrac{\partial\mathbf z/\partial\theta}{|\partial\mathbf z/\partial\theta|}t^=∣∂z/∂θ∣∂z/∂θ​ to get the length of the tangential displacement, then divide by "the arc length per unit θ\thetaθ along the loop, ∣∂z/∂θ∣|\partial\mathbf z/\partial\theta|∣∂z/∂θ∣" to convert into Δθ\Delta\thetaΔθ; the two factors of ∣∂z/∂θ∣|\partial\mathbf z/\partial\theta|∣∂z/∂θ∣ combine into the squared denominator.
  • sin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=1\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1sin2θ+cos2θ=1 is the identity that lets the denominator collapse cleanly to A2A^2A2 (the uniform speed of circular motion).

Step C — substitute Δv=Δq/C\Delta v=\Delta q/CΔv=Δq/C:

Δϕ=−sin⁡θA⋅ΔqC=−sin⁡θAC Δq.\Delta\phi=\frac{-\sin\theta}{A}\cdot\frac{\Delta q}{C}=\frac{-\sin\theta}{AC}\,\Delta q.Δϕ=A−sinθ​⋅CΔq​=AC−sinθ​Δq.

Step D — recognize qmaxq_{max}qmax​: the node's maximum charge swing is qmax=C Vmax=CAq_{max}=C\,V_{max}=C Aqmax​=CVmax​=CA. Substituting:

Δϕ=−sin⁡θqmax Δq⟹ Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ \Delta\phi=\frac{-\sin\theta}{q_{max}}\,\Delta q\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{\ \Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\theta\ }Δϕ=qmax​−sinθ​Δq⟹ Γ(θ)=−sinθ ​

which lands exactly on the definition Δϕ=Γ(θ) Δq/qmax\Delta\phi=\Gamma(\theta)\,\Delta q/q_{max}Δϕ=Γ(θ)Δq/qmax​. Dimension check: Γ=−sin⁡θ\Gamma=-\sin\thetaΓ=−sinθ dimensionless ✓; Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ dimensionless ✓; Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ rad ✓.

How to read this −sin⁡θ-\sin\theta−sinθ (matching the intuition of [P1] Fig. 4, p.181):

  • Inject at the peak (θ=0\theta=0θ=0, where the output v=Acos⁡θv=A\cos\thetav=Acosθ is maximal): Γ(0)=0\Gamma(0)=0Γ(0)=0. The finger pokes along the voltage axis, almost perpendicular to the trajectory (purely radial) → it changes only the amplitude and barely touches the phase. The amplitude disturbance gets pulled back, so this poke "leaves no permanent trace".
  • Inject at the zero crossing (θ=π/2\theta=\pi/2θ=π/2, v=0v=0v=0, maximum waveform slope): ∣Γ∣=1|\Gamma|=1∣Γ∣=1 (maximal). The poke along the voltage axis is almost tangent to the trajectory (purely tangential) → nearly all of it becomes a permanent phase jump.
  • In between: the tangential/radial split follows −sin⁡θ-\sin\theta−sinθ continuously.

This is the essence of LTV: the same Δq\Delta qΔq, injected at a different instant (different θ\thetaθ), produces a completely different effect. No LTI system exhibits this "it matters when you strike" behavior. See lti_vs_ltv.

Notation trap: [P3] writes Γ(θ+ϕ)\Gamma(\theta+\phi)Γ(θ+ϕ), taking "the injection waveform phase θ\thetaθ" plus "the oscillator's own excess phase ϕ\phiϕ" as the argument — in essence it is still the same Γ\GammaΓ, only with the argument recast as a relative phase. The APF Λ\LambdaΛ of [P4] is the amplitude sensitivity, complementary to Γ\GammaΓ (the phase sensitivity); in the ideal LC the two are orthogonal (one ∝sin⁡\propto\sin∝sin, the other ∝cos⁡\propto\cos∝cos). Format per spec §10.4: problem → step-by-step substitution (with units) → result → dimension check → one-line Python verification.

Example 1: Γ=−sin⁡\Gamma=-\sinΓ=−sin at θ=0,π/4,π/2\theta=0,\pi/4,\pi/2θ=0,π/4,π/2 and the resulting Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ​

Problem: the ideal LC has Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ. Inject the same packet of charge at three phases — θ=0\theta=0θ=0 (peak), θ=π/4\theta=\pi/4θ=π/4 (halfway), θ=π/2\theta=\pi/2θ=π/2 (zero crossing) — with the injected-charge ratio fixed at Δq/qmax=10−3\Delta q/q_{max}=10^{-3}Δq/qmax​=10−3. Find Γ\GammaΓ and the phase step Δϕ=Γ⋅Δq/qmax\Delta\phi=\Gamma\cdot\Delta q/q_{max}Δϕ=Γ⋅Δq/qmax​ at each point.

Step-by-step substitution: compute Γ\GammaΓ first, then multiply by Δq/qmax=10−3\Delta q/q_{max}=10^{-3}Δq/qmax​=10−3.

θ=0:Γ=−sin⁡0=0,Δϕ=0×10−3=0 rad.θ=π4:Γ=−sin⁡π4=−12≈−0.7071,Δϕ=−0.7071×10−3=−7.07×10−4 rad.θ=π2:Γ=−sin⁡π2=−1,Δϕ=−1×10−3=−1.0×10−3 rad.\begin{aligned} \theta=0:\quad &\Gamma=-\sin0=0, &\Delta\phi&=0\times10^{-3}=0\ \text{rad}.\\ \theta=\tfrac{\pi}{4}:\quad &\Gamma=-\sin\tfrac{\pi}{4}=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\approx-0.7071, &\Delta\phi&=-0.7071\times10^{-3}=-7.07\times10^{-4}\ \text{rad}.\\ \theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}:\quad &\Gamma=-\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}=-1, &\Delta\phi&=-1\times10^{-3}=-1.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{rad}. \end{aligned}θ=0:θ=4π​:θ=2π​:​Γ=−sin0=0,Γ=−sin4π​=−2​1​≈−0.7071,Γ=−sin2π​=−1,​ΔϕΔϕΔϕ​=0×10−3=0 rad.=−0.7071×10−3=−7.07×10−4 rad.=−1×10−3=−1.0×10−3 rad.​

Result: the same packet of charge barely moves the phase at the peak (Δϕ=0\Delta\phi=0Δϕ=0), delivers the maximum phase step at the zero crossing (∣Δϕ∣=1|\Delta\phi|=1∣Δϕ∣=1 mrad), and lands in between at the halfway point (0.7070.7070.707 mrad). This is the core LTV phenomenon: the effect is decided by when you strike.

Dimension check: Γ\GammaΓ dimensionless, Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ dimensionless → Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ dimensionless (rad) ✓. The minus sign means the phase is pushed backward (it lags); the order of magnitude is set by Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​, the same order as Example A's 5×10−45\times10^{-4}5×10−4 rad (Example A uses Γ=0.5\Gamma=0.5Γ=0.5).

import numpy as np
from simulations.common.isf_utils import gamma_lc_ideal, impulse_to_phase_step
theta = np.array([0.0, np.pi/4, np.pi/2])
g = gamma_lc_ideal(theta)                       # -> [ 0.    -0.7071 -1.    ]
dphi = impulse_to_phase_step(delta_q=1e-3, gamma_value=g, qmax=1.0)  # Δq/qmax = 1e-3
print(g)                                        # ISF values
print(dphi)                                     # -> [ 0.  -7.07e-04  -1.0e-03 ] rad

Key takeaways​

  • Γ(ω0τ)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)Γ(ω0​τ) = the sensitivity weight answering "inject one unit of charge at waveform phase ω0τ\omega_0\tauω0​τ — how much becomes permanent phase?"
  • The derivation chain: impulse → charge Δq\Delta qΔq → voltage step ΔV\Delta VΔV → state displacement → projection onto the tangent (the phase direction) → permanent phase Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ.
  • Γ\GammaΓ is dimensionless, 2π2\pi2π-periodic, not the noise itself, set by the large-signal periodic operating point, and there is one per node / per noise source.
  • Ideal LC: Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ — peak injection gives Γ=0\Gamma=0Γ=0 (amplitude only), zero crossing gives ∣Γ∣=1|\Gamma|=1∣Γ∣=1 (maximum phase) — that is LTV.
  • Across the papers: [P1][P2] use Γ\GammaΓ for phase noise; [P3] applies the same Γ\GammaΓ to injection; [P4]'s APF Λ\LambdaΛ is the amplitude counterpart; [P5] is unrelated to the ISF.
  • Sources: [P1] Eqs.(10),(11), p.182; verification figures in lab_02 / lab_04.
