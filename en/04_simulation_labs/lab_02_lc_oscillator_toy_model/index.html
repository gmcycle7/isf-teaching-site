Lab 02 — Ideal LC oscillator toy model: Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ and charge linearity

lab_01 gave the geometric intuition: phase = tangential, amplitude = radial. This lab turns that into a concrete function. For an ideal lossless parallel LC oscillator, the permanent phase shift caused by injecting a current impulse into the capacitor node can be written fully analytically, giving the ISF:

Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ,θ=ω0τ.\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\theta,\qquad \theta=\omega_0\tau.Γ(θ)=−sinθ,θ=ω0​τ.

This is the site's first ISF that "actually has a shape", and it is not a toy assumption — it is derived from the state geometry. We will (a) plot the waveform and its ISF, (b) verify numerically that in the small-signal regime Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ is strictly proportional to Δq\Delta qΔq, and (c) watch in state space how a zero-crossing injection becomes a "pure phase jump".

Physical intuition (conclusion first): the LC state z=(v,w)=A(cos⁡θ,sin⁡θ)z=(v,w)=A(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)z=(v,w)=A(cosθ,sinθ) rotates at constant speed on a circle. A current impulse pushes only the capacitor voltage vvv, one horizontal step Δv=Δq/C\Delta v=\Delta q/CΔv=Δq/C. How much of that step becomes phase depends on the angle, at that instant, between the radius (pointing outward from the center) and the tangential direction (perpendicular to the radius). At the peak (θ=0\theta=0θ=0, v=Av=Av=A) the step lies entirely along the radius → all amplitude; at the zero crossing (θ=π/2\theta=\pi/2θ=π/2, v=0v=0v=0) the step lies entirely along the tangent → all phase. Writing this projection as a phase increment gives exactly −sin⁡θ-\sin\theta−sinθ.

1. Learning objectives​

  • Derive from physics the ideal-LC ISF Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ — no memorization, no assumptions.
  • Understand how the "shape" of the ISF maps onto the waveform: ∣Γ∣|\Gamma|∣Γ∣ is largest at the zero crossings and zero at the peaks.
  • Verify small-signal linearity by simulation: Δϕ=Γ Δq/qmax\Delta\phi=\Gamma\,\Delta q/q_{max}Δϕ=ΓΔq/qmax​, i.e. at a fixed injection phase Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ is proportional to Δq\Delta qΔq (reproducing the conclusion of [P1] Fig. 6).
  • See in state space that "zero-crossing injection = a tangential push = a pure phase jump, trajectory radius unchanged".
  • Prepare a clean, analytically tractable example for convolution_derivation and the Fourier decomposition of the ISF (lab_05).

2. Mathematical model​

Ideal lossless parallel LC: energy shuttles losslessly between LLL and CCC; the state traces a circle at constant speed in the 2-D plane. In normalized state form (A=1A=1A=1):

z(θ)=(v, w)=(cos⁡θ,  sin⁡θ),θ=ω0t.z(\theta)=(v,\,w)=(\cos\theta,\;\sin\theta),\qquad \theta=\omega_0 t .z(θ)=(v,w)=(cosθ,sinθ),θ=ω0​t.
  • vvv = capacitor voltage (∝cos⁡θ\propto\cos\theta∝cosθ); www = (proportional to) inductor current (∝sin⁡θ\propto\sin\theta∝sinθ).
  • Here μ=0\mu=0μ=0 (no amplitude restoration), corresponding to the "marginally stable ideal LC". In the simulations, to keep the trajectory cleanly on the ring, we use a small μ=0.3\mu=0.3μ=0.3 (weak restoration) in (a)(b), and μ=0\mu=0μ=0 in (c) to watch pure rotation.

Impulse → voltage step ([P1] Eq.(9), p.182):

ΔV=ΔqCnode.\Delta V=\frac{\Delta q}{C_{node}}.ΔV=Cnode​Δq​.

It moves only vvv (the inductor current cannot change instantaneously), i.e. the state jumps by Δv=Δq/C\Delta v=\Delta q/CΔv=Δq/C along the +v+v+v axis.

Projecting the voltage step onto phase. At z=(cos⁡θ,sin⁡θ)z=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)z=(cosθ,sinθ), the unit vector along the tangential (phase-increasing) direction is t^=(−sin⁡θ,cos⁡θ)\hat t=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)t^=(−sinθ,cosθ). A step along the +v+v+v axis, Δv v^=Δv (1,0)\Delta v\,\hat v=\Delta v\,(1,0)Δvv^=Δv(1,0), has tangential projection (divided by the radius A=1A=1A=1 to convert into an angle increment):

Δϕ=t^⋅(Δv,0)A=(−sin⁡θ) ΔvA=−sin⁡θ  ΔvA.\Delta\phi=\frac{\hat t\cdot(\Delta v,0)}{A}=\frac{(-\sin\theta)\,\Delta v}{A}=-\sin\theta\;\frac{\Delta v}{A}.Δϕ=At^⋅(Δv,0)​=A(−sinθ)Δv​=−sinθAΔv​.

Substituting Δv=Δq/C\Delta v=\Delta q/CΔv=Δq/C and using qmax=C⋅Aq_{max}=C\cdot Aqmax​=C⋅A:

  Δϕ=−sin⁡θ  Δq/CA=−sin⁡θCA Δq=Γ(θ)qmax Δq,Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ.  \boxed{\;\Delta\phi=-\sin\theta\;\frac{\Delta q/C}{A}=\frac{-\sin\theta}{C A}\,\Delta q=\frac{\Gamma(\theta)}{q_{max}}\,\Delta q,\qquad \Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\theta.\;}Δϕ=−sinθAΔq/C​=CA−sinθ​Δq=qmax​Γ(θ)​Δq,Γ(θ)=−sinθ.​
  • dimension check: Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ is in rad (dimensionless); Δq/qmax=[C]/[C]\Delta q/q_{max}=[\text{C}]/[\text{C}]Δq/qmax​=[C]/[C] is dimensionless; hence Γ\GammaΓ is dimensionless ✓. Γ\GammaΓ is 2π2\pi2π-periodic, consistent with the definitions in [P1] Eq.(10),(11).
  • Physics check: θ=0\theta=0θ=0 (peak) → Γ=0\Gamma=0Γ=0 (amplitude change only, matching the red dot in lab_01 figure two); θ=π/2\theta=\pi/2θ=π/2 (rising zero crossing) → Γ=−1\Gamma=-1Γ=−1 (∣Γ∣|\Gamma|∣Γ∣ maximal, pure phase, matching the green dot). The minus sign is the sign convention for "phase being pushed backward".

Toy-model note: ideal lossless LC + pure 2-D rotation + small-signal projection is a pedagogical toy model, not transistor-level. A real LC has finite QQQ, harmonics, and cyclostationary noise, which pull Γ\GammaΓ away from the clean −sin⁡θ-\sin\theta−sinθ (see effective_isf).

4. Core Python code​

Excerpted from simulations/lab_02_lc_toy_model.py (checked against the source). The ideal-LC ISF comes directly from gamma_lc_ideal (i.e. −sin⁡θ-\sin\theta−sinθ); for linearity, simulate_lc injects a range of Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ at a fixed zero-crossing phase, and excess_phase measures the persistent phase shift:

from oscillator_models import simulate_lc, excess_phase
from simulations.common.isf_utils import gamma_lc_ideal

f0, fs = 1.0, 8000.0
theta = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 400)

# (a) waveform cos(theta) and its ISF -sin(theta)
V     = np.cos(theta)
Gamma = gamma_lc_ideal(theta)          # = -np.sin(theta)

# (b) linearity: inject a series of dq/qmax at the zero crossing (theta=pi/2, ISF=-1), measure persistent Δφ
dq_list = np.linspace(-0.05, 0.05, 11)            # dq / q_max
T = 1.0 / f0
t_inj = 4 * T + (np.pi / 2) / (2 * np.pi * f0)    # injection time corresponding to theta=pi/2
t_ref, xr, yr = simulate_lc(f0, 10 * T, fs, mu=0.3)
phi_ref = excess_phase(t_ref, xr, yr, f0)

dphi = []
for dq in dq_list:
    t_p, xp, yp = simulate_lc(f0, 10 * T, fs, mu=0.3,
                              impulse_time=t_inj, impulse_dx=dq)
    phi_p = excess_phase(t_p, xp, yp, f0)
    m = t_p >= (10 * T - T)                        # take the settled value over the last period
    dphi.append(np.mean(phi_p[m] - phi_ref[m]))
# theory: Δφ = Γ·Δq/q_max = -1 · (dq/q_max)

excess_phase subtracts the carrier rotation 2πf0t2\pi f_0 t2πf0​t, keeping only the perturbation-induced phase (ϕ=unwrap⁡(atan2⁡(y,x))−2πf0t\phi=\operatorname{unwrap}(\operatorname{atan2}(y,x))-2\pi f_0 tϕ=unwrap(atan2(y,x))−2πf0​t) — exactly the ϕ(t)\phi(t)ϕ(t) in [P1] Eq.(1).

9. How to read the figures​

(a) Waveform and ISF:

  • Blue: V(θ)=cos⁡θV(\theta)=\cos\thetaV(θ)=cosθ (tank voltage); red: Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ (ISF).
  • Note the 90∘90^\circ90∘ phase difference between them: Γ=0\Gamma=0Γ=0 when VVV is at its peak, ∣Γ∣=1|\Gamma|=1∣Γ∣=1 when VVV crosses zero. Physically this is exactly right — the magnitude of the ISF is set by the waveform slope (large slope = large tangential component = phase-sensitive), and the slope of cos⁡\coscos is −sin⁡-\sin−sin. This is precisely the picture "the most phase-sensitive instant is the zero crossing, the least sensitive is the peak".

(b) Linearity:

  • The purple dots are the persistent Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ measured from the numerical simulation; the black dashed line is the theory Δϕ=−Δq/qmax\Delta\phi=-\Delta q/q_{max}Δϕ=−Δq/qmax​ (because the injection is at Γ=−1\Gamma=-1Γ=−1). They nearly coincide over the entire ±0.05\pm0.05±0.05 range — this is seeing-is-believing for the strict small-signal proportionality of Δϕ\Delta\phiΔϕ to Δq\Delta qΔq, reproducing [P1] Fig. 6.
  • The slope is −1-1−1 (not +1+1+1), confirming the minus sign of Γ(π/2)=−1\Gamma(\pi/2)=-1Γ(π/2)=−1.

(c) State space:

  • The black dashed line is the unperturbed unit circle; the green curve is the trajectory after being "kicked tangentially at a zero crossing". Key observation: the trajectory radius is essentially unchanged (amplitude untouched), but the phase of the rotation is permanently shifted — this is the "pure phase jump". By contrast, injecting at the peak makes the radius first grow then get pulled back while the phase barely moves (readers can move t_inj to a peak and try).

11. Limitations and approximations​

  • Pedagogical toy model, not transistor-level. Γ=−sin⁡θ\Gamma=-\sin\thetaΓ=−sinθ is the result for an ideal lossless LC; a real LC has finite QQQ, a waveform with harmonics, and cyclostationary device noise (gated by the operating point), so the actual Γ\GammaΓ deviates from the clean −sin⁡-\sin−sin and must be extracted with transient/adjoint/Floquet methods (see effective_isf; the related PPV/adjoint material is not among the five downloaded PDFs and is supplemented from standard literature).
  • Small-signal / linearization: projecting onto the tangent assumes Δq≪qmax\Delta q\ll q_{max}Δq≪qmax​. Panel (b) sweeps only up to ±0.05 qmax\pm0.05\,q_{max}±0.05qmax​; larger injections bring in AM–PM conversion, change the ISF itself, or even kick the oscillator off the limit cycle.
  • Single node, single injection: real circuits have many nodes, each with its own Γ\GammaΓ and qmaxq_{max}qmax​; multi-node ISF is discussed in isf_definition.
  • Numerical integration error: simulate_lc uses fixed-step RK4; the weak μ=0.3\mu=0.3μ=0.3 introduces a tiny bias into the phase measurement. This lab averages over the last period to suppress transient residue. extract_isf_by_injection sweeps the full Γ(θ)\Gamma(\theta)Γ(θ) with a smaller Δq/qmax=10−3\Delta q/q_{max}=10^{-3}Δq/qmax​=10−3; the maximum error against −sin⁡θ-\sin\theta−sinθ is about 0.001 (see lab_04).

Key takeaways​

  • The ideal-LC ISF is analytically derivable: Γ(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓ(θ)=−sinθ (from the tangential projection of the voltage step).
  • ∣Γ∣|\Gamma|∣Γ∣ magnitude = waveform slope: most sensitive at zero crossings (Γ=−1\Gamma=-1Γ=−1), insensitive at peaks (Γ=0\Gamma=0Γ=0).
  • In the small-signal regime Δϕ=Γ Δq/qmax\Delta\phi=\Gamma\,\Delta q/q_{max}Δϕ=ΓΔq/qmax​; simulation confirms linearity with slope −1-1−1 ([P1] Fig. 6).
  • Zero-crossing injection = pure phase jump (state radius unchanged, phase permanently shifted).
  • Sources: [P1] Eq.(9),(10),(11), p.182; Fig. 4, 6, 7(a).
