Lab 34 — The N·f0 Selection Rule for Correlated Supply/Substrate Noise

Prerequisites: lab_03 (ring toy and accumulated jitter), lab_05 (numerical extraction of cnc_ncn​), fourier_series_of_isf (cnc_ncn​ as "receive channels") | Next: varactor_tuning_supply_pushing (the quasi-static KpushK_{push}Kpush​ door for supply noise), device_noise_mapping (the full harmonics-equal-channels map)

Every phase-noise computation so far carried an unstated assumption: each noise source injects into ONE node, and the sources on different nodes are independent (uncorrelated). For device thermal / flicker noise that is indeed the case — [P2] p.792 says it explicitly: with uncorrelated sources on the nodes, the total phase noise of NNN sources is NNN times the single-source result ([P2] Eq.(6)) (2N2N2N times for a differential ring) — powers simply add, with no frequency selection whatsoever.

But supply and substrate noise are not like that. [P2] Sec. VI (p.797) points out two key differences from internal device noise:

  1. their PSD is usually nonwhite, often with strong peaks at specific frequencies (switching-regulator harmonics, digital-block switching harmonics);
  2. the same supply line / the same substrate hits every node of the ring with a nearly identical disturbance — the nodes see strongly correlated (nearly identical) noise.

This page turns point 2 into a selection rule you can measure: the effective ISF for correlated noise is the SUM of NNN per-stage ISFs shifted by 2π/N2\pi/N2π/N, and its Fourier components — except those with n≡0 (mod N)n\equiv0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)n≡0 (mod N) — all cancel as phasors. Hence correlated noise converts into phase only from bands near DC and near k⋅N⋅f0k\cdot N\cdot f_0k⋅N⋅f0​.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): think of the NNN-stage ring as NNN antennas pointing in different directions — every stage's ISF has the same shape, offset only by a 2π/N2\pi/N2π/N phase ([P2] Fig. 10 draws them as five phasors ej2πn/5e^{j2\pi n/5}ej2πn/5). When the same disturbance hits all NNN antennas, the mmm-th harmonic channel receives the sum of NNN phasors: only when mmm is an integer multiple of NNN do the five phasors add in phase (gain NNN); otherwise they walk a full circle in the complex plane and their vector sum is exactly zero. This is not an approximation — it is a finite-geometric-series identity, and this lab computes it down to machine precision for you.

Model-level statement: the per-stage ISF in this lab is a pedagogical toy model (dual triangular lobes, deliberately rise/fall-asymmetric), not transistor-level (for a measured ring ISF go to lab_32). The selection rule itself does not depend on the ISF shape — the derivation holds for any 2π2\pi2π-periodic Γ\GammaΓ, which is exactly what makes it powerful. The phase model is linear LTV ([P1] Eq.(11)) with no amplitude dynamics; a real oscillator's amplitude response leaves residual sidebands ([P2] Fig. 11 measured them; see Section 11).

1. Teaching goals​

  • Transcribe verbatim and explain [P2] Eq.(37)–(38) (p.797): with identical noise sources on all NNN nodes, the total phase is the superposition of NNN phase-shifted ISFs.
  • Prove, with a finite geometric sum and no skipped steps, that the summed ISF keeps only the Fourier components with n≡0 (mod N)n\equiv0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)n≡0 (mod N), and that the survivors are amplified by NNN.
  • Numerical verification on an N=5N=5N=5 toy ring (frequency domain): the summed ISF's ∣cn∣\lvert c_n\rvert∣cn​∣ comb keeps only n=0,5,10,15n=0,5,10,15n=0,5,10,15; forbidden components drop to the numeric floor (165.8 dB selection ratio).
  • Time-domain verification (after [P2] Fig. 11's 10 µA experiment): sweep a common sinusoidal disturbance across nf0+Δfn f_0+\Delta fnf0​+Δf; the phase response peaks only at n=0,5,10n=0,5,10n=0,5,10 (66 dB selection, coherent gain 4.998≈N4.998\approx N4.998≈N, matching the [P1] Eq.(15)/(16) theory to 10−510^{-5}10−5 relative error).
  • Translate into design language: the N⋅f0N\cdot f_0N⋅f0​ selection rule — keep supply peaks away from k⋅N⋅f0k\cdot N\cdot f_0k⋅N⋅f0​; rise/fall symmetry closes the DC door; stage mismatch makes the cancellation incomplete.

2.3 Frequency-domain consequence — only bands near DC and k⋅N⋅f0k\cdot N\cdot f_0k⋅N⋅f0​ turn into phase​

Apply the single-tone injection result [P1] Eq.(15)/(16) (p.183) to ΓΣ\Gamma_\SigmaΓΣ​: with i(τ)=I0cos⁡((mω0+Δω)τ)i(\tau)=I_0\cos((m\omega_0+\Delta\omega)\tau)i(τ)=I0​cos((mω0​+Δω)τ),

ϕ(t)≈I0 cΣ,m sin⁡(Δωt)2 qmax Δω\phi(t)\approx\frac{I_0\,c_{\Sigma,m}\,\sin(\Delta\omega t)}{2\,q_{max}\,\Delta\omega}ϕ(t)≈2qmax​ΔωI0​cΣ,m​sin(Δωt)​

(the 2 in the amplitude comes from the product-to-sum identity cos⁡Acos⁡B=12[cos⁡(A−B)+cos⁡(A+B)]\cos A\cos B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)]cosAcosB=21​[cos(A−B)+cos(A+B)] — the slow-term coefficient — and again is not the SSB bookkeeping 2.) Therefore:

  • Correlated noise: cΣ,m=0c_{\Sigma,m}=0cΣ,m​=0 unless m≡0 (mod N)m\equiv0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)m≡0 (mod N) ⇒ only noise near DC (through ΓΣ,dc=Nc0/2\Gamma_{\Sigma,dc}=Nc_0/2ΓΣ,dc​=Nc0​/2, upconverted to close-in) and near k⋅N⋅f0k\cdot N\cdot f_0k⋅N⋅f0​ (downconverted) becomes phase; within each surviving band the integrator behaviour 1/Δω1/\Delta\omega1/Δω still applies ⇒ sidebands fall at −20-20−20 dB/dec of offset ([P2] Fig. 11 measured this slope).
  • Uncorrelated noise (the control): independent per-node sources ⇒ powers add; total phase noise is NNN times a single source ([P2] p.792, NNN times Eq.(6)); every harmonic channel stays open — no selection rule.
  • The difference on the surviving bands: a correlated source gets amplitude gain NNN ⇒ power N2N^2N2; NNN uncorrelated sources get power NNN ⇒ correlated is worse by 10log⁡10N10\log_{10}N10log10​N dB (N=5N=5N=5: 7.0 dB). Coherent addition cuts both ways: it saves you on the forbidden bands (total kill) and punishes you on the surviving ones (N2N^2N2).

2.4 The DC door and rise/fall asymmetry — why low-frequency supply noise is the most dangerous​

Supply-noise power is concentrated at low frequency (regulator ripple, load transients, 1/f1/f1/f) — exactly aligned with the DC channel of ΓΣ\Gamma_\SigmaΓΣ​. How wide that channel opens is set by the single-stage c0c_0c0​, and c0c_0c0​ is set by rise/fall asymmetry ([P2] App. B, p.803): Eq.(53) defines A=frise′/ffall′A=f_{rise}'/f_{fall}'A=frise′​/ffall′​ (rise/fall slope ratio) and Eq.(56) gives

Γdc=2πη2N2⋅1−A1+A\Gamma_{dc}=\frac{2\pi}{\eta^2N^2}\cdot\frac{1-A}{1+A}Γdc​=η2N22π​⋅1+A1−A​

— perfect symmetry (A=1A=1A=1) slams the DC door shut; the more asymmetric, the wider it opens, raising the 1/f31/f^31/f3 corner through Eq.(57). This lab's toy ISF is deliberately asymmetric (positive-lobe peak 1.0, negative-lobe peak 0.6), so that c0≠0c_0\neq0c0​=0 and the DC channel is open — letting you see both stories, "the DC door" and "the Nf0N f_0Nf0​ comb", in one figure.

Design language (the N⋅f0N\cdot f_0N⋅f0​ selection rule): to correlated supply/substrate noise, the ring is a comb receiver — it only listens at DC and at k⋅N⋅f0k\cdot N\cdot f_0k⋅N⋅f0​. Hence: (i) if the supply has a known switching spur at fswf_{sw}fsw​ and harmonics, choose NNN and f0f_0f0​ so that k⋅N⋅f0k\cdot N\cdot f_0k⋅N⋅f0​ avoids the spurs (or choose fswf_{sw}fsw​ the other way around); (ii) larger NNN pushes the first high-frequency sensitive band Nf0N f_0Nf0​ higher, where package/decoupling attenuation is usually better; (iii) rise/fall symmetry (A→1A\to1A→1) narrows the DC door — the same knob as the device-flicker c0c_0c0​ counter-measure (symmetry); (iv) stage mismatch makes the phasor cancellation incomplete, leaking residue into the forbidden bands (Section 11).

4. Python core code​

Excerpts from simulations/lab_34_correlated_supply.py (checked against the source). Toy per-stage ISF, summed ISF, and the time-domain injection measurement:

def gamma_stage(theta):
    """Per-stage toy ring ISF: +1.0 triangular lobe at the rising edge (θ=0),
    −0.6 triangular lobe at the falling edge (θ=π).
    Deliberately asymmetric (1.0 vs 0.6) → c0 ≠ 0 ([P2] App.B: A≠1 ⇒ Γdc≠0)."""
    th = wrap_phase(theta)
    d_rise = np.minimum(th, 2 * np.pi - th)
    d_fall = np.abs(th - np.pi)
    return H_RISE * _tri(d_rise, W_LOBE) - H_FALL * _tri(d_fall, W_LOBE)

def gamma_summed(theta):
    """Sum of N per-stage ISFs shifted by 2π/N (the bracket of [P2] Eq.(37))."""
    acc = np.zeros_like(np.asarray(theta, dtype=float))
    for n in range(N_STAGES):
        acc += gamma_stage(theta + 2 * np.pi * n / N_STAGES)
    return acc

def response(n_h, g_t):
    """Common injection i(t)=I0·cos(2π(n_h·f0+Δf)t); return the amplitude of φ at Δf [rad]."""
    i_inj = I0 * np.cos(2 * np.pi * (n_h * F0 + DF) * t)
    phi = np.cumsum(i_inj * g_t) * dt / QMAX      # discrete version of [P1] Eq.(11)
    return 2.0 * np.abs(np.mean(phi * proj))      # project onto the Δf bin (integer slow periods)

The frequency-domain part uses compute_fourier_coefficients (the same ruler as lab_05) to compute c0…c15c_0\dots c_{15}c0​…c15​ of both the single-stage and the summed ISF; the phasor identity is evaluated directly as ∣Sm∣=∣∑nej2πmn/5∣\lvert S_m\rvert=\lvert\sum_n e^{j2\pi mn/5}\rvert∣Sm​∣=∣∑n​ej2πmn/5∣.

11. Limitations and approximations​

  • Pure-phase linear LTV: this lab has only the phase integral of [P1] Eq.(11), no amplitude dynamics. [P2] Fig. 11's measurement shows residual sidebands from the amplitude response at non-multiples of NNN — that path (AM, and AM-PM back into phase) does not exist in this model; our forbidden-band residue (−66-66−66 dB) is purely numerical (sampling leakage). Do not use it to predict the forbidden-band depth of a real circuit.
  • Identical stages: exact cancellation relies on every stage's ISF having the same shape at exactly 2π/N2\pi/N2π/N spacing. Real mismatch (load, drive strength, layout) leaves a phasor residue ∝\propto∝ mismatch — to first order, a relative mismatch ε\varepsilonε leaks an amplitude ∼ε\sim\varepsilon∼ε into the forbidden channels (20log⁡10ε20\log_{10}\varepsilon20log10​ε dB). 1% mismatch ⇒ the forbidden bands are only suppressed by 40 dB, not infinitely deep.
  • "Fully correlated, equal-strength, same-sign" injection: real supply/substrate coupling coefficients differ from node to node (IR drop, layout distance), landing between "fully correlated" and "uncorrelated"; the two extremes on this page bracket the answer.
  • Toy ISF: the dual triangular lobes are hand-placed (lab_03-style, plus asymmetry); for the real single-ended ring ISF shape see lab_32. But the selection rule does not depend on shape — the proof of 2.2 holds for any 2π2\pi2π-periodic Γ\GammaΓ; changing the ISF changes the cnc_ncn​ values of each channel, not which channels exist.
  • Supply noise is modeled here as node-current injection: low-frequency supply noise also has a quasi-static FM path (KpushK_{push}Kpush​, shifting f0f_0f0​ through the operating point) — the subject of varactor_tuning_supply_pushing; both paths coexist, the KpushK_{push}Kpush​ door usually dominating at low offsets while the kNf0k N f_0kNf0​ comb is the dedicated entrance for high-frequency supply spurs.
  • Cyclostationary weighting not included: strictly each stage's injection should also be multiplied by the NMF α(ω0t)\alpha(\omega_0t)α(ω0​t) (effective_isf); for identical stages the α\alphaα's are the same phase-shifted family, so the selection rule holds unchanged for Γeff=Γα\Gamma_{eff}=\Gamma\alphaΓeff​=Γα (the product is still 2π2\pi2π-periodic).

Key takeaways​

  • The essential difference between supply/substrate noise and device noise: nonwhite PSD (with spurs) + strong cross-node correlation ([P2] Sec. VI, p.797).
  • The effective ISF of a correlated source is the SUM of NNN per-stage ISFs shifted by 2π/N2\pi/N2π/N ([P2] Eq.(37)); the finite geometric sum gives Sm=NS_m=NSm​=N (m≡0 mod Nm\equiv0\ \mathrm{mod}\ Nm≡0 mod N) or 000 (otherwise) ⇒ only the n≡0 (mod N)n\equiv0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)n≡0 (mod N) channels remain, with gain NNN ([P2] Eq.(38)).
  • Numerical verification (N=5N=5N=5 toy): ∣Sm∣\lvert S_m\rvert∣Sm​∣ to machine precision; cΣ,n/cn=5.000000c_{\Sigma,n}/c_n=5.000000cΣ,n​/cn​=5.000000; 165.8 dB frequency-domain selection; the time-domain sweep peaks only at n=0,5,10n=0,5,10n=0,5,10 (66.0 dB, coherent gain 4.9978, theory match 10−510^{-5}10−5).
  • The N⋅f0N\cdot f_0N⋅f0​ selection rule: correlated noise enters only from DC (door size =Nc0/2=Nc_0/2=Nc0​/2, set by the rise/fall asymmetry AAA, [P2] Eq.(56)) and from near k⋅N⋅f0k\cdot N\cdot f_0k⋅N⋅f0​. In design: keep kNf0kNf_0kNf0​ away from supply spurs, symmetrize to close the DC door, and watch mismatch, which makes the cancellation incomplete.
  • On the surviving bands, correlated is worse than uncorrelated by 10log⁡10N10\log_{10}N10log10​N dB (coherent N2N^2N2 vs power NNN); on the forbidden bands, correlated is better all the way down to the mismatch/amplitude residue. Get the correlation wrong and both numbers are wrong.
