ADC aperture jitter: how clock jitter eats SNR and ENOB

β: This English translation is in beta — the Traditional-Chinese original is the authoritative version.

Prerequisites: psd_phase_noise_jitter (the L→Sϕ→σϕ→σt\mathcal{L}\to S_\phi\to\sigma_\phi\to\sigma_tL→Sϕ​→σϕ​→σt​ four-step chain), lab_08_jitter_integration (how the 447.9 fs is integrated) | Next: serdes_clocking_connection, exercises

The problem: you swapped in a 12-bit ADC — why didn't the high-frequency SNR improve at all?​

A high-speed ADC (analog-to-digital converter) "freezes" the input voltage at the sampling instant. But the sampling instant itself is not ideal: the sampling clock has jitter, and the trigger instant of the sampling switch carries its own random uncertainty — the two combined are called aperture jitter (the rms random error of the sampling instant, σt\sigma_tσt​, in s). As the input frequency rises, the waveform slope steepens, and the same timing error is amplified by that slope into an ever larger voltage error — beyond some input frequency the SNR is completely pinned by clock quality, regardless of how many bits of ADC you bought.

This page does four things:

  1. Derive the signature formula SNRjitter=−20log⁡10(2πfinσt)\text{SNR}_{jitter}=-20\log_{10}(2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t)SNRjitter​=−20log10​(2πfin​σt​) step by step from first principles;
  2. Derive the ENOB (effective number of bits) conversion ENOB=(SNR−1.76)/6.02\text{ENOB}=(\text{SNR}-1.76)/6.02ENOB=(SNR−1.76)/6.02;
  3. Use this site's canonical Example C value σt=447.9\sigma_t=447.9σt​=447.9 fs to compute an honest design table (1 / 2.5 / 5 / 10 GHz), and back-solve "how clean a clock does 10 ENOB @ 5 GHz require";
  4. Connect it back to this site's main thread: L(Δf)→\mathcal{L}(\Delta f)\toL(Δf)→ integrate →σt→\to\sigma_t\to→σt​→ SNR — the oscillator's phase noise plot directly sets the data converter's effective number of bits.

External-literature note: the aperture-jitter SNR formula is a standard data-converter textbook result (external literature, not among the five source PDFs); e.g. W. Kester, "MT-007: Aperture Time, Aperture Jitter, Aperture Delay Time—Removing the Confusion," Analog Devices Tutorial MT-007, 2008; R. H. Walden, "Analog-to-digital converter survey and analysis," IEEE J. Sel. Areas Commun., vol. 17, no. 4, pp. 539–550, Apr. 1999. The input number σt=447.9\sigma_t=447.9σt​=447.9 fs on this page, however, is this site's own verified canonical Example C (lab_08).

Physical intuition (conclusion first): sampling error = slope × timing error. The steepest slope of a sine is A⋅2πfinA\cdot2\pi f_{in}A⋅2πfin​ (V/s), so the same clock (the same σt\sigma_tσt​) hitting an input at twice the frequency gives twice the voltage error and 4× the noise power — SNR drops 6 dB per octave, i.e. 1 bit per octave. This is why in direct RF-sampling systems the most expensive part is often not the ADC itself, but the low-noise clock that feeds it.

Step 1: the voltage error of jittered sampling (error = slope × timing error)​

Write down the signal and the sampling instants. Let the ADC sample a full-scale sine:

V(t)=Asin⁡(2πfint),V(t)=A\sin(2\pi f_{in}t),V(t)=Asin(2πfin​t),

where AAA is the amplitude (V) and finf_{in}fin​ the input frequency (Hz). The ideal sampling instants are tn=nTst_n=nT_stn​=nTs​ (Ts=1/fsT_s=1/f_sTs​=1/fs​, s); the actual sampling instants carry an additional random error δtn\delta t_nδtn​ (s):

V^n=V(nTs+δtn)=Asin⁡(2πfin(nTs+δtn)).\hat V_n=V(nT_s+\delta t_n)=A\sin\big(2\pi f_{in}(nT_s+\delta t_n)\big).V^n​=V(nTs​+δtn​)=Asin(2πfin​(nTs​+δtn​)).

Extract the error with a first-order Taylor expansion. Because δtn\delta t_nδtn​ is tiny (fs scale), expand VVV around tnt_ntn​, keeping only the first order:

V^n≈V(nTs)+dVdt∣t=nTs ⁣⋅δtn⟹en≡V^n−V(nTs)=A 2πfincos⁡(2πfinnTs) δtn.\hat V_n\approx V(nT_s)+\left.\frac{dV}{dt}\right|_{t=nT_s}\!\cdot\delta t_n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e_n\equiv\hat V_n-V(nT_s)=A\,2\pi f_{in}\cos(2\pi f_{in}nT_s)\,\delta t_n.V^n​≈V(nTs​)+dtdV​​t=nTs​​⋅δtn​⟹en​≡V^n​−V(nTs​)=A2πfin​cos(2πfin​nTs​)δtn​.
  • Math used: ddtAsin⁡(2πfint)=A⋅2πfincos⁡(2πfint)\frac{d}{dt}A\sin(2\pi f_{in}t)=A\cdot2\pi f_{in}\cos(2\pi f_{in}t)dtd​Asin(2πfin​t)=A⋅2πfin​cos(2πfin​t).
  • Unit check (term by term): AAA (V) × 2πfin\times\,2\pi f_{in}×2πfin​ (rad/s) × δtn\times\,\delta t_n×δtn​ (s) === V·rad; rad is dimensionless, so ene_nen​ is in V ✓. The slope A⋅2πfinA\cdot2\pi f_{in}A⋅2πfin​ has units of V/s — "how many volts of change per second"; multiplied by "how many seconds off", you naturally get volts.
  • Validity condition of the first-order approximation: the second-order Taylor term relative to the first is O(2πfinδtn)O(2\pi f_{in}\delta t_n)O(2πfin​δtn​), so we need 2πfinσt≪12\pi f_{in}\sigma_t\ll12πfin​σt​≪1 rad. The worst case on this page (10 GHz, 447.9 fs) is 2π×1010×4.479×10−13=2.81×10−22\pi\times10^{10}\times4.479\times10^{-13}=2.81\times10^{-2}2π×1010×4.479×10−13=2.81×10−2 rad ✓, far below 1.
  • Physical meaning: this step is the core of the whole page — a timing error is amplified by the waveform slope into a voltage error. The slope is proportional to finf_{in}fin​, so the damage is proportional to finf_{in}fin​. This is the same physical quantity, seen from the other side, as waveform_slope's point that inside an oscillator "the steeper the slope, the less sensitive to noise": inside the oscillator, a steep slope is good (voltage noise converted to phase noise gets divided by the slope); at the sampling interface, a steep slope is bad (the timing error gets multiplied by it).

Step 2: mean-square over jitter and sampling phase → noise power​

Now turn the single-sample error ene_nen​ into a statistical noise power. Assume (Section 5 below audits each item):

  • δtn\delta t_nδtn​ is zero-mean Gaussian, independent between samples (white RJ), with variance E[δtn2]=σt2\mathrm{E}[\delta t_n^2]=\sigma_t^2E[δtn2​]=σt2​ (s²);
  • δtn\delta t_nδtn​ is independent of the signal phase (the jitter does not know where the waveform currently is).

Take the expectation of en2e_n^2en2​; because δtn\delta t_nδtn​ and cos⁡(⋅)\cos(\cdot)cos(⋅) are independent, the expectation factorizes:

E[en2]=A2(2πfin)2 E[cos⁡2(2πfinnTs)]⏟= 1/2  E[δtn2]⏟= σt2.\mathrm{E}[e_n^2]=A^2(2\pi f_{in})^2\,\underbrace{\mathrm{E}\big[\cos^2(2\pi f_{in}nT_s)\big]}_{=\ 1/2}\;\underbrace{\mathrm{E}[\delta t_n^2]}_{=\ \sigma_t^2}.E[en2​]=A2(2πfin​)2= 1/2E[cos2(2πfin​nTs​)]​​= σt2​E[δtn2​]​​.

Where that 12\tfrac1221​ comes from: the sampling phase 2πfinnTs2\pi f_{in}nT_s2πfin​nTs​ sweeps uniformly over a full period (coherent sampling over an integer number of periods, or treating the phase as uniformly distributed), and the average of cos⁡2\cos^2cos2 is 12\tfrac1221​ — the same fact as "the power of a sine is half the amplitude squared". So the noise power (V²) is:

Pe=A2(2πfin)2σt22[V2].P_e=\frac{A^2(2\pi f_{in})^2\sigma_t^2}{2}\quad[\mathrm{V}^2].Pe​=2A2(2πfin​)2σt2​​[V2].
  • Dimension check: V2×(rad/s)2×s2=V2\mathrm{V}^2\times(\mathrm{rad/s})^2\times\mathrm{s}^2=\mathrm{V}^2V2×(rad/s)2×s2=V2 (rad dimensionless) ✓.
  • Physical meaning: the error is largest at the zero crossings (steepest slope, cos⁡2=1\cos^2=1cos2=1), and at the peaks (zero slope, cos⁡2=0\cos^2=0cos2=0) jitter is completely harmless — this is exactly the LTV sensitivity idea of [P1] replayed at the sampling interface: "when you get hit" determines how much it hurts (compare lab_02's LC ISF: an oscillator is most sensitive to charge perturbations at the zero crossings — the same cos⁡\coscos/sin⁡\sinsin geometry). The 12\tfrac1221​ is just the average of "sometimes hit on the steep part, sometimes on the flat part".

Step 3: SNR — the signature formula​

The power of the sine signal itself is

Psig=A22[V2].P_{sig}=\frac{A^2}{2}\quad[\mathrm{V}^2].Psig​=2A2​[V2].

SNR (signal-to-noise ratio) is the ratio of the two. Note that A2A^2A2 and the two 12\tfrac1221​'s all cancel:

SNR=PsigPe=A2/2A2(2πfin)2σt2/2=1(2πfinσt)2.\text{SNR}=\frac{P_{sig}}{P_e}=\frac{A^2/2}{A^2(2\pi f_{in})^2\sigma_t^2/2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t)^2}.SNR=Pe​Psig​​=A2(2πfin​)2σt2​/2A2/2​=(2πfin​σt​)21​.

In dB (a 10log⁡1010\log_{10}10log10​ power ratio; the square comes out as 20):

 SNRjitter=−20log⁡10 ⁣(2πfin σt)  [dB] \boxed{\ \text{SNR}_{jitter}=-20\log_{10}\!\big(2\pi f_{in}\,\sigma_t\big)\ \ [\mathrm{dB}]\ } SNRjitter​=−20log10​(2πfin​σt​)  [dB] ​
  • Dimension check: 2πfinσt=2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t=2πfin​σt​= (rad/s) × (s) === rad, dimensionless ✓ — the argument of a log must be dimensionless, automatically satisfied here. SNR itself is a power ratio, dimensionless ✓.
  • Physical reading (the single most important sentence): 2πfinσt2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t2πfin​σt​ is the "timing jitter converted to an equivalent rms phase error on the input sine", σϕ,in\sigma_{\phi,in}σϕ,in​ (rad). So this formula is really saying: SNR=1/σϕ,in2\text{SNR}=1/\sigma_{\phi,in}^2SNR=1/σϕ,in2​ — when sampling a sine, the SNR is the inverse square of the equivalent phase jitter.
  • The elegant connection to this site's main thread: the clock's own rms phase at f0f_0f0​ is σϕ=2πf0σt\sigma_\phi=2\pi f_0\sigma_tσϕ​=2πf0​σt​; applied to an input at frequency finf_{in}fin​, the equivalent phase scales as σϕ,in=2πfinσt=(fin/f0) σϕ\sigma_{\phi,in}=2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t=(f_{in}/f_0)\,\sigma_\phiσϕ,in​=2πfin​σt​=(fin​/f0​)σϕ​. When fin=f0=5f_{in}=f_0=5fin​=f0​=5 GHz, the SNR is directly −20log⁡10(σϕ)=−20log⁡10(0.01407)=37.0-20\log_{10}(\sigma_\phi)=-20\log_{10}(0.01407)=37.0−20log10​(σϕ​)=−20log10​(0.01407)=37.0 dB — Example C's 14.07 mrad becomes the ADC's SNR unchanged.
  • Validity/failure: holds only when 2πfinσt≪12\pi f_{in}\sigma_t\ll12πfin​σt​≪1 rad (the first-order Taylor of Step 1) and jitter is the only noise term; full conditions in Section 5.

Step 4: ENOB — converting to an "effective number of bits"​

To phrase SNR in the language of ADC designers, first derive the SNR of an ideal N-bit quantizer (an external standard result: W. R. Bennett, "Spectra of quantized signals," Bell Syst. Tech. J., vol. 27, pp. 446–472, Jul. 1948 (external literature, not among the five source PDFs); derived self-contained here).

(a) Quantization noise power q2/12q^2/12q2/12. Let the LSB (least significant bit, the smallest quantization step) be qqq (V). The quantization error eee is approximately uniformly distributed over [−q/2,+q/2][-q/2,+q/2][−q/2,+q/2], with power (variance):

E[e2]=∫−q/2q/2e2 deq=1q⋅e33∣−q/2q/2=1q⋅2(q/2)33=q212[V2].\mathrm{E}[e^2]=\int_{-q/2}^{q/2}e^2\,\frac{de}{q}=\frac{1}{q}\cdot\frac{e^3}{3}\Big|_{-q/2}^{q/2}=\frac{1}{q}\cdot\frac{2(q/2)^3}{3}=\frac{q^2}{12}\quad[\mathrm{V}^2].E[e2]=∫−q/2q/2​e2qde​=q1​⋅3e3​​−q/2q/2​=q1​⋅32(q/2)3​=12q2​[V2].

(b) Full-scale sine power. The full-scale range of N bits is 2Nq2^N q2Nq (V), so the sine amplitude is A=2Nq/2=2N−1qA=2^N q/2=2^{N-1}qA=2Nq/2=2N−1q, with power:

Psig=A22=22N−2q22=22Nq28[V2].P_{sig}=\frac{A^2}{2}=\frac{2^{2N-2}q^2}{2}=\frac{2^{2N}q^2}{8}\quad[\mathrm{V}^2].Psig​=2A2​=222N−2q2​=822Nq2​[V2].

(c) Divide and take dB.

SNRq=22Nq2/8q2/12=128 22N=32⋅22N\text{SNR}_q=\frac{2^{2N}q^2/8}{q^2/12}=\frac{12}{8}\,2^{2N}=\frac{3}{2}\cdot2^{2N}SNRq​=q2/1222Nq2/8​=812​22N=23​⋅22N SNRq[dB]=10log⁡10 ⁣32+2N⋅10log⁡102=1.76+6.02 N.\text{SNR}_q[\mathrm{dB}]=10\log_{10}\!\tfrac32+2N\cdot10\log_{10}2=1.76+6.02\,N.SNRq​[dB]=10log10​23​+2N⋅10log10​2=1.76+6.02N.
  • Dimension check: q2q^2q2 cancels top and bottom, SNR dimensionless ✓; NNN is a bit count (dimensionless).

(d) Invert to define ENOB. Plug any SNR (here the jitter-limited one) back into this formula and solve for NNN: that is "how many bits of ideal quantizer this ADC is equivalent to":

 ENOB=SNR[dB]−1.766.02  [bit] \boxed{\ \text{ENOB}=\frac{\text{SNR}[\mathrm{dB}]-1.76}{6.02}\ \ [\mathrm{bit}]\ } ENOB=6.02SNR[dB]−1.76​  [bit] ​
  • Validity/failure: q2/12q^2/12q2/12 assumes the quantization error is uniform and signal-independent (true only for a busy signal; for very small or DC inputs it becomes a deterministic error and requires dither); ENOB is defined against a full-scale sine — back off the input by some dB and the SNR drops by the same dB.
  • Composition rule: in a real ADC, jitter, quantization, and thermal noise add in power: SNRtot=−10log⁡10(10−SNRj/10+10−SNRq/10+10−SNRth/10)\text{SNR}_{tot}=-10\log_{10}\big(10^{-\text{SNR}_j/10}+10^{-\text{SNR}_q/10}+10^{-\text{SNR}_{th}/10}\big)SNRtot​=−10log10​(10−SNRj​/10+10−SNRq​/10+10−SNRth​/10). At low frequency quantization/thermal noise dominates and SNR is flat versus finf_{in}fin​; at high frequency the jitter term comes down at −20-20−20 dB/dec and takes over — the crossover is the boundary of "how many ADC bits this clock deserves".

Step 5: factor-of-2 bookkeeping check (against this site's conventions)​

This site asks of every 2: "where does it come from, and which convention is it?" This formula has three places to check:

  1. The formula itself hides no 2. The 12\tfrac1221​ in the numerator Psig=A2/2P_{sig}=A^2/2Psig​=A2/2 (sine power) and the ⟨cos⁡2⟩=12\langle\cos^2\rangle=\tfrac12⟨cos2⟩=21​ in the denominator (sampling-phase average) cancel each other; the 2π2\pi2π is a genuine angular-frequency conversion (Hz→rad/s), not a bookkeeping convention. So SNR=1/(2πfinσt)2\text{SNR}=1/(2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t)^2SNR=1/(2πfin​σt​)2 does not depend on SSB/DSB or any convention of L\mathcal{L}L.
  2. The convention hides upstream of σt\sigma_tσt​. This site's σt=447.9\sigma_t=447.9σt​=447.9 fs is integrated from the measured L(1 MHz)=−100\mathcal{L}(1\,\mathrm{MHz})=-100L(1MHz)=−100 dBc/Hz using Sϕ=2⋅10L/10S_\phi=2\cdot10^{\mathcal{L}/10}Sϕ​=2⋅10L/10 (the small-angle SSB convention L≈12Sϕ\mathcal{L}\approx\tfrac12S_\phiL≈21​Sϕ​) (psd_phase_noise_jitter, Step 2).   3. If L\mathcal{L}L is "predicted" from circuit noise, /2-vs-/4 enters. This site's Example B (f0=5f_0=5f0​=5 GHz, Γrms=0.5\Gamma_{rms}=0.5Γrms​=0.5, qmax=1q_{max}=1qmax​=1 pC, Si=10−24S_i=10^{-24}Si​=10−24 A²/Hz) with [P1] Eq. Numerical cross-check: a 3 dB difference in L\mathcal{L}L → σt∝10L/20\sigma_t\propto10^{\mathcal{L}/20}σt​∝10L/20 differs by 103/20=1.41≈210^{3/20}=1.41\approx\sqrt2103/20=1.41≈2​ → SNRjitter\text{SNR}_{jitter}SNRjitter​ differs by 20log⁡102=3.020\log_{10}\sqrt2=3.020log10​2​=3.0 dB. Inverse design: for 10 ENOB @ 5 GHz, how clean must the clock be?​

Problem: the system must retain 10 effective bits on an fin=5f_{in}=5fin​=5 GHz input (jitter as the only contributor); find the aperture-jitter ceiling σt\sigma_tσt​.

Step 1 (ENOB→SNR):

The full chain: from the phase-noise plot to ENOB​

The first four boxes are exactly the four-step chain of psd_phase_noise_jitter (Example C); this page merely appends the last two. The whole chain in a few lines of Python (reusing lab_08's library):

import numpy as np
from simulations.common.noise_utils import leeson_one_over_f2, integrate_rms_jitter

f = np.logspace(6, 8, 4000)                             # 1 MHz -> 100 MHz
L = leeson_one_over_f2(f, L_ref_dbc=-100, f_ref=1e6)    # 1/f^2 skirt (Example C)
sigma_t, _ = integrate_rms_jitter(f, L, f0=5e9, fmin=1e6, fmax=100e6)
print(sigma_t*1e15)                                     # -> 447.9
print(-20*np.log10(2*np.pi*5e9*sigma_t))                # -> 37.03

An honest warning about integration bandwidth (continuing the psd page's "the lower limit dominates"): Example C's 447.9 fs is the "integrated 1→100 MHz" number. For an ADC, "which offset frequencies count as jitter" depends on observation length and system architecture: an FFT record lasts TrecT_{rec}Trec​, and phase drift slower than 1/Trec1/T_{rec}1/Trec​ looks like a frequency offset rather than a noise floor; if the clock is cleaned by a PLL, the close-in part is taken over by the reference. Change the integration bandwidth and σt\sigma_tσt​ changes, and the SNR with it — when reporting a jitter-limited SNR, the integration bandwidth of σt\sigma_tσt​ must be reported with it.

Design knobs: what you can turn in a jitter-limited system​

  1. The clock source itself: L∝Γrms2Si/qmax2\mathcal{L}\propto\Gamma_{rms}^2S_i/q_{max}^2L∝Γrms2​Si​/qmax2​ ([P1] Eq. (21), p.185) — raise qmaxq_{max}qmax​ (swing, tank capacitance), pick symmetric waveforms to suppress Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​, replace the ring with an LC (representative value Γrms=0.5\Gamma_{rms}=0.5Γrms​=0.5; true LC 1/21/\sqrt21/2​; rings worse, see lc_vs_ring).
  2. PLL loop bandwidth: inside the loop, close-in noise is taken over by the reference; outside the loop it belongs to the VCO — the optimum bandwidth minimizes the integrated jitter (pll_noise_budget).
  3. Every stage of the clock chain: buffers, dividers, distribution all add jitter into the RSS; the ADC datasheet's internal aperture jitter is one more term. The budget must be apportioned from σt,tot2=∑σt,i2\sigma_{t,tot}^2=\sum\sigma_{t,i}^2σt,tot2​=∑σt,i2​.
  4. Lower the finf_{in}fin​ seen at the sampling interface: mixing down before sampling (IF sampling) trades mixer complexity for clock spec — every octave down buys 1 bit.
  5. Oversampling + digital filtering: white jitter noise spreads flat across the Nyquist band; filtering out the out-of-band part buys 10log⁡10(OSR)10\log_{10}(\mathrm{OSR})10log10​(OSR) of processing gain. But colored (close-in skirt) jitter hugs the signal and cannot be filtered — one more example of "the white/colored assumption decides the answer".
  6. Waveform slope: sharpening the clock edges (limiting, buffering) suppresses the clock chain's own additive-noise-to-jitter conversion (waveform_slope), but is powerless against phase noise that is "already σt\sigma_tσt​" — the slope knob works only on additive voltage noise.

The SerDes connection: one σt\sigma_tσt​, two consumers​

serdes_clocking_connection takes the same 447.9 fs and computes eye closure and BER (Q−1(10−12)=7.03Q^{-1}(10^{-12})=7.03Q−1(10−12)=7.03, consuming ±7.03σt=±3.1\pm7.03\sigma_t=\pm3.1±7.03σt​=±3.1 ps of eye width); this page uses it for SNR/ENOB. Both are "the time-domain bill for the oscillator's phase noise":

  • SerDes cares about the tail probability (7σ7\sigma7σ events causing bit errors);
  • the ADC cares about the mean-square power (σ2\sigma^2σ2 goes straight into the SNR).

The same clock, the same L(Δf)\mathcal{L}(\Delta f)L(Δf) curve, starting from [P1]'s Γrms/qmax\Gamma_{rms}/q_{max}Γrms​/qmax​, ends up setting the spec ceiling at both ends of a communication system (the sampler and the transceiver).

Key takeaways​

  • Signature formula: SNRjitter=−20log⁡10(2πfinσt)\text{SNR}_{jitter}=-20\log_{10}(2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t)SNRjitter​=−20log10​(2πfin​σt​); the derivation's core is "error = slope × timing error"; the two 12\tfrac1221​'s (sine power, cos⁡2\cos^2cos2 average) cancel, and the formula contains no SSB bookkeeping convention whatsoever.
  • 2πfinσt=σϕ,in2\pi f_{in}\sigma_t=\sigma_{\phi,in}2πfin​σt​=σϕ,in​: the SNR is just −20log⁡10-20\log_{10}−20log10​ of the equivalent input phase jitter; at fin=f0f_{in}=f_0fin​=f0​ it directly recycles Example C's σϕ=14.07\sigma_\phi=14.07σϕ​=14.07 mrad → 37.0 dB.
  • ENOB =(SNR−1.76)/6.02=(\text{SNR}-1.76)/6.02=(SNR−1.76)/6.02: obtained by deriving SNRq=6.02N+1.76\text{SNR}_q=6.02N+1.76SNRq​=6.02N+1.76 from q2/12q^2/12q2/12 and the full-scale sine power, then inverting.
  • Design table (σt=447.9\sigma_t=447.9σt​=447.9 fs): 1 / 2.5 / 5 / 10 GHz → 51.0 / 43.1 / 37.0 / 31.0 dB → 8.18 / 6.86 / 5.86 / 4.86 bit; 6.02 dB = 1 bit lost per octave.
  • Inverse design: 10 ENOB @ 5 GHz → SNR ≥61.96\ge61.96≥61.96 dB → σt≤25.4\sigma_t\le25.4σt​≤25.4 fs (17.6× cleaner than 447.9 fs = the whole skirt down −24.9-24.9−24.9 dB → L(1 MHz)≤−124.9\mathcal{L}(1\,\mathrm{MHz})\le-124.9L(1MHz)≤−124.9 dBc/Hz).
  • Convention discipline: upstream of σt\sigma_tσt​, [P1] Eq. (21)'s /4 (SSB) vs the time-domain /2 bookkeeping makes L\mathcal{L}L differ by 3 dB → σt\sigma_tσt​ by 2\sqrt22​ → SNR by 3 dB; only a chain-consistent choice is meaningful; a jitter-limited SNR must always be reported with the integration bandwidth of σt\sigma_tσt​.
