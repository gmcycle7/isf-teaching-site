Clock-chain noise accounting: ×N, ÷N, PLL, buffer — a one-page lookup table

Prerequisites: psd_phase_noise_jitter (SϕS_\phiSϕ​, L\mathcal{L}L, phase↔time conversion), pll_noise_budget (∣Hlp∣2,∣Hhp∣2\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2,\lvert H_{hp}\rvert^2∣Hlp​∣2,∣Hhp​∣2 and the five-source budget — this page reuses them directly, no re-derivation), white_noise_to_phase_noise (where the VCO's −148-148−148 dBc/Hz comes from) | Next: serdes_clocking_connection, exercises

In a real system there is no such thing as "one oscillator, used directly": the reference crystal is multiplied up by a PLL, divided back down by dividers, and passes through several buffer stages before reaching the sampler. The system engineer's daily question is: given the source L(f)\mathcal{L}(f)L(f), what is L(f)\mathcal{L}(f)L(f) at every node of the clock tree? What is the integrated jitter of the final clock? The good news: bookkeeping for the entire chain needs only four rules. This page derives each of the four rules step by step (no skipped steps, with units, with failure conditions), then strings them together in one complete worked chain, computed all the way to the end.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): only two kinds of things ever happen to phase along a clock chain — (1) Deterministic phase scaling: ×N multiplies phase by NNN (+20log⁡10N+20\log_{10}N+20log10​N dB), ÷N divides phase by NNN (−20log⁡10N-20\log_{10}N−20log10​N dB), a PLL applies ×N to the reference in-band and low-passes it, and high-passes the VCO. Scaling acts on the entire curve; the offset axis does not move. (2) Additive independent noise: the buffer's and divider's own noise floor, uncorrelated with the input phase, adds in power (never in dB). There is also one beautiful conserved quantity: under ideal ×N/÷N, the time jitter σt\sigma_tσt​ in seconds is exactly unchanged — what changes is only "what angular fraction of one period the same error in seconds occupies".

Rule 1: ideal ×N multiplication — why it is +20log⁡10N+20\log_{10}N+20log10​N​

Step 1 (write the signal as a function of phase). Using the decomposition of [P1] Eq.(1), p.181, take a sinusoidal waveform:

Vin(t)=cos⁡(Φin(t)),Φin(t)=ωref t+ϕin(t)V_{in}(t)=\cos\big(\Phi_{in}(t)\big),\qquad \Phi_{in}(t)=\omega_{ref}\,t+\phi_{in}(t)Vin​(t)=cos(Φin​(t)),Φin​(t)=ωref​t+ϕin​(t)

Φin\Phi_{in}Φin​ is the total phase (rad), ϕin\phi_{in}ϕin​ is the excess phase (rad), ωref=2πfref\omega_{ref}=2\pi f_{ref}ωref​=2πfref​ (rad/s).

Step 2 (ideal multiplier = memoryless nonlinearity + bandpass). Any memoryless nonlinearity g(⋅)g(\cdot)g(⋅) acting on cos⁡Φ\cos\PhicosΦ: because g(cos⁡Φ)g(\cos\Phi)g(cosΦ) is a 2π2\pi2π-periodic function of Φ\PhiΦ, it can be expanded as a Fourier series in Φ\PhiΦ:

g(cos⁡Φ(t))=∑k=0∞akcos⁡(k Φ(t)+θk)g\big(\cos\Phi(t)\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k\cos\big(k\,\Phi(t)+\theta_k\big)g(cosΦ(t))=k=0∑∞​ak​cos(kΦ(t)+θk​)

The key is the argument: every term is "an integer multiple of the instantaneous total phase, kΦ(t)k\Phi(t)kΦ(t)" — a memoryless element has no notion of time, it can only act on "the phase right now", so the excess phase is carried along completely intact.

Step 3 (bandpass selects the NNN-th harmonic). A bandpass filter centered at NfrefN f_{ref}Nfref​ picks the k=Nk=Nk=N term:

Vout(t)∝cos⁡(NΦin(t))=cos⁡(Nωref t+Nϕin(t))⟹ ϕout(t)=N ϕin(t) V_{out}(t)\propto\cos\big(N\Phi_{in}(t)\big)=\cos\big(N\omega_{ref}\,t+N\phi_{in}(t)\big) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\ \phi_{out}(t)=N\,\phi_{in}(t)\ }Vout​(t)∝cos(NΦin​(t))=cos(Nωref​t+Nϕin​(t))⟹ ϕout​(t)=Nϕin​(t) ​

This identity holds instant by instant — every frequency component of ϕin\phi_{in}ϕin​ is multiplied by NNN, with no frequency selectivity whatsoever.

Step 4 (convert to PSD and dB). Phase multiplied by NNN (amplitude), power spectral density multiplied by N2N^2N2:

Sϕ,out(f)=N2 Sϕ,in(f)  [rad2/Hz],Lout(f)=Lin(f)+20log⁡10N  [dBc/Hz]S_{\phi,out}(f)=N^2\,S_{\phi,in}(f)\ \ [\text{rad}^2/\text{Hz}],\qquad \mathcal{L}_{out}(f)=\mathcal{L}_{in}(f)+20\log_{10}N\ \ [\text{dBc/Hz}]Sϕ,out​(f)=N2Sϕ,in​(f)  [rad2/Hz],Lout​(f)=Lin​(f)+20log10​N  [dBc/Hz]

The second equation used L=12Sϕ\mathcal{L}=\tfrac12 S_\phiL=21​Sϕ​ — input and output use the same convention, the 12\tfrac1221​ cancels, so +20log⁡10N+20\log_{10}N+20log10​N is independent of the /2-vs-/4 convention. For N=50N=50N=50, +20log⁡1050=+33.98+20\log_{10}50=+33.98+20log10​50=+33.98 dB ≈+34\approx+34≈+34 dB.

  • Physical meaning: multiplication creates no noise. It magnifies "the same absolute time jitter" into an NNN-times larger angle — one output period is only 1/N1/N1/N as long as the input's, so the same error in seconds occupies NNN times the fraction of an output period.
  • The offset axis does not move (common mistake): what gets multiplied by NNN is the phase amplitude, not the rhythm of the phase fluctuations. The entire L\mathcal{L}L curve shifts vertically up by 20log⁡10N20\log_{10}N20log10​N; the horizontal axis (offset fff) is completely unchanged.
  • Dimension check: NNN dimensionless, ϕ\phiϕ in rad, SϕS_\phiSϕ​ in rad²/Hz, 20log⁡10N20\log_{10}N20log10​N in dB ✓.
  • Time-jitter conservation: Δtout=ϕout2πNfref=Nϕin2πNfref=ϕin2πfref=Δtin\Delta t_{out}=\dfrac{\phi_{out}}{2\pi N f_{ref}}=\dfrac{N\phi_{in}}{2\pi N f_{ref}} =\dfrac{\phi_{in}}{2\pi f_{ref}}=\Delta t_{in}Δtout​=2πNfref​ϕout​​=2πNfref​Nϕin​​=2πfref​ϕin​​=Δtin​ — the edge error in seconds is unchanged (verified numerically in Step 5 below).

Failure conditions: (1) small-angle approximation — σϕ,out=Nσϕ,in\sigma_{\phi,out}=N\sigma_{\phi,in}σϕ,out​=Nσϕ,in​; for large NNN (e.g. N=1000N=1000N=1000, +60+60+60 dB) it can approach 1 rad, L≈12Sϕ\mathcal{L}\approx\tfrac12 S_\phiL≈21​Sϕ​ collapses, and the carrier energy redistributes into a Lorentzian (the linewidth diffusion constant DDD is magnified by N2N^2N2, see lorentzian_linewidth); (2) sideband overlap — for offsets near fref/2f_{ref}/2fref​/2 the skirts of the N±1N\pm1N±1 harmonics mix into the bandpass; (3) real multipliers have their own additive floor (Rule 4).

ILCM addendum: this rule assumes a "memoryless nonlinearity + bandpass" — that kind of multiplier creates no noise; it just scales the input's excess phase by NNN, unchanged. A real injection-locked clock multiplier (ILCM) is not that machine: it is a locked oscillator, so the carrier path is still deterministically ϕout≈Nϕin\phi_{out}\approx N\phi_{in}ϕout​≈Nϕin​ (in-band reference noise still eats N2SrefN^2S_{ref}N2Sref​, +20log⁡10N+20\log_{10}N+20log10​N, consistent with this rule) — but its own free-running phase noise is high-pass shaped by a first-order discrete-time loop (corner ≈βfref/2π\approx\beta f_{ref}/2\pi≈βfref​/2π, where β\betaβ is the realignment factor per reference pulse), replacing this rule's "clean constant shift" assumption. Past the corner, the output noise is set by the ILCM's own oscillator quality — no longer something +20log⁡10N+20\log_{10}N+20log10​N describes. Full derivation: subharmonic_injection.

Rule 2: ideal ÷N division — the rigorous origin of −20log⁡10N-20\log_{10}N−20log10​N​

The quadrature_and_coupled_oscillators page, in the ÷2-generates-I/Q section, directly cites Lout=Lin−20log⁡10N\mathcal{L}_{out}=\mathcal{L}_{in}-20\log_{10}NLout​=Lin​−20log10​N; this is the rigorous derivation's home for that equation, and the two pages' numbers agree (÷2 is −6.02-6.02−6.02 dB).

Step 1 (timing of the input edges). The kkk-th input rising zero crossing tkt_ktk​ is defined by the total phase: Φin(tk)=2πk\Phi_{in}(t_k)=2\pi kΦin​(tk​)=2πk. Substituting Φin=ωreft+ϕin(t)\Phi_{in}=\omega_{ref}t+\phi_{in}(t)Φin​=ωref​t+ϕin​(t) and solving:

tk=k Tref−ϕin(tk)ωref⟹δtk=−ϕin(kTref)ωreft_k=k\,T_{ref}-\frac{\phi_{in}(t_k)}{\omega_{ref}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \delta t_k=-\frac{\phi_{in}(kT_{ref})}{\omega_{ref}}tk​=kTref​−ωref​ϕin​(tk​)​⟹δtk​=−ωref​ϕin​(kTref​)​

The second equation used "ϕ\phiϕ slowly varying" (offset ≪fref\ll f_{ref}≪fref​) to replace ϕin(tk)\phi_{in}(t_k)ϕin​(tk​) with ϕin(kTref)\phi_{in}(kT_{ref})ϕin​(kTref​). Dimension check: [rad]/[rad/s]=[s][\text{rad}]/[\text{rad/s}]=[\text{s}][rad]/[rad/s]=[s] ✓.

Step 2 (a divider only drops edges, never moves them). An ideal ÷N is an edge-picking machine: for every NNN input edges it outputs one, and the output edge's time is exactly the time of the selected input edge. So the absolute time error δt\delta tδt passes to the output completely intact:

δtm(out)=δtmN\delta t^{(out)}_m=\delta t_{mN}δtm(out)​=δtmN​

Step 3 (fold the time error back into the output carrier's phase). The output carrier is ωout=ωref/N\omega_{out}=\omega_{ref}/Nωout​=ωref​/N. The output excess phase follows from the same phase definition in reverse (Φout(tm′)=2πm\Phi_{out}(t'_m)=2\pi mΦout​(tm′​)=2πm, tm′=mTout+δtmt'_m=mT_{out}+\delta t_mtm′​=mTout​+δtm​):

ϕout=− ωout δt(out)=ωoutωref ϕin⟹ ϕout=ϕinN \phi_{out}=-\,\omega_{out}\,\delta t^{(out)} =\frac{\omega_{out}}{\omega_{ref}}\,\phi_{in} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\ \phi_{out}=\frac{\phi_{in}}{N}\ }ϕout​=−ωout​δt(out)=ωref​ωout​​ϕin​⟹ ϕout​=Nϕin​​ ​

Step 4 (PSD and dB).

Sϕ,out(f)=Sϕ,in(f)N2,Lout(f)=Lin(f)−20log⁡10NS_{\phi,out}(f)=\frac{S_{\phi,in}(f)}{N^2},\qquad \mathcal{L}_{out}(f)=\mathcal{L}_{in}(f)-20\log_{10}NSϕ,out​(f)=N2Sϕ,in​(f)​,Lout​(f)=Lin​(f)−20log10​N

÷2 is −20log⁡102=−6.02-20\log_{10}2=-6.02−20log10​2=−6.02 dB. Physical meaning: the same jitter in seconds, spread over a period NNN times longer, is an angle NNN times smaller. Perfectly symmetric with Rule 1: ×N then ÷N brings L\mathcal{L}L back to where it started, and σt\sigma_tσt​ (seconds) is unchanged throughout.

Failure conditions (both matter):

  1. Sampling foldover (aliasing): ϕout\phi_{out}ϕout​ is defined only at the output edge instants — this is a system sampled at ∼fout\sim f_{out}∼fout​. Components of the input phase noise at offsets above ∼fout/2\sim f_{out}/2∼fout​/2 fold back into the output band; for a flat wideband noise floor, division does not earn the full 20log⁡10N20\log_{10}N20log10​N (foldover stacks the power back). The clean −20log⁡10N-20\log_{10}N−20log10​N holds only for close-in noise at offsets ≪fout\ll f_{out}≪fout​. (External literature, not among the five source PDFs; for the standard divider noise model see Egan at the end of this page.)
  2. The divider's own floor: real dividers (CML latch, TSPC) have their own additive floor (Rule 4), often higher than "the cleanly divided-down signal" — after division the output can never be better than the divider's own floor.

Relation to [P4]: an injection-locked frequency divider (ILFD) uses the ISF's 2nd harmonic to lock 2f02f_02f0​ to f0f_0f0​, implementing ÷2 ([P4], frequency division in Part II, see paper_004). The ÷N phase accounting (ϕ/N\phi/Nϕ/N) holds equally for the ILFD's carrier path; but an ILFD near the edge of its lock range has its own noise behavior, outside this page's ideal accounting.

Rule 3: through a PLL — the reference goes "×N + lowpass", the VCO goes "highpass"​

A PLL is the closed-loop implementation of Rule 1: the divider brings the output back to freff_{ref}fref​ for phase comparison, which forces "output phase =N×=N\times=N× reference phase" — so reference noise first takes +20log⁡10N+20\log_{10}N+20log10​N (Rule 1), and is then shaped by the closed-loop lowpass ∣Hlp∣2\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2∣Hlp​∣2; the VCO's own noise is shaped by the highpass ∣Hhp∣2\lvert H_{hp}\rvert^2∣Hhp​∣2:

Sout(f)=N2 Sref(f) ∣Hlp(f)∣2+Svco(f) ∣Hhp(f)∣2[rad2/Hz]S_{out}(f)=N^2\,S_{ref}(f)\,\lvert H_{lp}(f)\rvert^2+S_{vco}(f)\,\lvert H_{hp}(f)\rvert^2 \qquad[\text{rad}^2/\text{Hz}]Sout​(f)=N2Sref​(f)∣Hlp​(f)∣2+Svco​(f)∣Hhp​(f)∣2[rad2/Hz]

The two transfer functions (type-II second order, ωn,ζ\omega_n,\zetaωn​,ζ) and the full five-source budget were derived step by step and verified in pll_noise_budget; this page reuses them directly without re-deriving (that page also includes the charge-pump floor Scp∣Hlp∣2S_{cp}\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2Scp​∣Hlp​∣2; to keep this page's worked chain focused on the four rules, the CP floor is folded conceptually into the "in-band floor" and omitted numerically, marked illustrative). The brick-wall accounting used for the lookup table is its asymptotic version:

  • in-band (f≪fnf\ll f_nf≪fn​): ∣Hlp∣2→1\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2\to1∣Hlp​∣2→1, ∣Hhp∣2→0\lvert H_{hp}\rvert^2\to0∣Hhp​∣2→0 ⇒ Lout≈Lref+20log⁡10N\mathcal{L}_{out}\approx\mathcal{L}_{ref}+20\log_{10}NLout​≈Lref​+20log10​N.
  • out-of-band (f≫fnf\gg f_nf≫fn​): ∣Hlp∣2→0\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2\to0∣Hlp​∣2→0, ∣Hhp∣2→1\lvert H_{hp}\rvert^2\to1∣Hhp​∣2→1 ⇒ Lout≈Lvco\mathcal{L}_{out}\approx\mathcal{L}_{vco}Lout​≈Lvco​ (the free-running VCO skirt).
  • Take the crossover at the loop bandwidth fnf_nfn​.

Dimension check: all SSS in rad²/Hz, N2N^2N2 and ∣H∣2\lvert H\rvert^2∣H∣2 dimensionless ✓. The brick-wall version is convenient but has one famous trap — the pure second-order loop's reference tail, laid out numerically in Step 6.

Rule 4: the buffer/divider additive floor — power addition, never dB addition​

Step 1 (why a buffer is "additive"). A buffer regenerates edges: at the instant the input waveform crosses the switching threshold, the noise voltage vnv_nvn​ (V) of the buffer's internal devices sits on top of the threshold and displaces the output edge by

Δtadd=vn(tk)SR[VV/s=s] ✓\Delta t_{add}=\frac{v_n(t_k)}{SR}\qquad \Big[\frac{\text{V}}{\text{V/s}}=\text{s}\Big]\ \checkmarkΔtadd​=SRvn​(tk​)​[V/sV​=s] ✓

(SRSRSR = slew rate at the threshold crossing, V/s. This is the same physics as waveform_slope's "most sensitive where the slope is small".) vnv_nvn​ comes from the buffer's own devices and is uncorrelated with the input clock's phase.

Step 2 (uncorrelated ⇒ PSDs add). In phase this is pure addition:

ϕout=ϕin+ϕadd⟹Sϕ,out(f)=Sϕ,in(f)+Sbuf(f)\phi_{out}=\phi_{in}+\phi_{add} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad S_{\phi,out}(f)=S_{\phi,in}(f)+S_{buf}(f)ϕout​=ϕin​+ϕadd​⟹Sϕ,out​(f)=Sϕ,in​(f)+Sbuf​(f)

(The cross term ⟨ϕinϕadd⟩=0\langle\phi_{in}\phi_{add}\rangle=0⟨ϕin​ϕadd​⟩=0.) Converting to dBc/Hz gives the lookup-table formula — note you must convert to linear first, add, then convert back to dB:

 Lout(f)=10log⁡10(10Lin(f)/10+10Lbuf(f)/10) \boxed{\ \mathcal{L}_{out}(f)=10\log_{10}\Big(10^{\mathcal{L}_{in}(f)/10}+10^{\mathcal{L}_{buf}(f)/10}\Big)\ } Lout​(f)=10log10​(10Lin​(f)/10+10Lbuf​(f)/10) ​

Both L\mathcal{L}L are SSB on the same carrier with the same convention; the 12\tfrac1221​ of L=12Sϕ\mathcal{L}=\tfrac12 S_\phiL=21​Sϕ​ cancels on both sides — so accounting directly in L\mathcal{L}L is legitimate, independent of the /2-vs-/4 convention.

Step 3 (multiplicative vs additive — the most important classification on this page). Rules 1–3 are multiplicative: they scale/shape the incoming phase curve as a whole; a clean source gives a clean output. Rule 4 is additive: the buffer injects new, independent noise, and the output can never be better than the buffer's own floor — however clean the source, one noisy buffer stage ruins it. That is what "floor dominates" means.

Step 5: the conserved quantity — under ideal ×N/÷N, σt\sigma_tσt​ (seconds) is unchanged​

Put the conclusions of Rules 1 and 2 side by side: ×N gives ϕ×N\phi\times Nϕ×N while the carrier gets f0×Nf_0\times Nf0​×N; ÷N gives ϕ/N\phi/Nϕ/N while f0/Nf_0/Nf0​/N. Substituting into Δt=ϕ/(2πf0)\Delta t=\phi/(2\pi f_0)Δt=ϕ/(2πf0​) (canonical formula 17), the two NNN's cancel:

σt,out=σϕ,out2πf0,out=N±1 σϕ,in2π N±1f0,in=σt,in\sigma_{t,out}=\frac{\sigma_{\phi,out}}{2\pi f_{0,out}} =\frac{N^{\pm1}\,\sigma_{\phi,in}}{2\pi\,N^{\pm1} f_{0,in}}=\sigma_{t,in}σt,out​=2πf0,out​σϕ,out​​=2πN±1f0,in​N±1σϕ,in​​=σt,in​

Time jitter in seconds is the invariant of ideal multiplication/division. The L\mathcal{L}L that got worse (or better) is only a change in the exchange rate "the same error in seconds converted to angle". This gives you an extremely useful sanity check: on any chain segment that is "pure ×N/÷N with no additive floor", the σt\sigma_tσt​ computed at both ends over the same integration band must be equal. Verify with the worked chain's numbers (the 5 GHz stage vs the 2.5 GHz after ideal ÷2, both without the buffer):

import numpy as np
from simulations.common.noise_utils import integrate_rms_jitter
f = np.logspace(4, 8, 20001)
L5G = np.where(f <= 1e6, -126.02, -148.0 - 20*np.log10(f/1e6))
st5, _ = integrate_rms_jitter(f, L5G, f0=5e9, fmin=1e4, fmax=1e8)
st25, _ = integrate_rms_jitter(f, L5G - 6.02, f0=2.5e9, fmin=1e4, fmax=1e8)
print(round(st5*1e15, 1))    # -> 22.5
print(round(st25*1e15, 1))   # -> 22.5

The two 22.5 fs values are identical — ÷2 improved L\mathcal{L}L by 6 dB yet saved not a single fs. For SerDes this is actually the flip side of bad news: converted to UI, σt\sigma_tσt​ is unchanged while the UI gets longer, so after division the "fraction of a UI" does shrink — what you save is a ratio, not seconds.

Step 7: honest comparison — brick-wall lookup vs full type-II shaping​

Brick-wall is lookup-table-grade approximation. Computing with pll_noise_budget's actual ∣Hlp∣2\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2∣Hlp​∣2 (pll_utils, fn=1f_n=1fn​=1 MHz, ζ=0.707\zeta=0.707ζ=0.707), the difference at the two offsets is plain:

  • in-band (100 kHz): shaped −131.9-131.9−131.9 vs brick-wall −132.0-132.0−132.0 — only 0.1 dB apart (slight peaking of ∣Hlp∣2\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2∣Hlp​∣2 at fn/10f_n/10fn​/10). The lookup table is reliable ✓.
  • out-of-band (10 MHz): shaped −148.0-148.0−148.0 vs brick-wall −154.9-154.9−154.9 — 7 dB apart!

The reason: the type-II second-order closed loop's zero makes ∣Hlp∣2\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2∣Hlp​∣2 roll off at only −20-20−20 dB/dec for f≫fnf\gg f_nf≫fn​ (∣Hlp∣2≈(2ζfn/f)2\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2\approx(2\zeta f_n/f)^2∣Hlp​∣2≈(2ζfn​/f)2), so the reference floor raised by ×N2\times N^2×N2 leaks a −20-20−20 dB/dec tail into the out-of-band region; and the VCO skirt is also −20-20−20 dB/dec — the two lines are parallel, the gap is constant and can never be closed:

import numpy as np
from simulations.common.pll_utils import H_lowpass_mag2
S_refN2 = 2 * 10**(-126.02/10)          # N²·S_ref (S_phi of the in-band floor) [rad²/Hz]
lp = H_lowpass_mag2(10e6, 1e6)          # |H_lp|² @ 10 MHz, fn = 1 MHz
L_refpath = 10*np.log10(0.5 * S_refN2 * lp)
print(round(L_refpath, 1))              # -> -143.0
print(round(L_refpath - (-168.0), 1))   # -> 25.0

The reference tail at 10 MHz is −143.0-143.0−143.0 dBc/Hz (5 GHz carrier), 25 dB above the VCO's −168-168−168 — and because both have the same slope, these 25 dB hold at every out-of-band offset. The lookup-table cell "out-of-band = VCO" is, for a pure second-order loop, simply unreachable. The consequence for integrated jitter (actual run of fig_clock_chain.py):

ModelFinal σt\sigma_tσt​ (10 kHz–100 MHz)
brick-wall lookup27.6 fs

second order + 3rd pole @ 3 MHz (illustrative)38.6 fs

How to fix it: real synthesizers add a third pole to the loop filter (plus higher-order post-filters) precisely for this, turning the ref tail into −40-40−40 dB/dec or steeper; the third row above shows one 3 MHz pole cutting the damage by a third (the pole-placement vs loop-stability trade-off belongs to the standard PLL literature — external literature, not among the five source PDFs).

One level more honest: this chain's fn=1f_n=1fn​=1 MHz was never the jitter-optimal choice to begin with — the crossover of the in-band floor (−126-126−126) and the VCO skirt sits at 79.679.679.6 kHz, far below 1 MHz. Sweeping fnf_nfn​ for this chain with pll_noise_budget's U-curve method (shaped model, 3rd pole tracking at 3fn3f_n3fn​): the minimum is at fn\*≈53f_n^\*\approx53fn\*​≈53 kHz, σt≈19.6\sigma_t\approx19.6σt​≈19.6 fs. Lookup accounting (this page) tells you each stage's bill; loop optimization (that page) tells you how to change the bill — two different things, don't mix them.

Connection to SerDes​

The final 2.5 GHz clock's σt=27.6\sigma_t=27.6σt​=27.6 fs feeds directly into serdes_clocking_connection's eye/BER machinery: if this clock drives a 5 Gb/s half-rate link (UI =200=200=200 ps), the RJ overhead at BER =10−12=10^{-12}=10−12 (Q−1≈7.03Q^{-1}\approx7.03Q−1≈7.03, site canonical) is 2×7.03×27.6 fs=0.392\times7.03\times27.6\ \text{fs}=0.392×7.03×27.6 fs=0.39 ps =0.19%=0.19\%=0.19% UI — quite healthy; but note every extra buffer stage in the clock tree adds another Rule-4 floor (power addition) — in a high-fan-out tree the buffers alone can eat the whole budget. Accumulated jitter of the free-running segment ([P2] Eq.(8), p.792, σΔt=κΔt\sigma_{\Delta t}=\kappa\sqrt{\Delta t}σΔt​=κΔt​) is high-pass truncated once it enters the PLL/CDR loop — in this chain, "who free-runs and who is locked" determines which noise accumulates and which does not (Step 6 of that page).

Key takeaways​

  • The four rules: ×N adds 20log⁡10N20\log_{10}N20log10​N (ϕout=Nϕin\phi_{out}=N\phi_{in}ϕout​=Nϕin​, offset axis unchanged); ÷N subtracts 20log⁡10N20\log_{10}N20log10​N (edge-picking, time error intact, angle divided by NNN); PLL = reference through N2∣Hlp∣2N^2\lvert H_{lp}\rvert^2N2∣Hlp​∣2, VCO through ∣Hhp∣2\lvert H_{hp}\rvert^2∣Hhp​∣2 (transfer functions reused from pll_noise_budget); buffer/divider floor = power addition, Lout=10log⁡10(10Lin/10+10Lbuf/10)\mathcal{L}_{out}=10\log_{10}(10^{\mathcal{L}_{in}/10}+10^{\mathcal{L}_{buf}/10})Lout​=10log10​(10Lin​/10+10Lbuf​/10).
  • Conserved quantity: under ideal ×N/÷N, σt\sigma_tσt​ (seconds) is unchanged (both ends 22.5 fs in this example); ÷N saves "fraction of a UI", not seconds.
  • Worked chain (100 MHz→×50→5 GHz→÷2→2.5 GHz→buffer): at 100 kHz, −160→−126.02→−132.04→−132.02-160\to-126.02\to-132.04\to-132.02−160→−126.02→−132.04→−132.02; at 10 MHz, −160→−168.00→−174.02→−154.95-160\to-168.00\to-174.02\to-154.95−160→−168.00→−174.02→−154.95 (floor dominates).
  • Final integrated jitter (10 kHz–100 MHz) = 27.6 fs; breakdown = ref×N2\times N^2×N2 floor 65.9% + buffer floor 33.7% + VCO 0.42% — accounting first, don't blindly upgrade the VCO.
  • Honest comparison: the pure type-II second-order loop's ref tail runs parallel to the VCO skirt (a constant +25+25+25 dB here), shaped σt=44.0\sigma_t=44.0σt​=44.0 fs (59% above the lookup); adding a 3rd pole (3 MHz) → 38.6 fs; this chain's jitter-optimal loop BW is actually fn\*≈53f_n^\*\approx53fn\*​≈53 kHz (σt≈19.6\sigma_t\approx19.6σt​≈19.6 fs).
  • Convention discipline: the rules are all ratio/addition operations, /2-vs-/4 cancels; the only convention-sensitive item is the VCO anchor (−148-148−148 = [P1] Eq.(21)'s /4 SSB; the time-domain /2 gives −145-145−145).
