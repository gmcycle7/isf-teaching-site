Device noise → ISF harmonics mapping

β: This English translation is in beta — the Traditional-Chinese original is the authoritative version.

Prerequisites: white_noise_to_phase_noise (white → 1/f²), flicker_noise_upconversion (flicker → 1/f³), fourier_series_of_isf (c0c_0c0​, cnc_ncn​) | Next: symmetry, tank_swing, serdes_clocking_connection

This page collects the scattered design intuition from earlier pages into a single map: how does the noise (white and flicker) of one transistor end up as the oscillator's phase noise? The key is two things — which frequency band of device noise is picked up by which ISF harmonic, and the device does not leak noise throughout the entire period (cyclostationary), so what actually matters is the effective ISF Γeff=Γ⋅α\Gamma_{eff}=\Gamma\cdot\alphaΓeff​=Γ⋅α.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): think of the ISF as a multi-channel radio. Its Fourier coefficients cnc_ncn​ are the antenna gains of each channel: c0c_0c0​ picks up device flicker "near DC" (upconverted to 1/f³); c1c_1c1​ picks up device white noise "near ω0\omega_0ω0​", c2c_2c2​ "near 2ω02\omega_02ω0​", and so on (downconverted to 1/f²). But the device only actually conducts — only actually leaks noise — during certain phases of the waveform. This "when does it leak" switching function α(ω0t)\alpha(\omega_0 t)α(ω0​t) must be multiplied into Γ\GammaΓ first, becoming the effective ISF, before it is the radio's true antenna.

Step 1: two "bands" of device noise​

The drain current noise of a MOS device (referred to the injection node) splits roughly into two bands ([P1] Eqs.(19),(22), p.185):

Si(f)=in2‾Δf⏟white (flat)  +  in2‾Δf⋅ω1/f2πf⏟flicker (1/f)S_i(f)=\underbrace{\frac{\overline{i_n^2}}{\Delta f}}_{\text{white (flat)}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{\overline{i_n^2}}{\Delta f}\cdot\frac{\omega_{1/f}}{2\pi f}}_{\text{flicker (1/f)}}Si​(f)=white (flat)Δfin2​​​​​+flicker (1/f)Δfin2​​​⋅2πfω1/f​​​​
  • White noise: thermal (4kTγgm4kT\gamma g_m4kTγgm​-type) + shot, flat with frequency, energy spread over all frequencies, including near ω0,2ω0,…\omega_0,2\omega_0,\dotsω0​,2ω0​,….
  • Flicker (1/f) noise: slow trap/release processes in the channel, energy concentrated at low frequency (Δω≪ω0\Delta\omega\ll\omega_0Δω≪ω0​), significant below the device's 1/f corner ω1/f\omega_{1/f}ω1/f​.
  • Units: both bands are A²/Hz.

Step 2: ISF harmonics are "receive channels" — who picks up what​

Substitute the ISF Fourier expansion ([P1] Eq.(12)) into the phase response ([P1] Eq.(13)): each cnc_ncn​ term is a mixer that shifts "noise near nω0n\omega_0nω0​" down to baseband phase:

  • One-line summary: Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ governs white (1/f²); c0c_0c0​ governs flicker (1/f³). This is the skeleton of the whole map.

Step 3: cyclostationary — the device does not leak noise all the time​

The Γ\GammaΓ above is the "bare ISF" — it assumes the noise is equally large at every instant. But a real device's noise strength is periodically varying (cyclostationary): e.g. a tail current source conducts only during part of the cycle, and a switching pair's gmg_mgm​ peaks only during transitions. Hajimiri–Lee describe "when the device is leaking noise" with a noise-modulating function (NMF) α(ω0t)\alpha(\omega_0 t)α(ω0​t) (0≤α≤10\le\alpha\le10≤α≤1, periodic), folded into the ISF ([P1] Sec. II-D, Eq.(25)–(27), p.186; in(t)=in0(t)⋅α(ω0t)i_n(t)=i_{n0}(t)\cdot\alpha(\omega_0 t)in​(t)=in0​(t)⋅α(ω0​t) is Eq.(25), Γeff=Γ⋅α\Gamma_{eff}=\Gamma\cdot\alphaΓeff​=Γ⋅α is Eq.(27)):

 Γeff(ω0τ)=Γ(ω0τ)⋅α(ω0τ) \boxed{\ \Gamma_{eff}(\omega_0\tau)=\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)\cdot\alpha(\omega_0\tau)\ } Γeff​(ω0​τ)=Γ(ω0​τ)⋅α(ω0​τ) ​
  • Meaning: what actually enters the phase-noise formulas is not Γ\GammaΓ but Γeff\Gamma_{eff}Γeff​. All of cnc_ncn​, c0c_0c0​, Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ must be recomputed using Γeff\Gamma_{eff}Γeff​.
  • A design-critical consequence: even if waveform symmetry makes the bare Γ\GammaΓ's c0=0c_0=0c0​=0, if the device leaks noise only during half the cycle (α\alphaα asymmetric), Γeff=Γ⋅α\Gamma_{eff}=\Gamma\cdot\alphaΓeff​=Γ⋅α can regrow a nonzero c0c_0c0​ → flicker gets upconverted again. Conversely, aligning the device's "conduction window α\alphaα" with the low-value phase of the bare Γ\GammaΓ can simultaneously lower c0c_0c0​ and Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​.
  • A more rigorous mathematical foundation (PPV / adjoint / Floquet) is not in the five downloaded PDFs and is supplemented from standard literature (Demir–Mehrotra–Roychowdhury 2000, etc.); see effective_isf.

A toy calculation using the built-in functions (pedagogical toy model, not transistor-level):

import numpy as np
from simulations.common.isf_utils import (
    gamma_lc_ideal, effective_isf, compute_fourier_coefficients, gamma_rms)

theta = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 2048, endpoint=False)
gamma = gamma_lc_ideal(theta)            # bare ISF = -sin(theta), c0 = 0
# Asymmetric NMF: device conducts only in the first half-cycle (alpha=1 for theta<pi, else 0)
alpha = np.where(theta < np.pi, 1.0, 0.0)
gamma_eff = effective_isf(gamma, alpha)       # multiply by NMF (positional: gamma_values, alpha_values)
a0, a, b, c, ph = compute_fourier_coefficients(theta, gamma_eff, n_harmonics=8)
print("c0 =", a0, " Gamma_rms =", gamma_rms(theta, gamma_eff))
# -> c0 ≈ -0.637 (bare Γ had c0=0; regrown by the asymmetric α), Gamma_rms ≈ 0.500

(Full API in simulations/common/isf_utils.py; effective_isf and gamma_rms are real functions.)

Step 4: two independent levers — which knobs move Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​, which move qmaxq_{max}qmax​​

The core phase-noise ratios are Γrms2qmax2\dfrac{\Gamma_{rms}^2}{q_{max}^2}qmax2​Γrms2​​ (white, [P1] Eq.(21)) and c02qmax2\dfrac{c_0^2}{q_{max}^2}qmax2​c02​​ (flicker, [P1] Eq.(23)). The numerator (ISF shape) and denominator (charge swing) are two mutually independent knobs. Classifying every design knob by "which quantity it moves":

Numerical example (building intuition)​

Map a single device's two noise bands onto the two phase-noise regions.

Take canonical values: f0=5f_0=5f0​=5 GHz, qmax=1q_{max}=1qmax​=1 pC, Γrms=0.5\Gamma_{rms}=0.5Γrms​=0.5, Si=10−24S_i=10^{-24}Si​=10−24 A²/Hz, asymmetric c0=0.4c_0=0.4c0​=0.4, device f1/f=1f_{1/f}=1f1/f​=1 MHz.

  • Flicker → 1/f³ (@ Δf=1\Delta f=1Δf=1 MHz, [P1] Eq.(23)): relative to 1/f² there is an extra factor c022Γrms2⋅ω1/fΔω\dfrac{c_0^2}{2\Gamma_{rms}^2}\cdot\dfrac{\omega_{1/f}}{\Delta\omega}2Γrms2​c02​​⋅Δωω1/f​​. Only below 320 kHz does flicker dominate.
  • Intuition: at 1 MHz offset flicker is barely visible (masked by white); to see 1/f³ you need to look below 100 kHz. If a SerDes loop bandwidth filters out the close-in region, the 1/f³ impact shrinks (see serdes_clocking_connection).

Key takeaways​

  • Device noise has two bands: white (spread over all frequencies), flicker (concentrated at low frequency).
  • ISF harmonics are receive channels: c0c_0c0​ receives flicker → 1/f³; c1,c2,…c_1,c_2,\dotsc1​,c2​,… receive white → 1/f² (collapsed to Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ via Parseval).
  • What actually matters is the effective ISF Γeff=Γ⋅α\Gamma_{eff}=\Gamma\cdot\alphaΓeff​=Γ⋅α (cyclostationary); a symmetric bare Γ\GammaΓ can regrow c0c_0c0​ because of an asymmetric α\alphaα.
  • Two independent levers: move Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​/c0c_0c0​ (waveform symmetry, fast edges, differential, α\alphaα alignment) vs. move qmaxq_{max}qmax​ (swing, QQQ, differential).
  • Ring Γrms∝N−3/2\Gamma_{rms}\propto N^{-3/2}Γrms​∝N−3/2 ([P2] Eq.(16), p.794, re-verified in v7: the square root covers only the constant); at fixed f0f_0f0​/power, phase noise is roughly independent of NNN.
