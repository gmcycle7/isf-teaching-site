DJ and the Dual-Dirac Model: The Industry-Standard Tool for TJ@BER

Prerequisites: serdes_clocking_connection (first appearance of RJ/DJ/TJ, eye and BER), lab_12 (RJ-only bathtub), lab_08 (σt\sigma_tσt​ integrated from phase noise) | Next: exercises, lab_13

serdes_clocking_connection already gave the shorthand "TJ === DJ + 2Q⋅+\,2Q\cdot+2Q⋅RJ". This page builds that formula rigorously from scratch: what RJ is, what DJ is, which integral QQQ comes from, how the bathtub curve is derived from the CDF (cumulative distribution function) of jitter, and the dual-Dirac model the industry actually uses (SerDes specs from Fibre Channel MJSQ onward) — including its most misunderstood and most important honesty note: the model parameter DJδδ\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}DJδδ​ is inherently less than or equal to the actual peak-to-peak DJ, and this "under-report" is deliberate — it is precisely what makes the TJ extrapolation accurate.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): jitter has two fundamentally different components. RJ (random jitter) is the time-domain incarnation of oscillator phase noise — Gaussian, unbounded; the longer you wait (the tighter the BER target), the larger it "grows", so it must be booked as σ\sigmaσ times a multiplier that increases with the BER target. DJ (deterministic jitter) is driven by deterministic physical mechanisms (inter-symbol interference, duty-cycle distortion, supply ripple); its amplitude has a physical upper bound, so it is booked peak-to-peak and does not grow with BER. The dual-Dirac model compresses "bounded DJ of arbitrary shape" into two Diracs and keeps RJ as a Gaussian, in exchange for a straight line that can be extrapolated to 10−1210^{-12}10−12. Measuring a BER of 10−1210^{-12}10−12 requires waiting for 101210^{12}1012 bits — even at 10 Gb/s that is 100 s just to "see one error on average", and statistical confidence takes hours; extrapolation is not laziness, it is an engineering necessity.

RJ's key property: no upper bound. The Gaussian tail is never zero — there is no "guaranteed never to cross" margin; you can only ask "how small is the crossing probability". This is why RJ must be booked as σ\sigmaσ paired with a target BER (the QQQ function of Step 3).

Step 2: DJ — bounded, driven by deterministic mechanisms​

Step 4: The dual-Dirac model — definition and PDF​

The model does two things (external literature, not among the five source PDFs; methodology references at the bottom of the page):

  1. The TJ PDF is the convolution of the DJ PDF with the Gaussian RJ (RJ and DJ statistically independent): pTJ=pDJ∗gσp_{TJ}=p_{DJ}*g_\sigmapTJ​=pDJ​∗gσ​, where gσ(x)=1σ2πe−x2/2σ2g_\sigma(x)=\tfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2}gσ​(x)=σ2π​1​e−x2/2σ2 (unit: 1/s).
  2. Compress the bounded pDJp_{DJ}pDJ​ of arbitrary shape into two equal-weight Diracs, with spacing denoted DJδδ\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}DJδδ​:
pDJ(x) ⟶ 12 δ(x−μR)+12 δ(x−μL),DJδδ≡μR−μLp_{DJ}(x)\ \longrightarrow\ \frac12\,\delta(x-\mu_R)+\frac12\,\delta(x-\mu_L),\qquad \mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}\equiv\mu_R-\mu_LpDJ​(x) ⟶ 21​δ(x−μR​)+21​δ(x−μL​),DJδδ​≡μR​−μL​

The convolution uses the Dirac sampling property ∫δ(v−μ)gσ(x−v)dv=gσ(x−μ)\int\delta(v-\mu)g_\sigma(x-v)dv=g_\sigma(x-\mu)∫δ(v−μ)gσ​(x−v)dv=gσ​(x−μ), giving the model PDF in one step:

pδδ(x)=12 gσ(x−μR)+12 gσ(x−μL)p_{\delta\delta}(x)=\frac12\,g_\sigma(x-\mu_R)+\frac12\,g_\sigma(x-\mu_L)pδδ​(x)=21​gσ​(x−μR​)+21​gσ​(x−μL​)

— two Gaussians with the same σ\sigmaσ, each carrying half the probability. In the symmetric case (this page and lab_31), μR=−μL=μ=DJδδ/2\mu_R=-\mu_L=\mu=\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}/2μR​=−μL​=μ=DJδδ​/2.

The tail function, integrated step by step. Define T(x)≡P(jitter>x)=1−F(x)T(x)\equiv P(\text{jitter}\gt x)=1-F(x)T(x)≡P(jitter>x)=1−F(x) (FFF is the CDF). Apply Step 3's result term by term to the model PDF, ∫x∞gσ(v−μ) dv=Q(x−μσ)\int_x^\infty g_\sigma(v-\mu)\,dv=Q\big(\tfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\big)∫x∞​gσ​(v−μ)dv=Q(σx−μ​):

Tδδ(x)=12 Q ⁣(x−μRσ)+12 Q ⁣(x−μLσ)T_{\delta\delta}(x)=\frac12\,Q\!\Big(\frac{x-\mu_R}{\sigma}\Big)+\frac12\,Q\!\Big(\frac{x-\mu_L}{\sigma}\Big)Tδδ​(x)=21​Q(σx−μR​​)+21​Q(σx−μL​​)

Only one term survives in the deep tail. The ratio of the two terms (asymptotic form, symmetric case):

Q(x+μσ)Q(x−μσ)≈exp⁡ ⁣(−(x+μ)2−(x−μ)22σ2)=exp⁡ ⁣(−2μxσ2)=exp⁡ ⁣(−DJδδ xσ2)\frac{Q\big(\tfrac{x+\mu}{\sigma}\big)}{Q\big(\tfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\big)}\approx\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(x+\mu)^2-(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big)=\exp\!\Big(-\frac{2\mu x}{\sigma^2}\Big)=\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}\,x}{\sigma^2}\Big)Q(σx−μ​)Q(σx+μ​)​≈exp(−2σ2(x+μ)2−(x−μ)2​)=exp(−σ22μx​)=exp(−σ2DJδδ​x​)

With lab_31's numbers (DJδδ=3.16\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}=3.16DJδδ​=3.16 ps, σ=1.03\sigma=1.03σ=1.03 ps, region of interest x≈8.6x\approx8.6x≈8.6 ps), this ratio is ≈e−25.6≈8×10−12\approx e^{-25.6}\approx8\times10^{-12}≈e−25.6≈8×10−12 — completely negligible. So the deep tail is just a single Gaussian with weight ½:

Tδδ(x)≈12 Q ⁣(x−μσ)(x in the deep right tail)T_{\delta\delta}(x)\approx\frac12\,Q\!\Big(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\Big)\qquad(x\ \text{in the deep right tail})Tδδ​(x)≈21​Q(σx−μ​)(x in the deep right tail)

The Q-scale straight line — the principle behind extracting (DJδδ,σ)(\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta},\sigma)(DJδδ​,σ). Invert the expression above:

Q−1(2 T(x))=x−μσQ^{-1}\big(2\,T(x)\big)=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}Q−1(2T(x))=σx−μ​

— on the "Q-scale" (vertical axis Q−1(2T)Q^{-1}(2T)Q−1(2T), horizontal axis xxx), the deep tail is a straight line: slope 1/σ1/\sigma1/σ, horizontal-axis intercept μ=DJδδ/2\mu=\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}/2μ=DJδδ​/2. Note the factor 2 inside Q−1Q^{-1}Q−1: it books the fact that "each Dirac carries only half the probability" — the first factor-of-2 to keep an eye on in this page (Step 7 has two more).

Step 5: The bathtub curve — derived step by step from the jitter CDF​

Assume NRZ data with UI (unit interval) =Tb=T_b=Tb​ (unit: s). Take the eye center as the time origin and let the sampling-instant offset be ttt; the left data edge sits nominally at −UI/2-UI/2−UI/2, the right edge at +UI/2+UI/2+UI/2, each carrying jitter xxx (identically distributed, tail TTT, CDF FFF).

  1. Error event one (left edge late): the left edge actually lands at −UI/2+x-UI/2+x−UI/2+x; if it arrives later than the sampling instant, i.e. −UI/2+x>t⇔x>UI/2+t-UI/2+x\gt t\Leftrightarrow x\gt UI/2+t−UI/2+x>t⇔x>UI/2+t, the sampler reads the previous bit. Probability =T(UI/2+t)=T(UI/2+t)=T(UI/2+t).
  2. Error event two (right edge early): the right edge actually lands at +UI/2+x+UI/2+x+UI/2+x; if it arrives earlier than the sampling instant, i.e. UI/2+x<t⇔x<−(UI/2−t)UI/2+x\lt t\Leftrightarrow x\lt-(UI/2-t)UI/2+x<t⇔x<−(UI/2−t), probability =F(−(UI/2−t))=F(-(UI/2-t))=F(−(UI/2−t)), which for a symmetric distribution =T(UI/2−t)=T(UI/2-t)=T(UI/2−t).
  3. Only a transition can produce an error: for random data the probability that adjacent bits differ is 12\tfrac1221​ (transition density ρT=12\rho_T=\tfrac12ρT​=21​). Both events are rare, so the probability of the union ≈\approx≈ the sum (the intersection is second-order small):
 BER(t)=12[T ⁣(UI2−t)+T ⁣(UI2+t)] \boxed{\ \mathrm{BER}(t)=\frac12\Big[T\!\Big(\frac{UI}{2}-t\Big)+T\!\Big(\frac{UI}{2}+t\Big)\Big]\ } BER(t)=21​[T(2UI​−t)+T(2UI​+t)] ​

Consistency check: for pure RJ, T(x)=Q(x/σt)T(x)=Q(x/\sigma_t)T(x)=Q(x/σt​) and the expression reduces to BER(t)=12[Q(UI/2−tσt)+Q(UI/2+tσt)]\mathrm{BER}(t)=\tfrac12[Q(\tfrac{UI/2-t}{\sigma_t})+Q(\tfrac{UI/2+t}{\sigma_t})]BER(t)=21​[Q(σt​UI/2−t​)+Q(σt​UI/2+t​)] — exactly the formula of lab_12 and serdes_utils.ber_bathtub ✓. The bathtub's two "walls" are just the left and right tails of the jitter CDF, replotted with the horizontal axis changed from "jitter magnitude" to "sampling position". DJ's effect is immediately visible: it shifts the entire tail by ≈μ\approx\mu≈μ → both walls move inward; RJ sets the walls' slope (on a log-BER axis, the slope is set by σ\sigmaσ).

  • Dimension check: the argument of TTT is [s][\text{s}][s], its output a probability (dimensionless); BER dimensionless ✓.
  • Applicability: rare events (BER≪1\mathrm{BER}\ll1BER≪1); edge jitter stationary and data-independent (strictly speaking, ISI is data-correlated — see the failure conditions in Step 8).

Step 7: The honesty note — DJδδ≤DJpp\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}\le\mathrm{DJ}_{pp}DJδδ​≤DJpp​, and the under-report is deliberate​

This is the most commonly misunderstood point about dual-Dirac: DJδδ\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}DJδδ​ is a model parameter, not the actual peak-to-peak DJ. lab_31's numbers: the true sinusoidal DJ has DJpp=4.0\mathrm{DJ}_{pp}=4.0DJpp​=4.0 ps, while the fit gives DJδδ=3.16\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}=3.16DJδδ​=3.16 ps.

Why it is necessarily smaller (derivation). The total jitter tail is the average of the Gaussian tail over the DJ distribution (obtained by swapping the order of integration in the convolution once):

T(x)=P(u+n>x)=∫−AApDJ(u) Q ⁣(x−uσ) duT(x)=P(u+n\gt x)=\int_{-A}^{A}p_{DJ}(u)\,Q\!\Big(\frac{x-u}{\sigma}\Big)\,duT(x)=P(u+n>x)=∫−AA​pDJ​(u)Q(σx−u​)du

Because u≤Au\le Au≤A and QQQ is strictly decreasing, the integrand satisfies pointwise Q(x−uσ)≤Q(x−Aσ)Q\big(\tfrac{x-u}{\sigma}\big)\le Q\big(\tfrac{x-A}{\sigma}\big)Q(σx−u​)≤Q(σx−A​). Split the integral into the right half (u>0u\gt0u>0, total mass ½) and the left half (u≤0u\le0u≤0, whose deep-tail contribution is smaller by another factor of e−Ax/σ2e^{-Ax/\sigma^2}e−Ax/σ2):

T(x) ≤ 12 Q ⁣(x−Aσ)+12 Q ⁣(xσ)⏟exponentially smallT(x)\ \le\ \frac12\,Q\!\Big(\frac{x-A}{\sigma}\Big)+\underbrace{\frac12\,Q\!\Big(\frac{x}{\sigma}\Big)}_{\text{exponentially small}}T(x) ≤ 21​Q(σx−A​)+exponentially small21​Q(σx​)​​

Equality holds only when the entire right-half mass is concentrated at u=Au=Au=A (i.e. a two-point distribution like DCD). Flip to the Q-scale: Q−1(2T(x)) ≥ x−AσQ^{-1}(2T(x))\ \ge\ \tfrac{x-A}{\sigma}Q−1(2T(x)) ≥ σx−A​ — the true tail curve always lies above the line with "Diracs placed at the true extreme AAA" (blue line vs gray dashed line in lab_31 panel (b)). A straight-line fit to the true curve therefore necessarily has intercept μ≤A\mu\le Aμ≤A, i.e.:

DJδδ=2μ ≤ 2A=DJpp\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}=2\mu\ \le\ 2A=\mathrm{DJ}_{pp}DJδδ​=2μ ≤ 2A=DJpp​

Why the under-report actually makes TJ accurate. For the extrapolation to be accurate, what is needed is that "over the few decades around the target BER, the line hugs the true tail height" — and the fit is anchored exactly that way (lab_31: the dual-Dirac extrapolated eye opening is 82.7682.7682.76 ps vs 82.7782.7782.77 ps for the exact composite — a 0.010.010.01 ps difference). Conversely, forcing the Diracs onto the true extremes ±A\pm A±A (using DJpp=4.0\mathrm{DJ}_{pp}=4.0DJpp​=4.0 ps as DJδδ\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}DJδδ​) over-reports the DJ term alone by 4.0−3.16=0.844.0-3.16=0.844.0−3.16=0.84 ps: plugging into the formula (true σ=1.0\sigma=1.0σ=1.0 ps) gives TJ=4.0+14.07×1.0=18.07\mathrm{TJ}=4.0+14.07\times1.0=18.07TJ=4.0+14.07×1.0=18.07 ps, pessimistic by 0.840.840.84 ps compared with the exact bathtub's 17.2317.2317.23 ps — margin thrown away for nothing. Intuition: the DJ distribution carries only finite probability mass near its extremes (the sinusoid's double horns are still integrable singularities), so the deep tail is effectively a "discounted Gaussian" and the equivalent center naturally pulls inward.

The costs (also stated honestly):

  • DJδδ\mathrm{DJ}_{\delta\delta}DJδδ​ depends on the fit depth. lab_31 sweeps three fit windows: T∈[10−8,10−4]→3.07T\in[10^{-8},10^{-4}]\to3.07T∈[10−8,10−4]→3.07 ps, [10−10,10−6]→3.16[10^{-10},10^{-6}]\to3.16[10−10,10−6]→3.16 ps, [10−14,10−10]→3.27[10^{-14},10^{-10}]\to3.27[10−14,10−10]→3.27 ps — the deeper the window, the closer to (but never exceeding) DJpp\mathrm{DJ}_{pp}DJpp​. A DJ/RJ decomposition report should state its fit window; the spec documents (the MJSQ family) provide explicit methodology for this.
  • DJ "leaks" into σ\sigmaσ: the fit gives σ=1.03\sigma=1.03σ=1.03 ps, slightly larger than the true RJ's 1.001.001.00 ps — the residual curvature of the DJ tail is absorbed by the straight line into its slope. So do not take the instrument-reported RJ directly as the integral of the oscillator's phase noise when cross-checking (a few % discrepancy is normal); to cross-check, isolate DJ with a clean clock pattern.

Relation to ISF / this site's chain, and design knobs​

  • ISF owns RJ: every knob for σt\sigma_tσt​ lives in the earlier chapters — lower Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ (waveform symmetry, symmetry), raise qmaxq_{max}qmax​ (tank_swing), lower SiS_iSi​, use the loop high-pass to cut the lower integration limit (serdes_clocking_connection Step 6).
