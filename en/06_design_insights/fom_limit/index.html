The Theoretical Ceiling of FOM

Prerequisites: tank_swing (FOM definition and the phase-noise × power trade-off), white_noise_to_phase_noise ([P1] Eq.(21) and the factor-of-2 convention), lc_vs_ring ([P2] Eq.(23) ring FOM, the −91-91−91 dBc/Hz example) | Next: real_oscillator_topologies, pll_noise_budget

This page answers three questions every VCO designer eventually asks:

  1. Does FOM (figure of merit — the oscillator quality metric) have a physical upper bound? What is it, and what sets it?
  2. The best published LC designs sit around FOM ≈190\approx190≈190 dB — how many dB below the ceiling are they?
  3. Why does the ring oscillator inherently trail LC by about 25 dB? Which term eats each dB of that gap?

Physical intuition (conclusion first): FOM is constructed to exactly cancel (f0/Δf)2(f_0/\Delta f)^2(f0​/Δf)2 and power PPP. What's left are only two things: nature's price list, kTkTkT (thermal-noise energy in a 1 Hz bandwidth vs. 1 mW), which comes to 173.8 dB at 300 K; and the topology noise factor FeffF_{eff}Feff​ — how much your circuit amplifies kTkTkT (ring: Feff≥3.6F_{eff}\ge3.6Feff​≥3.6, ceiling 168.3 dB) or beats it down with high-QQQ energy storage (LC: Feff∝1/Q2F_{eff}\propto 1/Q^2Feff​∝1/Q2, ceiling rises 20 dB per decade of QQQ). So there is no single magic number: the ceiling is a "family," and which member you land on is set by what physics you allow into FeffF_{eff}Feff​.

Step 0: FOM's definition and sign convention​

This page uses the positive-value convention (bigger is better, the most common form in survey tables):

  • L(Δf)\mathcal{L}(\Delta f)L(Δf): SSB phase noise at offset Δf\Delta fΔf, in dBc/Hz (negative, so −L-\mathcal{L}−L is positive).
  • f0f_0f0​: carrier frequency [Hz]; Δf\Delta fΔf: offset frequency [Hz]; PPP: total DC power dissipation [W], normalized to 1 mW.
  • Comparison with tank_swing: that page writes FOM′=L−20log⁡10(f0/Δf)+10log⁡10(P/1mW)\mathrm{FOM}'=\mathcal{L}-20\log_{10}(f_0/\Delta f)+10\log_{10}(P/1\text{mW})FOM′=L−20log10​(f0​/Δf)+10log10​(P/1mW) — the same quantity with the sign flipped (FOM=−FOM′\mathrm{FOM}=-\mathrm{FOM}'FOM=−FOM′; under that convention more negative is better).   • A hidden reference unit: the "per Hz" in L\mathcal{L}L is really "sideband power in a 1 Hz measurement bandwidth ÷ carrier power."   • Physical meaning (the single most important sentence on this page): the Q2Q^2Q2 in the denominator is how LC punches through the 173.8 dB "reference line" — the resonant tank stores signal energy without adding noise; each watt of loss only buys kTkTkT worth of noise once, through RpR_pRp​. The reference line is not LC's ceiling; LC's ceiling is set by whatever QQQ the process can provide (on-chip spiral inductors at GHz frequencies typically give Q≈8∼15Q\approx8\sim15Q≈8∼15).
  • Breakdown conditions: swing large enough to distort the waveform (changing Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​, FFF), ηP\eta_PηP​ collapsing once voltage-limited, varactor/switch loss eating into QQQ, flicker-dominated offsets (the universal form only covers the 1/f21/f^21/f2 region).

How many dB below the ceiling?​

  • Good published LC designs: survey-table standouts commonly fall in the 188∼195188\sim195188∼195 dB range (order-of-magnitude statement; corresponding to Feff≈0.008∼0.03F_{eff}\approx0.008\sim0.03Feff​≈0.008∼0.03, which can be back-derived from this page's bound family, e.g. worked example 2's 189189189 dB → an equivalent Q≈5.7Q\approx5.7Q≈5.7). Against their own QQQ-based ceiling (Q=10∼15Q=10\sim15Q=10∼15 → 197.6∼201.2197.6\sim201.2197.6∼201.2 dB, /4 convention), the typical gap is about 5∼105\sim105∼10 dB — eaten by ηP<1\eta_P\lt1ηP​<1, tail/bias noise (F>1+γF\gt1+\gammaF>1+γ), varactor/switch loss (lowering effective QQQ), and layout parasitics. In other words: the LC problem has already been solved to within arm's reach of the physical limit, and every remaining dB costs more than the last.
  • Colpitts vs. LC-tank (Andreani et al., JSSC 2005; external literature, not among the five source PDFs, full citation at page bottom): this paper gives closed-form noise factors for CMOS Colpitts and differential LC-tank oscillators and validates them experimentally, with the well-known conclusion that in CMOS, LC-tank phase-noise performance is at least as good as Colpitts's — Colpitts's cyclostationary advantage (current pulse aligned to the ISF trough; this site's real_oscillator_topologies example 2 estimates roughly a 7 dB reduction in Γeff,rms2\Gamma_{eff,rms}^2Γeff,rms2​) is offset by its bias-efficiency and startup-margin cost. Both topologies sit under the same LC ceiling family (both have an FFF from the 1+γ1+\gamma1+γ family and a 1/Q21/Q^21/Q2 term) — neither can bypass the wall set by QQQ.
  • Ring: this site's example sits only 3.53.53.5 dB below the ring ceiling — the ring's universal form has no QQQ to buy with, and the ceiling itself (168.3 dB) is already about 30 dB below LC's.

  • FOM=−L+20log⁡10(f0/Δf)−10log⁡10(P/1mW)\mathrm{FOM}=-\mathcal{L}+20\log_{10}(f_0/\Delta f)-10\log_{10}(P/1\text{mW})FOM=−L+20log10​(f0​/Δf)−10log10​(P/1mW) (this page's positive-value convention; tank_swing uses its negative).
