LC vs ring oscillator through the ISF lens

Prerequisites: tank_swing (L∝Γrms2/qmax2\mathcal{L}\propto\Gamma_{rms}^2/q_{max}^2L∝Γrms2​/qmax2​ and the swing lever), tank_Q_and_energy_restoration (what LC's "high-QQQ energy storage" actually buys, why ring lacks this advantage), rms_isf (Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​, Parseval, ring's N−3/2N^{-3/2}N−3/2 scaling) | Next: serdes_clocking_connection, varactor_tuning_supply_pushing

This page treats the ISF as a single unified ruler and measures the difference between LC and ring oscillators item by item. The point is not "which is better" (each has its use) but what the ISF framework lets us see, and what it doesn't. We first build an explicitly-labeled honest toy model, then quantify the differences using the [P1] / [P2] formulas.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): LC is like a heavy pendulum — most of the energy is stored in the tank (LLL and CCC trading back and forth), with only a small device occasionally topping up the loss. The waveform is close to sinusoidal, the ISF is smooth (−sin⁡-\sin−sin), and phase sensitivity is spread across the whole period. ring is like a row of relay-passing gates — no energy-storage element, every stage is switching, every stage leaks noise, the waveform is close to a square wave, the ISF is concentrated at the transition, and phase sensitivity is sharp. LC buys low phase noise with "energy storage + high QQQ"; ring trades phase noise for "small area, tunability, multiphase output."

Toy model assumptions (explicitly labeled)​

The following is a pedagogical toy model, not transistor-level. It captures the qualitative difference but is not a measurement of any real circuit.

  • LC: ideal sinusoidal state, V(θ)=cos⁡θV(\theta)=\cos\thetaV(θ)=cosθ, ISF ΓLC(θ)=−sin⁡θ\Gamma_{LC}(\theta)=-\sin\thetaΓLC​(θ)=−sinθ ([P1] Fig. 7(a)). Amplitude restoration is provided by the limit cycle ([P1] Sec. III-A).
  • ring: NNN-stage inverter delay + independent timing noise per stage; ISF modeled as a triangular toy shape, peak ∼1/N\sim1/\sqrt{N}∼1/N​, energy concentrated at the transition (actual simulated ISF curve: [P2] Fig. 5, p.793; triangular approximation: [P2] Fig. 6, p.793). Accumulated jitter uses a random-walk model σΔt=σedgeΔN\sigma_{\Delta t}=\sigma_{edge}\sqrt{\Delta N}σΔt​=σedge​ΔN​ ([P2] Eq.(8)).
  • Full scripts: simulations/lab_02_lc_toy_model.py, simulations/lab_03_ring_toy_model.py.

(d) N-independence conclusion (claim C7, verified):

Key fact: [P2] Eq.(23)'s FOM contains no NNN at all — at fixed f0f_0f0​ and power PPP, a single-ended ring's phase noise is independent of the stage count NNN. Microscopically, increasing NNN lowers Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ (Eq.16), but it also lowers the per-stage swing qmaxq_{max}qmax​ (each stage must be faster at fixed f0f_0f0​) and increases the number of noisy stages; these NNN-dependent effects cancel each other at fixed PPP, f0f_0f0​, so Eq.(23) ends up with no NNN. Full derivation: [P2] Sec. V and paper_002 deep-dive.

  • Conclusion: at fixed center frequency and power dissipation, adding stages does not improve ring phase noise/jitter — [P2]'s signature counter-intuitive result.
  • Design implication: choose NNN based on tuning range, multiphase requirements, area, and maximum f0f_0f0​ — not for phase noise.

Step 2b: differential ring — the lost other half of N-independence ([P2] Eq.(31)–(35), p.796, verified ✓)​

The N-independence above tells only half the story: it is the conclusion for a single-ended CMOS ring. [P2] Sec. V-B (p.796) redoes the same bookkeeping for a differential MOS ring (resistive load RLR_LRL​, tail-current biasing) and the ending flips — the phase-noise formula contains an explicit factor of NNN. All five formulas below have been verified verbatim against the original [P2] PDF, p.796 (rendered-page comparison).

(a) The power bookkeeping turns from "dynamic" into "static" ([P2] Eq.(31), p.796):

P=N Itail VDDP=N\,I_{tail}\,V_{DD}P=NItail​VDD​
  • The differential pair is biased by a tail current source: whether or not it is switching, each stage continuously draws ItailI_{tail}Itail​ from VDDV_{DD}VDD​ (class-A-style static bookkeeping); NNN stages means NNN shares.
  • Unit check: (dimensionless)⋅[A]⋅[V]=[W](\text{dimensionless})\cdot[\text{A}]\cdot[\text{V}]=[\text{W}](dimensionless)⋅[A]⋅[V]=[W] ✓.

[P2]'s own conclusion sentence (pp.796–797, verbatim): "Note that, in contrast with the single-ended ring oscillator, a differential oscillator does exhibit a phase noise and jitter dependency on the number of stages, with the phase noise degrading as the number of stages increases for a given frequency and power dissipation. Applicability / failure conditions:

  • Applies when: white-noise dominated (symmetry criteria already met, 1/f upconversion already suppressed — the stated premise at the start of [P2] Sec. V); fully switching differential pair with resistive load; Eq.(34)/(35) hold in both long- and short-channel regimes (with the right VcharV_{char}Vchar​); MOS or bipolar (the latter via Eq.(36) with Vchar=4kT/qeV_{char}=4kT/q_eVchar​=4kT/qe​).
  • Caveats: Lmin\mathcal{L}_{min}Lmin​/κmin\kappa_{min}κmin​ are lower bounds (the same logic as Eq.(25)/(26) — extra noise sources, asymmetry, supply/substrate only make things worse); if the design fixes RLR_LRL​ instead of fixing the swing, then VDD/(RLItail)∝NV_{DD}/(R_L I_{tail})\propto NVDD​/(RL​Itail​)∝N and the degradation is steeper than 10log⁡10N10\log_{10}N10log10​N; the N−3/2N^{-3/2}N−3/2 of Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ carries over from Eq.(16) — [P2] Fig. 9 (p.795) measures the rms ISF of differential rings under three constraint scenarios (fixed power/fixed swing, fixed power/fixed RLR_LRL​, fixed tail current/fixed RLR_LRL​), all hugging the same scaling.

One-line design rule: single-ended ring = phase noise immune to NNN (no NNN in Eq.23); differential ring = fewest stages wins (explicit NNN in Eq.34, +3.01+3.01+3.01 dB per doubling) — stop at the smallest NNN that phase margin, quadrature/multiphase, and frequency-division requirements allow.

Step 3: jitter accumulation — LC slow, ring fast​

Ring is free-running with no absolute time reference; each stage transition adds a bit of independent timing noise, and the edge time performs a random walk, with accumulated jitter growing as the square root of the measurement interval ([P2] Eq.(8), p.792; κ\kappaκ from Eq.(12), p.793):

σΔϕ=κΔt\sigma_{\Delta\phi}=\kappa\sqrt{\Delta t}σΔϕ​=κΔt​
  • This is phase jitter (dimensionless): per [P2] Eq.(11) σΔϕ2=Γrms2 in2‾/Δf2qmax2 Δt\sigma_{\Delta\phi}^2=\dfrac{\Gamma_{rms}^2\,\overline{i_n^2}/\Delta f}{2q_{max}^2}\,\Delta tσΔϕ2​=2qmax2​Γrms2​in2​​/Δf​Δt, so κΔt\kappa\sqrt{\Delta t}κΔt​ gives the phase jitter σΔϕ\sigma_{\Delta\phi}σΔϕ​. Time jitter then follows from [P2] Eq.(10)'s phase-to-time conversion σΔt=σΔϕ/ω0\sigma_{\Delta t}=\sigma_{\Delta\phi}/\omega_0σΔt​=σΔϕ​/ω0​. ω0\omega_0ω0​ lives in Eq.(10), not in κ\kappaκ.
  • Unit check: κ\kappaκ has units 1/s1/\sqrt{\text{s}}1/s​ (in2‾/Δf\overline{i_n^2}/\Delta fin2​​/Δf is [A2 ⁣⋅ ⁣s][\text{A}^2\!\cdot\!\text{s}][A2⋅s], qmaxq_{max}qmax​ is [A ⁣⋅ ⁣s][\text{A}\!\cdot\!\text{s}][A⋅s]), so κΔt=[1/s]⋅[s]=\kappa\sqrt{\Delta t}=[1/\sqrt{\text{s}}]\cdot[\sqrt{\text{s}}]=κΔt​=[1/s​]⋅[s​]= dimensionless ✓ (phase); dividing by ω0\omega_0ω0​ [1/s][1/\text{s}][1/s] gives σΔt=[s]\sigma_{\Delta t}=[\text{s}]σΔt​=[s] ✓ (time).
  • κ2∝Γrms2/qmax2⋅in2‾/Δf\kappa^2\propto\Gamma_{rms}^2/q_{max}^2\cdot\overline{i_n^2}/\Delta fκ2∝Γrms2​/qmax2​⋅in2​​/Δf ([P2] Eq.(12), p.793, verified: κ=(Γrms/qmax)(in2‾/Δf)/2\kappa=(\Gamma_{rms}/q_{max})\sqrt{(\overline{i_n^2}/\Delta f)/2}κ=(Γrms​/qmax​)(in2​​/Δf)/2​, no ω0\omega_0ω0​) — the same core ratio shows up again.
  • LC drifts much more slowly in phase (equivalently, small κ\kappaκ) thanks to high QQQ; but as long as it's free-running, both eventually drift — locking to an absolute time reference requires a PLL/CDR (see serdes_clocking_connection).

Key takeaways​

  • LC: sinusoidal waveform, Γ=−sin⁡\Gamma=-\sinΓ=−sin, high-QQQ energy storage, large qmaxq_{max}qmax​, few devices, low phase noise, slow jitter accumulation.
  • ring: square wave, ISF concentrated at transition, no energy storage, NNN noise sources, Γrms∝N−3/2\Gamma_{rms}\propto N^{-3/2}Γrms​∝N−3/2, fast random-walk jitter.
  • [P2]'s three formulas: f0=1/(2NτD)f_0=1/(2N\tau_D)f0​=1/(2NτD​) (Eq.15), Γrms∝N−3/2\Gamma_{rms}\propto N^{-3/2}Γrms​∝N−3/2 (Eq.16, re-verified in v7: the radical covers only the constant), FOM 83η VDDVchar kTP(ω0/Δω)2\frac{8}{3\eta}\,\frac{V_{DD}}{V_{char}}\,\frac{kT}{P}(\omega_0/\Delta\omega)^23η8​Vchar​VDD​​PkT​(ω0​/Δω)2 (Eq.23, prefactor 8/(3η)8/(3\eta)8/(3η), verified).
  • N-independence: at fixed f0f_0f0​/power, ring phase noise is roughly independent of NNN (NNN-dependent factors cancel); choose NNN based on tuning/multiphase/area.
  • Differential-ring exception: [P2] Eq.(34), p.796 contains an explicit NNN — at fixed f0f_0f0​/power, L\mathcal{L}L degrades as 10log⁡10N10\log_{10}N10log10​N (the culprit is qmax∝1/N2q_{max}\propto1/N^2qmax​∝1/N2); N=4→12N=4\to12N=4→12 costs +4.77+4.77+4.77 dB. Differential designs should use the fewest necessary stages.
  • The ISF sees the distribution and scaling of phase sensitivity; it cannot see absolute QQQ, strong nonlinearity, coupling, or exact constants.
