Tank swing, qmaxq_{max}qmax​, and phase noise

Prerequisites: white_noise_to_phase_noise ([P1] Eq.(21) signature result L∝Γrms2/qmax2\mathcal{L}\propto\Gamma_{rms}^2/q_{max}^2L∝Γrms2​/qmax2​), impulse_to_phase_shift (why qmax=CVmaxq_{max}=CV_{max}qmax​=CVmax​ lands in the denominator), tank_Q_and_energy_restoration (why high QQQ is "nearly-free swing", where RpR_pRp​ comes from) | Next: varactor_tuning_supply_pushing, lc_vs_ring

This page answers the question asked earliest — and most often underestimated — in oscillator design: why does making the tank's (LC resonator's) voltage swing larger lower phase noise? The answer is the cleanest scaling in ISF theory: phase noise is inversely proportional to qmax2q_{max}^2qmax2​, and qmax=CnodeVmaxq_{max}=C_{node}V_{max}qmax​=Cnode​Vmax​ is set directly by swing.

Physical intuition (conclusion first): phase error =ΓqmaxΔq=\dfrac{\Gamma}{q_{max}}\Delta q=qmax​Γ​Δq — the denominator is qmaxq_{max}qmax​. The larger the swing, the more charge qmaxq_{max}qmax​ the signal carries, and the same lump of noise charge Δq\Delta qΔq becomes proportionally more "negligible" against it, so the phase it can push is smaller. Make the signal big, outweigh the noise — this is the first principle of every low-phase-noise design. The cost: swing is bounded by supply / headroom, and pushing swing up usually burns more power.

Step 1: what qmaxq_{max}qmax​ is, and why it lands in the denominator​

qmaxq_{max}qmax​ is "the maximum charge corresponding to the signal swing" at a node:

qmax=Cnode Vmaxq_{max}=C_{node}\,V_{max}qmax​=Cnode​Vmax​
  • Unit check: [F]⋅[V]=[C][\text{F}]\cdot[\text{V}]=[\text{C}][F]⋅[V]=[C] ✓.
  • It appears in the denominator of the impulse→phase relation ([P1] Eq.(10)–(11), p.182): Δϕ=ΓqmaxΔq\Delta\phi=\dfrac{\Gamma}{q_{max}}\Delta qΔϕ=qmax​Γ​Δq.
  • Meaning: Δq/qmax\Delta q/q_{max}Δq/qmax​ is "the injected charge as a fraction of the signal charge." Larger qmaxq_{max}qmax​ → the same Δq\Delta qΔq produces a smaller relative disturbance → phase is more stable. Γ\GammaΓ (a dimensionless shape) itself does not change with swing; swing only moves qmaxq_{max}qmax​.

Step 2: the phase-noise ∝1/qmax2\propto 1/q_{max}^2∝1/qmax2​ scaling​

The signature result for white-noise-induced 1/f21/f^21/f2 phase noise ([P1] Eq.(21), p.185):

L{Δω}=10log⁡10 ⁣(Γrms2qmax2⋅in2‾/Δf4 Δω2)\mathcal{L}\{\Delta\omega\}=10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\Gamma_{rms}^2}{q_{max}^2}\cdot\frac{\overline{i_n^2}/\Delta f}{4\,\Delta\omega^2}\right)L{Δω}=10log10​(qmax2​Γrms2​​⋅4Δω2in2​​/Δf​)
  • The denominator has qmax2q_{max}^2qmax2​. So L∝1/qmax2\mathcal{L}\propto1/q_{max}^2L∝1/qmax2​.
  • Scaling (claim C3): doubling qmaxq_{max}qmax​ → L\mathcal{L}L drops by 10log⁡10(22)=6.0210\log_{10}(2^2)=6.0210log10​(22)=6.02 dB. Every doubling of swing (with CCC fixed) improves phase noise by 6 dB.

Step 3: two routes to raise qmaxq_{max}qmax​ — increase VmaxV_{max}Vmax​ vs. increase CCC​

qmax=C⋅Vmaxq_{max}=C\cdot V_{max}qmax​=C⋅Vmax​, so in principle either raising CCC or raising VmaxV_{max}Vmax​ raises qmaxq_{max}qmax​. But the two routes have completely different costs:

Increase VmaxV_{max}Vmax​ (swing)qmax∝Vmaxq_{max}\propto V_{max}qmax​∝Vmax​L∝1/Vmax2\mathcal{L}\propto1/V_{max}^2L∝1/Vmax2​ (most effective)Limited by supply / breakdown / headroom; needs more bias current to sustain swing

  • Increasing swing is usually the first choice: direct, 6 dB/octave, and does not move f0f_0f0​.
  • Increasing CCC has a trap: in an LC tank f0=1/(2πLC)f_0=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})f0​=1/(2πLC​), so raising CCC requires lowering LLL proportionally; sustaining the same swing voltage then needs more tank current (QQQ, gmg_mgm​ limited), and the real-world improvement is often eaten by "the extra power/noise spent driving the larger CCC." The genuinely clean lever is pushing swing to the headroom limit within the power budget.

Step 4: power / voltage headroom trade-off​

Swing cannot grow without bound — it hits two walls, supply and device:

  1. Voltage headroom: the single-ended swing limit of a differential LC tank ranges from about the supply VDDV_{DD}VDD​ (current-limited regime) up to ∼4π IbiasRp\sim\dfrac{4}{\pi}\,I_{bias}R_p∼π4​Ibias​Rp​ (where RpR_pRp​ is the tank's equivalent parallel resistance). Once pushed into the voltage-limited regime, adding more current no longer adds swing — the phase-noise improvement saturates.
  2. Power: in the current-limited regime, swing ≈4πIbiasRp\approx\dfrac{4}{\pi}I_{bias}R_p≈π4​Ibias​Rp​ is proportional to bias current. Doubling swing → doubling current → doubling power.

Putting the two together gives the FOM (figure of merit) trade-off:

  • phase noise L∝1/qmax2∝1/Vmax2\mathcal{L}\propto1/q_{max}^2\propto1/V_{max}^2L∝1/qmax2​∝1/Vmax2​ (bigger swing is always better),
  • but Vmax∝IbiasV_{max}\propto I_{bias}Vmax​∝Ibias​ (current-limited) → L∝1/Ibias2\mathcal{L}\propto1/I_{bias}^2L∝1/Ibias2​, while P∝IbiasP\propto I_{bias}P∝Ibias​,
  • so does L∝1/P2\mathcal{L}\propto1/P^2L∝1/P2? — No. Because the noise current in2‾\overline{i_n^2}in2​​ also rises with bias current (more current → more device noise), typically in2‾∝Ibias\overline{i_n^2}\propto I_{bias}in2​​∝Ibias​, and the net effect reverts to L⋅P≈\mathcal{L}\cdot P\approxL⋅P≈ constant (roughly 1 dB of phase noise bought per extra 1 dB of power burned).
  • This is why the industry uses FOM =L−20log⁡10(f0/Δf)+10log⁡10(P/1mW)=\mathcal{L}-20\log_{10}(f_0/\Delta f)+10\log_{10}(P/1\text{mW})=L−20log10​(f0​/Δf)+10log10​(P/1mW) to fairly compare oscillators at different power levels: it normalizes away this "phase noise × power ≈ constant" trade-off.

⚠️ The "current-limited / voltage-limited" boundary above and 4πIbiasRp\dfrac{4}{\pi}I_{bias}R_pπ4​Ibias​Rp​ are standard LC-oscillator design knowledge (not among the five downloaded source PDFs; supplemented from standard literature, e.g. the Hajimiri-Lee textbook, Razavi's RF Microelectronics). [P1] itself gives the 1/qmax21/q_{max}^21/qmax2​ scaling but does not expand the circuit-level detail of swing-vs-power. The exact swing-limit coefficients and phase-noise-factor floor can be found in E. Hegazi, H. Sjöland, A. A. Abidi, A Filtering Technique to Lower LC Oscillator Phase Noise, IEEE JSSC 36(12):1921–1930, 2001 (verified verbatim), and Razavi's RF Microelectronics (external literature, not among the five source PDFs).

Key takeaways​

  • qmax=C⋅Vmaxq_{max}=C\cdot V_{max}qmax​=C⋅Vmax​; phase noise L∝1/qmax2\mathcal{L}\propto1/q_{max}^2L∝1/qmax2​ ([P1] Eq.(21)).
  • Doubling qmaxq_{max}qmax​ → L\mathcal{L}L improves by 6.02 dB (e.g., −148→−154-148\to-154−148→−154 dBc/Hz).
  • Increasing swing is cleaner than increasing CCC (increasing CCC ties down f0f_0f0​ and needs more current); raising tank QQQ is nearly-free swing.
  • Trade-off: in the current-limited regime, swing ∝\propto∝ current, and device noise ∝\propto∝ current too → phase noise × power ≈ constant; use FOM for a fair comparison.
  • qmaxq_{max}qmax​ and Γrms\Gamma_{rms}Γrms​ are two independent levers (the numerator and denominator of [P1] Eq.(21)).
