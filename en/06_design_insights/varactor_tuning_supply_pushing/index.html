Tuning-line and supply-pushing phase noise

Prerequisites (read these first): white_noise_to_phase_noise (white noise → 1/f21/f^21/f2 via the 1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2 integrator), flicker_noise_upconversion (1/f1/f1/f → 1/f31/f^31/f3; this page draws the parallel), phase_vs_amplitude_noise (the AM-PM back door). This page assumes you already accept the main thread: "phase has no restoring force → noise integrates through 1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2 into the skirt."

Everything in the ISF framework so far has been about the device's own noise current in(t)i_n(t)in​(t) injected directly into the tank node. But a real VCO (voltage-controlled oscillator — output frequency set by a control voltage) has two more phase-noise gates that don't rely on the device's internal ini_nin​ at all, but on jitter of an external voltage node:

  1. tuning line: you use a voltage VtuneV_{tune}Vtune​ to tune the varactor (a varactor diode — bias changes its capacitance) and set f0f_0f0​ to the desired channel. Any noise voltage vnv_nvn​ on this line directly FM-modulates (frequency-modulates) the carrier.
  2. supply (VDDV_{DD}VDD​): supply voltage variation changes the effective f0f_0f0​ via the device operating point, parasitic capacitance, and similar paths — this is called supply pushing (supply voltage pushes the oscillation frequency around).

This page answers: how do these two external voltage gates turn low-frequency noise voltage into close-in phase noise? Why does the mechanism look identical to device flicker's 1/f31/f^31/f3? And how do design choices close these two gates down?

Physical intuition (the punchline first): think of the VCO as a "voltage → frequency" converter with sensitivity KVCO=∂f0/∂VtuneK_{VCO}=\partial f_0/\partial V_{tune}KVCO​=∂f0​/∂Vtune​ (Hz/V). A noise voltage vn(t)v_n(t)vn​(t) on the control node jitters the instantaneous frequency by Δf(t)=KVCO vn(t)\Delta f(t)=K_{VCO}\,v_n(t)Δf(t)=KVCO​vn​(t). Frequency is the derivative of phase, so phase is the integral of frequency — this integrator is the exact same machine that turns SvS_vSv​ (voltage noise) into the 1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2 skirt in the ISF white-noise result: literally the same integrator. Hence: white noise on the tune line → 1/f21/f^21/f2; 1/f1/f1/f noise on the tune line → 1/f31/f^31/f3. The larger KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​, the wider this gate opens; shrinking it (coarse tuning via switched-cap, fine tuning via varactor) and cleaning up Vtune/VDDV_{tune}/V_{DD}Vtune​/VDD​ (LDO, common-mode rejection) is the core of every design knob on this page.

The concrete circuit/topology/instrumentation details on this page — the varactor CCC–VVV model, LDO/cross-coupled VCO topology specifics, measurement methods — belong to standard RF IC design literature (Razavi, Leeson, vendor datasheets); not among the five source PDFs on this site, and this page explicitly flags them "(external literature, not among the five source PDFs)." But the main thread — "voltage noise → FM → 1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2 phase skirt" — follows strictly from [P1]'s existing phase-integration concept alone, which is what we do below.

Step 1: define KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​ and supply pushing KpushK_{push}Kpush​​

A VCO is by definition "output frequency varies with control voltage." Treat oscillation frequency f0f_0f0​ as a function of control voltage VtuneV_{tune}Vtune​ and supply VDDV_{DD}VDD​, and do a first-order Taylor expansion about the operating point:

f0(Vtune,VDD)≈f0,op+∂f0∂Vtune⏟KVCO (Vtune−Vop)+∂f0∂VDD⏟Kpush (VDD−VDD,op).f_0(V_{tune},V_{DD})\approx f_{0,op}+\underbrace{\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial V_{tune}}}_{K_{VCO}}\,(V_{tune}-V_{op})+\underbrace{\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial V_{DD}}}_{K_{push}}\,(V_{DD}-V_{DD,op}).f0​(Vtune​,VDD​)≈f0,op​+KVCO​∂Vtune​∂f0​​​​(Vtune​−Vop​)+Kpush​∂VDD​∂f0​​​​(VDD​−VDD,op​).

The two slopes are this page's two protagonists:

KVCO≡∂f0∂Vtune(units Hz/V)K_{VCO}\equiv\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial V_{tune}}\qquad(\text{units Hz/V})KVCO​≡∂Vtune​∂f0​​(units Hz/V) Kpush≡∂f0∂VDD(units Hz/V)K_{push}\equiv\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial V_{DD}}\qquad(\text{units Hz/V})Kpush​≡∂VDD​∂f0​​(units Hz/V)
  • KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​ (VCO gain, also called tuning sensitivity): how far frequency moves per 1 V of control voltage. It sets the tuning range (the frequency span it can sweep) and also the gain applied to tune-line noise — the core tension on this page: KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​ must be large enough to cover the band, yet small enough not to amplify noise.
  • KpushK_{push}Kpush​ (supply pushing coefficient): how far frequency moves per 1 V of supply variation. Ideally the VCO is supply-immune (Kpush=0K_{push}=0Kpush​=0); in practice the supply alters f0f_0f0​ via device bias point, parasitic capacitance, and effective swing, so Kpush≠0K_{push}\ne0Kpush​=0. (Vendors often quote a pushing figure in Hz/V\text{Hz/V}Hz/V or ppm/V\text{ppm/V}ppm/V — external literature, not among the five source PDFs.)
  • Dimension check: [∂f0/∂V]=Hz/V[\partial f_0/\partial V]=\text{Hz}/\text{V}[∂f0​/∂V]=Hz/V ✓. Both have the same dimension and are exactly parallel mathematically — every derivation below applies verbatim to tune line and supply; just swap KVCO,vn,tuneK_{VCO},v_{n,tune}KVCO​,vn,tune​ for Kpush,vn,DDK_{push},v_{n,DD}Kpush​,vn,DD​.
  • Physical meaning: these two coefficients connect the "external voltage world" to the "frequency world." They are designer-controllable — unlike the device's ini_nin​ (fixed by process/physics), KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​ and KpushK_{push}Kpush​ are outcomes of topology and bias choices.

Division of labor with ISF Γ\GammaΓ: ISF Γ(ω0τ)\Gamma(\omega_0\tau)Γ(ω0​τ) handles "a current impulse Δq\Delta qΔq injected at what phase of the tank node → how much phase shift"; KVCO/KpushK_{VCO}/K_{push}KVCO​/Kpush​ handles "a quasi-static (slow relative to the carrier) control/supply voltage → how much frequency shift." Tune/supply noise is slow (offset ≪f0\ll f_0≪f0​), so it need not go through ISF's per-impulse phase projection — it takes the more direct FM route: "voltage → frequency → integrate into phase." Both routes ultimately converge on the same 1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2 integrator (see end of Step 2) — exactly the parallel this page draws.

Step 2: how voltage noise FM-modulates the carrier → Sϕ=KVCO2Sv/Δω2S_\phi=K_{VCO}^2 S_v/\Delta\omega^2Sϕ​=KVCO2​Sv​/Δω2​

Step 3: white tune-line noise → 1/f21/f^21/f2; 1/f1/f1/f tune-line noise → 1/f31/f^31/f3 (paralleling device c0c_0c0​)​

Step 2's Sϕ∝Sv/Δω2S_\phi\propto S_v/\Delta\omega^2Sϕ​∝Sv​/Δω2 carries the tune line's spectral shape through unchanged, then multiplies by 1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2. Two cases:

Case A — tune line is white noise (Sv=S_v=Sv​= constant): e.g. series-resistor thermal noise, wideband buffer noise. Then

Sϕ=KVCO2 Sv,0Δf2 ∝ 1Δf2⇒−20 dB/decade (1/f2).S_\phi=\frac{K_{VCO}^2\,S_{v,0}}{\Delta f^2}\ \propto\ \frac{1}{\Delta f^2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad -20\ \text{dB/decade}\ (1/f^2).Sϕ​=Δf2KVCO2​Sv,0​​ ∝ Δf21​⇒−20 dB/decade (1/f2).

This has exactly the same slope, exactly the same mechanism as device white noise's 1/f21/f^21/f2 via ISF (both: white entry → 1/Δω21/\Delta\omega^21/Δω2 integrator).

Case B — tune line is 1/f1/f1/f noise (Sv=kv/ΔfS_v=k_v/\Delta fSv​=kv​/Δf): e.g. low-frequency flicker from a charge pump / bandgap / LDO reference, or 1/f1/f1/f in the varactor bias circuit. Then

Step 4: varactor C(V)C(V)C(V) nonlinearity → AM-PM​

So far KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​ has been treated as constant. But the varactor is inherently a nonlinear capacitor C(V)C(V)C(V) — this opens a second, more subtle gate: AM-PM conversion (amplitude modulation converted to phase modulation), the back door discussed in phase_vs_amplitude_noise, Step 4.

  3. Nonlinearity → effective capacitance varies with swing: because C(V)C(V)C(V) is curved, the average effective capacitance Cˉ(A)\bar C(A)Cˉ(A) over one cycle changes with amplitude AAA (Taylor-expand C(V)C(V)C(V) about the bias point; the second-order term 12C′′(Vtune)⟨vosc2⟩\tfrac12 C''(V_{tune})\langle v_{osc}^2\rangle21​C′′(Vtune​)⟨vosc2​⟩ is proportional to A2A^2A2 and nonzero).   4. This is exactly ∂ω/∂A≠0\partial\omega/\partial A\ne0∂ω/∂A=0: amplitude noise ΔA\Delta AΔA (which would normally be squashed by the limit cycle's restoring force) leaks into frequency/phase noise via ∂f0/∂A\partial f_0/\partial A∂f0​/∂A, then gets permanently accumulated by Step 2's integrator into a close-in skirt.

Step 5: design knobs (closing both gates)​

Translate the physics above directly into actionable knobs. Each knob states which quantity it acts on.

  • Split tuning — shrinks KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​. The single most important technique (external literature, not among the five source PDFs). Split tuning into two layers:
    • coarse: switched-capacitor bank (an array of switched capacitors, switched in/out by a digital code). It selects the band with a digital code, providing most of the tuning range, but is immune to continuous voltage noise (the code doesn't jitter, so the capacitance doesn't jitter).
    • fine: varactor, responsible only for continuous fine-tuning within a small sub-band.
    • Effect: total tuning range = coarse (digital, noise-free) ⊕ fine (small range), and the fine varactor's KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​ drops sharply because it only spans a small sub-band. By Step 2's Sϕ∝KVCO2S_\phi\propto K_{VCO}^2Sϕ​∝KVCO2​, halving KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​ cuts the tune-line phase-noise contribution by 6 dB. This is the standard way to untangle the "range vs. noise" tension.
  • Bias at the flat point of C(V)C(V)C(V) — suppresses AM-PM (controls C′′C''C′′). Choose the varactor operating point so C′′(Vtune)≈0C''(V_{tune})\approx0C′′(Vtune​)≈0 (small curvature), driving ∂ω/∂A→0\partial\omega/\partial A\to0∂ω/∂A→0 and closing the AM-PM back door. Note this is a different knob from shrinking KVCOK_{VCO}KVCO​: the flat point suppresses second-order curvature, not first-order slope.
  • Supply regulation / LDO — suppresses the Sv,DDS_{v,DD}Sv,DD​ entry (external literature, not among the five source PDFs). To combat supply pushing: place an LDO (low-dropout regulator) ahead of the VCO to filter a dirty supply into a clean local supply, reducing Sv,DDS_{v,DD}Sv,DD​ (the supply-noise PSD reaching the VCO) by tens of dB. By the supply-side formula Sϕ∝Kpush2Sv,DDS_\phi\propto K_{push}^2 S_{v,DD}Sϕ​∝Kpush2​Sv,DD​, reducing Sv,DDS_{v,DD}Sv,DD​ reduces phase noise proportionally. You can also reduce KpushK_{push}Kpush​ itself (topology-level: symmetric bias, less supply-to-swing modulation).
  • Common-mode rejection — makes a differential VCO immune to common-mode supply/substrate noise. On a differential tank, supply/substrate noise mostly appears as a common-mode disturbance; good differential symmetry keeps common-mode disturbances from converting to differential-mode frequency shifts (ideally Kpush,CM→0K_{push,CM}\to0Kpush,CM​→0). This shares its origin with flicker_noise_upconversion Step 6's differential concept, but here it combats external common-mode voltage rather than device c0c_0c0​.
  • Clean VtuneV_{tune}Vtune​ routing — suppresses the Sv,tuneS_{v,tune}Sv,tune​ entry. Loop-filter resistor thermal noise, charge-pump 1/f1/f1/f, and bandgap reference noise all feed into VtuneV_{tune}Vtune​; these are the physical origin of the "in-band" terms in pll_noise_budget. Within a PLL, the loop bandwidth also determines how much this tune-line noise gets high-pass/low-pass filtered.

Design rule of thumb (in one line): the phase noise from these two gates = (conversion gain K2K^2K2) × (entry voltage noise SvS_vSv​) × (1/Δf21/\Delta f^21/Δf2 integrator). Each of the three factors has its own knob: suppress KKK with split tuning / symmetric topology, suppress SvS_vSv​ with LDO / clean reference / low-noise loop filter, and suppress the curvature-driven AM-PM with a flat bias point.

Interface with the ISF: the supply sees the "coherent sum of per-stage sensitivities"​

In ISF language there is one key difference between device noise and supply noise:

  • device noise (each stage's own ini_nin​): the NNN stages' noise sources are mutually independent, so per-stage contributions add in power — this is [P2]'s bookkeeping for the ring analysis (one Γrms2\Gamma_{rms}^2Γrms2​ share per stage).
  • supply (the VDDV_{DD}VDD​ rail): the supply is a port shared by all NNN stages. One low-frequency (quasi-static) supply disturbance changes every stage's delay simultaneously and in the same direction — whichever stage is switching gets sped up or slowed down by the same vnv_nvn​, and within one period the delay changes of all 2N2N2N edges accumulate with the same sign into ΔT\Delta TΔT. So the supply's effective sensitivity is the coherent (amplitude) sum of the per-stage sensitivities, and the average (DC) component of that sum is exactly 2πKpush2\pi K_{push}2πKpush​: for a slow disturbance, "some stage is being pushed at every instant," and the summed sensitivity never changes sign.
  • Corollary 1: stacking NNN independent sources scales as N\sqrt NN​ (power addition); a coherent source scales as NNN (amplitude addition) — supply noise is not merely "one more noise source": it bypasses the statistical discount of independent sources.
  • Corollary 2: the DC term of the supply's effective sensitivity is inherently large (its role is that of c0c_0c0​ in device-flicker upconversion), so the supply's 1/f1/f1/f noise upconverts to 1/f31/f^31/f3 as in Step 3; but while the device version can push c0c_0c0​ near 0 via waveform symmetry (lab_32's symmetric ring measured c0=0.0014c_0=0.0014c0​=0.0014), the supply version has no such card to play — "every stage's delay tracks VDDV_{DD}VDD​" is not something symmetry can cancel. The remaining cards are suppressing the entry (LDO) and suppressing KpushK_{push}Kpush​ itself (differential/regulated topologies, stage designs whose delay is first-order insensitive to VDDV_{DD}VDD​ — external literature, not among the five source PDFs).

Limitations (honestly): Level-1 square law, λ=0\lambda=0λ=0, a single lumped CLC_LCL​, single point N=3N=3N=3 (no NNN-scaling verified); a real PDK ring's KpushK_{push}Kpush​ number will differ due to velocity saturation and short-channel effects (though it remains far larger than an LC's — external-literature experience), while this section's method (definition-based measurement + FM verification) and structural conclusions (coherent summing, the K2K^2K2 lever) stand.
